## Process

- The six main project management principles: adopt a holistic view, focus on value, embed quality
  into process and deliverables, be an accountable leader, integrate sustainability within all
  project areas, and build empowered culture.
- Tailoring steps: choose the most suitable delivery approach based on project complexity, risk,
  uncertainty, and stakeholder needs; adjust processes and governance to align with organizational
  culture, policies, standards, and available capabilities; customize methods, tools, and level of
  control to fit the project's size, scope, risk level, and life cycle; and regularly review
  performance, capture lessons learned, and refine practices throughout the project to enhance
  outcomes and efficiency.
- Every project should have a charter (agreement, statement of work, etc.).
- The three project development approaches are predictive/traditional/plan-driven/waterfall,
  adaptive/change-driven, and hybrid. There are three adaptive approaches: incremental, iterative,
  and agile.
- The point of the incremental approach is to deliver as soon as possible.
- The iterative process has a review step after the do phase, then makes adjustments as we go based
  on the review. The benefit of the iterative process is feedback.
- The agile approach combines the incremental and iterative approaches.
- The triangle of constraints is cost, scope, and schedule. The predictive approach has a fixed
  scope and variable cost and schedule. For an agile approach, the scope is variable and the cost
  and schedule are fixed.

## Business Environment

- Intended outcomes should be clearly defined in the business case.
- Intended outcomes should be regularly assessed and updated throughout the project lifecycle.
- OPA = Organizational Process Assets.
