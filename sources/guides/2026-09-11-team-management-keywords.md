# Project Team Management Keywords Review

*Yassine Tounsi — PMP 35 Hours Master Course*

An alphabetical glossary of project team management terms.

### A

- **1on1 meeting, 1:1 meeting, 1to1 meeting:** a regular check-in between two stakeholders, often
  between the project manager and a team member, to connect on work, career development, and growth.
- **360-degree appraisal, Multi-rater feedback, 360-degree feedback:** a holistic review process in
  which the PM evaluates a team member's performance based on feedback from their superiors, peers,
  clients, etc.
- **Ability test, Aptitude test:** a method for assessing an individual's skills and abilities in
  different domains or situations — used by recruiters to identify fit candidates, and by PMs to
  measure the team's abilities/aptitudes in order to provide proper training.
- **Accountability:** being responsible and answerable for the results and consequences of one's
  performance, decisions, and actions.
- **Acquire Resources:** the process of obtaining team members, facilities, equipment, materials,
  supplies, and other resources necessary to complete project work — via assignment when resources
  are available, or by hiring/recruiting.
- **Adaptive Leadership:** adapting your leadership style to changing and challenging situations,
  rather than relying on predefined solutions or authority.
- **Adjourning:** the fifth and final team development stage — project tasks are completed, the team
  breaks up, and members move on to other assignments.
- **Attitudinal survey:** measures team members' satisfaction level and feelings about their roles,
  work environment, career opportunities, organizational culture, etc.
- **Authentic Leadership:** leaders who manage by being totally transparent, honest, genuine, and
  self-aware — inspiring loyalty and trust.

### A-C

- **Autocratic, Authoritarian:** an authority figure (PM or sponsor) makes all project decisions
  without involving others.
- **Calendar day:** the daily period (typically 9am–5pm) during which team members work on their
  assigned tasks.
- **Caves and Common office design:** integrates private offices ("Caves," for uninterrupted work)
  with a shared open space ("Common," where the team typically works).
- **Charismatic Leadership:** leaders with high positive energy, typically seen as an inspiration by
  the people around them.
- **Code of Conduct:** outlines the main standards and principles the project team should act in
  accordance with daily.
- **Co-located team:** the whole team works in the same physical workspace.
- **Consensus:** coming to an agreement on a proposal/solution acceptable enough that all team
  members can support it, with no member opposing it.
- **Cultural norms:** expectations on how to behave within a certain culture or social group.
- **Cultural shock:** confusion/uncertainty experienced when encountering a vastly different culture.
  Four stages: honeymoon, anxiety/frustration, adjustment/adaptation, acceptance.

### D-E

- **Democratic Leadership, Participative Leadership:** takes team members' opinions/input into
  account during decision-making, but the ultimate decision is made by the leader.
- **Develop Team:** the process of improving competencies, team member interaction, and the overall
  team environment to enhance project performance.
- **Drexler-Sibbet Team Performance Model:** describes the stages of creating a project team and its
  sustainability/performance — 7 steps: Orientation, Trust building, Goal clarification, Commitment,
  Implementation, High performance, and Renewal.
- **Dreyfus Model of Adult Skill Acquisition:** assesses competency/skill development level as new
  learning occurs — 5 stages: novice, competence, proficiency, expertise, mastery.
- **Emotional intelligence:** the ability to identify, assess, manage, and be aware of one's own and
  others' emotions — requires 5 skills: Motivation, Empathy, Social skills, Self-regulation,
  Self-awareness.
- **Empathy:** recognizing emotions in others and understanding their perspective, in order to improve
  their mood or support them through challenging situations.
- **Esteem Needs:** the fourth level of Maslow's hierarchy — recognition, status, respect; pursued
  once love/belonging needs are fulfilled.
- **Expectancy theory:** employees behave based on what they expect as a result of their behavior —
  believing more effort leads to reward motivates continued productivity; if the expected reward
  doesn't materialize, motivation drops.
- **Expert power:** derived from someone's deep skills and long experience in a specific field.

### E-F-G-H

- **Extrinsic factors, Demotivators, Hygiene agents:** externally driven motivation — rewards or
  punishments.
- **Extrovert (E) personality:** energized by people, enjoys variety and a quick pace, good at
  multitasking.
- **Feeler (F) personality:** sensitive and cooperative, decides based on personal values and impact
  on others.
- **Fishbowl windows:** a remote-team collaboration tool — an ongoing video conferencing feed between
  locations, helping virtual teams feel and act as if co-located.
- **Forming:** the first team development stage — members begin to get to know each other and form
  impressions.
- **Ground rules:** establish how meetings will be run, how team members are expected to interact,
  and what behaviors are acceptable/unacceptable.
- **Guilt-based power:** the power holder makes stakeholders feel guilty (e.g. "not living up to
  expectations") to motivate the team or gain compliance.
- **Halo effect:** a decision influenced by someone's single impressive performance/attribute/quality
  in a specific area — e.g. promoting someone into a PM role purely for excellent technical skills.
- **Herzberg's Theory of Motivation, Two-Factor Theory:** two motivator categories — Extrinsic Hygiene
  Factors and Intrinsic Motivator Factors.

### I-J-L

- **Informational power:** holding control/ownership of data by being in charge of gathering and
  distributing it.
- **Ingratiating power:** using flattery to find common ground and gain cooperation/favor from the
  team and stakeholders.
- **Interactional Leader:** excited about the project work, and inspires others to be as motivated as
  they are.
- **Interview:** a structured conversation to assess qualifications, suitability, or compatibility for
  a role or purpose.
- **Intrinsic factors, Motivators, Motivating agents:** motivation fueled by enjoyment/interest in the
  work itself — new goals, appreciation, accomplishments, individual development.
- **Introvert (I) personality:** likes working alone/in small groups, a deliberate pace, one task at a
  time.
- **Intuitive (N) personality:** focuses on possibilities and the big picture, sees patterns, values
  innovation, seeks creative solutions.
- **Judger (J) personality:** organized and prepared, likes to make/stick to plans, comfortable
  following rules.
- **Laissez-faire:** a hands-off approach — teams are fully in charge of their own decisions and how
  to function.

### L-M

- **Legitimate, Formal, or Positional power:** originates from the official position someone holds
  (e.g. being assigned as PM).
- **Lifetime employment, Long-term employment:** an Ouchi concept — workers stay loyal to the
  organization until retirement because they're appreciated and valued.
- **Love and belonging Needs:** third level of Maslow's hierarchy — friendship, intimacy, family, and
  giving/receiving love and belonging to a group.
- **Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs:** five levels — Physiological, Safety, Love & Belonging, Esteem,
  Self-actualization. Higher-level needs can only be satisfied once lower-level ones are fulfilled.
- **McGregor's Theory of X and Y:** Type X employees don't want to work, lack ambition, and need
  constant supervision; Type Y want to work, want responsibility, and don't need much supervision.
- **Mentoring, Coaching:** guiding/supporting team members — helping navigate complex situations,
  offering advice, listening to ideas, giving constructive feedback, sharing knowledge/experience.
- **Mid-training assessment:** conducted mid-way through a training program to evaluate its efficiency
  so far, allowing adjustments if needed.
- **Mobbing:** a technique where multiple team members focus simultaneously and coordinate their
  contributions on a particular work item.
- **Motivation:** inspiring, encouraging, and stimulating individuals/teams to achieve defined goals.

### M-N-O-P

- **Motivational Theory Z, Ouchi's Theory Z:** workers are motivated by their sense of commitment,
  opportunity, and advancement — performance is directly tied to being challenged and motivated.
- **Multitasking:** managing/executing more than one task at a time — mastering it lets you divide
  focus without compromising quality; used inefficiently, it causes lost time and poor quality.
- **Myers-Briggs Type Indicator (MBTI):** a personality questionnaire indicating psychological
  preferences — four categories: Introversion/Extraversion, Sensing/Intuition, Thinking/Feeling,
  Judging/Perceiving.
- **Norming:** the third team development stage — members begin to work together and trust each
  other.
- **Offboarding:** transitioning a departing team member (leaving the project or organization) through
  steps like capturing knowledge, conducting an exit interview, and fulfilling HR requirements — so
  they leave on good terms with all loose ends tied up.
- **Onboarding:** introducing the team to the project, during project initiation, to get everyone on
  the same page.
- **Pairing, Pair work:** pairing two team members to work simultaneously on the same work item.
- **Penalty power, Coercive power:** the ability to penalize team members — uses fear of negative
  consequences to invoke discipline/compliance.
- **Perceiver (P) personality:** keeps opinions open, likes to act spontaneously, flexible with plans.

### P-Q

- **Performing:** the fourth team development stage — the team functions smoothly/effectively as a
  unit, without conflicts or need for supervision.
- **Persuasive power:** gained by persuading others via convincing arguments.
- **Physiological Needs:** the first level of Maslow's hierarchy — air, food, drink, shelter, clothing,
  warmth, sleep, health.
- **Post-training assessment:** determines training efficiency by comparing results to the
  pre-training baseline.
- **Pre-assignment:** advance assignment/acquisition of resources before a project starts, used when
  the project depends on particular expertise.
- **Pressure-based power:** using power to limit freedom of choice, to gain compliance.
- **Pre-training assessment:** a baseline of where the team stands before receiving intended training.
- **Project Team Directory:** a documented list of team members, roles, and communication addresses.
- **Quarterly, Biannual, or Annual Performance Review/appraisal:** sessions for the PM and team member
  to review recent months — achievements, areas of improvement, and goals ahead.

### R

- **RACI:** Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed.
- **Referent power:** when the person is respected and admired for their credibility.
- **Relational power:** gained through building a network of connections and alliances.
- **Resource Breakdown Structure, Organogram:** a hierarchical representation of a project's or
  organization's resources.
- **Resource calendar:** a schedule of when team members are available to work on tasks.
- **Resource histogram:** a bar chart showing the utilization frequency of physical and human
  resources across the project life cycle.
- **Resource Management Plan:** describes how project resources are acquired, allocated, monitored,
  and controlled.
- **Resource Optimization Technique:** adjusts activities' start/finish dates to make planned resource
  utilization equal to or less than resource availability.
- **Responsibility Assignment Matrix (RAM):** visually defines the roles/responsibilities of different
  team members and the nature of their involvement in project activities.

### R-S

- **Reverse shadowing:** a junior team member is shadowed by a senior colleague from a higher
  managerial/leadership level, to learn their skills or experience.
- **Reward power:** using rewards (monetary or non-monetary — recognition, promotion recommendations,
  training opportunities) to compensate and influence others.
- **Role:** a defined function to be performed by a project team member.
- **Safety Needs:** the second level of Maslow's hierarchy — security and protection, the desire for a
  predictable, orderly world within one's control.
- **Self-actualization:** the fifth and highest level of Maslow's hierarchy — reached when personal
  potential is fully realized after basic bodily and ego needs are fulfilled.
- **Self-awareness:** the ability to focus on yourself and how your actions/thoughts/emotions do or
  don't align with your internal standards.
- **Self-management, Self-control, Self-regulation:** the ability to regulate one's emotions,
  thoughts, and behaviors effectively — managing stress, motivating oneself, working toward personal
  and professional goals.
- **Self-organized team:** a group that manages its own tasks, roles, and workflows autonomously,
  making collective decisions and taking responsibility for its goals without direct external
  supervision.
- **Sensor (S) personality:** realistic, focused on facts/details, applies common sense and past
  experience to reach practical solutions.

### S-T

- **Shadowing:** a team member follows a work expert/professional for observation purposes, to learn
  or understand a specific job or role — via observation, regular briefing, or hands-on.
- **Situational Leadership:** the leader employs the appropriate leadership style depending on the
  situation, adapting behavior to the project needs and the group being influenced.
- **Situational power:** when the power holder happens to be the only one able to step up and rescue
  the team, project, or organization from a certain crisis.
- **Social awareness:** the ability to understand, empathize, and take the perspective of others,
  including those from diverse backgrounds and cultures.
- **Social skill:** the skills used to communicate and interact with each other — verbally and
  non-verbally, through gestures, body language, and personal appearance.
- **Storming:** the second team development stage — the most difficult/critical stage, as different
  individual personalities emerge, resulting in conflicts and poor performance.
- **Swarming:** a technique where multiple team members focus collectively on resolving a specific
  impediment.
- **Team assignments:** specify the resources assigned to each activity.
- **Team building:** the process of taking individuals with different needs, backgrounds, and
  expertise and transforming them into an integrated, effective work unit.

### T

- **Team charter:** an official record of established values, work guidelines, agreements, behavior
  and interaction directives, regulations, and rules the team should go by and adhere to.
- **Team Development Plan:** describes a training program for team members to enhance skills, both in
  the project work required and in working together as a team.
- **Team Management Plan:** describes when and how project team members will be acquired and how long
  they will be needed.
- **Team Member Turnover:** the rate at which people leave the team during a specified period — a high
  turnover rate usually signals a serious organizational issue and can cause decreased productivity,
  increased recruitment costs, bad reputation, and missed deadlines.
- **Teamwork:** the collaborative effort of a group to achieve a common goal or complete a task
  effectively and efficiently.
- **Thinker (T) personality:** makes decisions using logical analysis, objectively weighs pros/cons,
  values honesty, consistency, and fairness.
- **Training:** teaching, or developing in oneself or others, skills and knowledge related to useful
  competencies.
- **Training Baselining:** assessing a team's training efficiency by conducting a pre-training
  assessment (the baseline), then a post-training assessment to compare against it — showing the
  program's impact on the team's skills.
- **Training gap analysis:** determines the skills a team is lacking, in order to plan adequate
  training to close the gap.

### T-U-V-W-Z

- **Transactional leadership:** initially relies on rewards or punishment to motivate the team —
  "management by exception": the exception gets either rewarded or punished.
- **Transformational Leadership:** encourages teams to be innovative, creative, and ambitious, and not
  afraid to take action, while inspiring and motivating them toward the project's set goals.
- **Transition plan:** defines the priorities, goals, strategies, and techniques for a successful
  responsibility transfer — helps the team cope with change and maintain stability when a stakeholder
  permanently or temporarily departs.
- **Tuckman's Team Development Ladder:** a team goes through 5 development stages — Forming, Storming,
  Norming, Performing, and Adjourning.
- **Under-allocation:** when there are more resources than necessary to complete the job at hand, so
  there isn't enough workload for the available resources.
- **Virtual, remote, or distributed team:** has geographically distributed members who are not
  co-located and may never have met face-to-face, but still function as a unit.
- **Virtual collocation:** an arrangement whereby project team members "meet" as often as necessary
  through teleconferencing.
- **War room:** a physical room, office, or space for the team to use when they need to collectively
  address project activities, issues, or workflows.
- **Zero Sum reward:** rewarding only one or a few team members (e.g. an "employee of the month" award)
  — can create contempt or envy among those not rewarded.
