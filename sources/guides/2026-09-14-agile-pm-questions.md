## Question 1

While reviewing an important Epic, members of the self-organizing team had different interpretations of
how it should be broken down, which revealed a lack of common understanding of the project's purpose.
What should the project manager do?

A. Ask the team to check the release plan
B. Ask the team to check the acceptance criteria of the Epic
C. Remind the team of the sprint goal
D. Remind the team of the project vision

**Correct answer: D.** The project manager should remind the team of the project's vision since the team
lacks a common understanding of the project's purpose. The sprint goal only clarifies the vision of the
iteration, not the overall project vision. The release plan only helps the team understand the goal of
that specific release. An Epic's acceptance criteria help the team understand how to break it down, but
not the project's overall purpose.

## Question 2

After completing a release, the Scrum team is examining its burndown chart. Even though there were no
changes in estimates, the chart shows an increase in story point value during the 3rd sprint. How can
this be explained?

A. The team's velocity decreased
B. The team's velocity increased
C. Work was added to the Product Backlog
D. Work was removed from the Product Backlog

**Correct answer: C.** When work related to a release is added to the Product Backlog, the release story
points naturally increase. If work was not added, the team should have expected the total story points
to decrease according to their velocity — independent of whether velocity increased or decreased.

## Question 3

A project manager is leading a software project. The team finished all tasks in the iteration backlog
early and decided to work on code clean-up for the rest of the iteration. In spite of their intention to
decrease technical debt (which will save the customer maintenance fees in the future), neither the
customer nor the project manager seems happy. What could be the reason behind the customer's
dissatisfaction?

A. He wanted to avoid scope creep
B. He wanted to avoid gold plating
C. He wanted to prioritize the scope
D. He wanted to minimize costs by ending the iteration earlier

**Correct answer: C.** The probable reason is there were more prioritized items in the product backlog
the team could have worked on instead of code clean-up. Stakeholders are responsible for defining and
prioritizing scope and requirements for the project team (PMBOK 7th edition, page 14). Since the customer
didn't ask for the code clean-up work, it isn't scope creep.

## Question 4

A project manager is assigned to an internal project to create a new website. Months in, the completion
date is unclear because scope keeps changing as new knowledge is gained. New stakeholders joining later
express dissatisfaction, claiming outcomes don't align with business goals. What can the project manager
do to prevent similar issues in the future?

A. Develop a prioritized backlog and establish iteration review sessions with stakeholders to ensure
   alignment with business goals.
B. Create a detailed WBS and milestone schedule approved by all stakeholders.
C. Implement daily meetings to review team performance and address impediments promptly.
D. Organize kick-off meetings for each project phase to share the project management plan with new
   stakeholders.

**Correct answer: A.** Creating a prioritized backlog and holding iteration review sessions with
stakeholders — i.e., adopting an agile development approach — allows for continuous alignment with
business goals, enabling the team to adapt to evolving requirements and expectations.

## Question 5

During the 3rd sprint of an e-commerce project, the team hits unexpected technical complexity
integrating third-party payment gateways and user authentication. Despite estimation efforts, they
realize they won't complete all user stories in the sprint. What should the project manager do?

A. Cancel the sprint to regroup and plan more effectively for future sprints.
B. Engage in additional product backlog refinement to focus on achievable work in the remaining time.
C. Examine with the product owner the possibility of adjusting the sprint goal based on current
   progress and capacity.
D. Collaborate with the product owner to re-negotiate the remaining items of the sprint backlog.

**Correct answer: D.** During the sprint, the sprint backlog can be clarified and re-negotiated with the
product owner as more is learned, adjusting items based on the team's capacity and constraints while
keeping the sprint goal achievable.

## Question 6

To keep the agile team cross-functional, the project manager hires a designer. The designer reports that
his design application's subscription plan doesn't include functionality he needs, and asks for an
upgrade. What should the project manager do next?

A. Ask the designer to raise the issue during the next retrospective meeting
B. Tell the designer to be self-reliant and find workarounds
C. Explain that the current subscription plan is sufficient
D. Reach out to the appropriate stakeholder to upgrade the designer's subscription plan

**Correct answer: D.** As a servant leader, the project manager should reach out to the appropriate
stakeholder to resolve the impediment. In an agile environment, the PM should listen to impediments
raised by team members and act to solve them.

## Question 7

A product owner shares a table of key deliverables per quarter across a 9-month project, to guide the
development team on what they intend to achieve. What agile artifact does this table represent?

A. Product vision statement
B. Product wireframe
C. Product roadmap
D. Product backlog

**Correct answer: C.** The product roadmap is built by the product owner to demonstrate the anticipated
sequence of deliverables over the project duration (Agile Practice Guide, page 52) — it sets the
product's strategic view, short- and long-term.

## Question 8

A scrum master is facilitating a retrospective when a team member suggests changing the sprint timebox
from three weeks to two weeks. What should the scrum master do?

A. Investigate the cause behind the team member's suggestion to tailor the process
B. Accept the implementation of any suggestion from self-organizing team members
C. Decline the suggestion since it can reduce the team's velocity
D. Decline the suggestion since the sprint timebox is set at project start and should never change

**Correct answer: A.** The scrum master should investigate the cause behind the suggestion — it might
reveal other problems that could be fixed differently. The scrum master's role is to create and maintain
good working processes, examining and challenging new ideas with the team before implementation.

## Question 9

A project manager facilitates a meeting with the team and key stakeholders to prioritize product backlog
items by business value and risk level. (Drag-and-drop exercise ranking backlog placement from highest
to lowest priority.)

**Correct ranking (highest to lowest priority):** 1) Items of high value and low risk, 2) Items of high
value and high risk, 3) Items of low value and low risk, 4) Items of low value and high risk. This
reflects a risk-value matrix approach to backlog prioritization: maximize value while managing risk, so
high-value/low-risk work is pulled forward and low-value/high-risk work is pushed back.

## Question 10

A product owner joins a software company whose main measure of project success is the number of
features delivered into production. The team delivers a record number of features, but customers start
voicing frustration about bugs and issues. What should the product owner do?

A. Encourage the team to keep the same pace; issues will be fixed later on.
B. Add an item to the sprint backlog to deal with the technical debt.
C. Include customer satisfaction and quality KPIs as product success metrics.
D. Discuss customer feedback during the demonstration of the finished product.

**Correct answer: C.** This scenario depicts the **Hawthorne effect** — the act of measuring something
influences behavior. Measuring only output volume pushes the team toward quantity over
customer-satisfying quality (PMBOK 7th edition, page 112). Including customer satisfaction and quality
KPIs balances the team's focus.

## Question 11

Halfway through the current iteration, the progress report reveals the agile project is off track — even
though the project manager carefully sequenced and assigned all project work himself to meet the
iteration goal. What should the PM have done differently?

A. Train the team on how to implement the agile approach
B. Encourage the team to record their progress using the reporting system
C. Set up a contingency reserve
D. Encourage the team to self-organize to ensure their buy-in

**Correct answer: D.** The PM should have empowered the team to self-organize — handling task selection
and prioritization themselves — rather than solely sequencing and assigning work. This produces higher
buy-in and motivation by giving the team autonomy over how they accomplish their work (PMBOK 7th
edition, page 249).

## Question 12

During sprint planning, a task (an automatic reminder email for overdue invoices) is assigned 8 story
points total: 5 from the developers' estimate plus 3 from the testers' estimate. Which statement is
correct about this situation?

A. The project manager should be the one estimating the task, not the team
B. The task should be estimated as a whole and not per segment
C. Story points should never be estimated, they should be calculated
D. The team should involve relevant stakeholders in the task estimate

**Correct answer: B.** Story points estimate the total effort to bring a feature to life as a whole —
they shouldn't be assigned per segment/role. The team should consider the full effort a story requires
together, to ensure they can deliver the work they commit to.

## Question 13

An organization runs a pilot project to experiment with agile, hiring an agile coach since they've only
used the predictive method before. In the 4th sprint retrospective, the team complains that
organizational processes are hindering progress, causing delays and rework. What should the project
manager do?

A. Start dedicating part of daily standups to resolving impediments
B. Conduct a root cause analysis in the following sprint retrospective
C. Identify the organizational processes causing issues and work on removing, changing, or alleviating
   them
D. Encourage the team to be self-organizing by dealing with any problem in their way

**Correct answer: C.** Transitioning to agile doesn't mean every component of the approach will work
as-is for the organization. When new-process issues arise, best practice is to customize, tailor, and
adapt them to the team's needs for optimum efficiency.

## Question 14

A scrum master's team has doubled in size since they joined (originally 6 members), making collaboration
complicated, and standups keep running over their allotted time. What could the scrum master do?

A. Be more vigilant about standup duration so it doesn't exceed 15 minutes
B. Increase the standup timebox to 20 minutes
C. Split the team into two Scrum teams and apply Scrum of Scrums to coordinate between them
D. Switch to a predictive approach since Scrum doesn't suit teams over 9 members

**Correct answer: C.** Scrum of Scrums (aka meta-Scrum) coordinates the work of two or more Scrum teams
(Agile Practice Guide, page 111). Since the team now exceeds the recommended maximum of 9 members per
Scrum team, splitting it and using Scrum of Scrums is the logical alternative.

## Question 15

In a meeting, the product owner tells the agile project manager that many more functionalities need to
be added to the product, with no possibility of extending the deadline. What should the project manager
do?

A. Use the smoothing technique and allude that delays are possible in such cases
B. Inform the product owner that lower-priority work items will have to be dropped from the project
C. Create a separate backlog for the new functionalities and work on it when time allows
D. Inform the product owner that new functionalities must first be authorized through an official
   change request

**Correct answer: B.** Unlike predictive projects, agile projects don't require formal change requests
to change scope. But when higher-priority functionality is added and the timeframe can't extend,
lower-priority functionality has to be dropped to make room.

## Question 16

An organization assigns a Product Owner to a new agile project and asks them to create value and
generate ROI as early as possible. How can the product owner achieve this?

A. Share these priorities with the project team
B. Support and serve the project team
C. Prioritize product backlog items
D. Improve work processes

**Correct answer: C.** To get the most value earliest, the product owner should focus on prioritizing
product backlog items. Sharing priorities with the team matters, but the PO — not the team — owns the
product. Improving work processes and supporting/serving the team are the scrum master's focus, not the
product owner's.

## Question 17

During an iteration review meeting, the product owner rejects one of the features demonstrated by the
development team. What happens next to the rejected user story?

A. It's automatically moved to the next sprint backlog
B. It's deleted from the product backlog and the project
C. It's moved back to the product backlog for reprioritization
D. It's updated to address the reasons why it was rejected

**Correct answer: D.** The right step is understanding why the feature wasn't accepted, then making the
required updates — after which it can be moved back to the backlog for reprioritization. Deleting the
story isn't rational since the feature was already developed, implying it adds value to the product.

## Question 18

During the daily standup, two team members start debating which JavaScript framework to use (Angular,
React, or Vue). What should the project manager do?

A. Let the discussion continue since it's very important
B. Time-box the conversation and suggest the team carries on with this subject after the standup
C. Facilitate the conversation and invite the rest of the team to weigh in
D. Contribute to the discussion and share his own opinion on the matter

**Correct answer: B.** The daily scrum isn't an event for problem-solving activities; the team can
address the problem afterward with a small group of interested members (*Essential Scrum*, Rubin, page
24). The scrum master/PM should keep attendees focused on the core agenda and time-box side
conversations for after the standup.

## Question 19

During sprint planning for HR management software, a team member insists a user story about implementing
a rewards policy shouldn't be brought into the next sprint because it lacks detail on how rewards should
be set for different employee positions/roles. What's the most likely reason for pushing the story out?

A. The user story doesn't meet the Definition of Ready
B. The user story doesn't meet the Definition of Done
C. The user story involves an unachievable stakeholder requirement
D. The user story might result in a waste of time and effort

**Correct answer: A.** The story fails the **Definition of Ready (DoR)** — it isn't ready for
implementation because it doesn't describe how to define rewards per role/position. DoR is a checklist
of criteria that must be met before a user story is ready to enter a sprint for execution (Agile Practice
Guide, page 151).

## Question 20

A 7-day iteration burndown chart shows: planned 14 story points at Day 1, with the actual-progress line
staying above the ideal-burndown line throughout, ending at 3 remaining story points on Day 7. Which
statement is true about this chart?

A. The figure represents a burnup chart of the remaining story points
B. Throughout the iteration, completed story points are always more than projected
C. The team planned 14 story points for the iteration, which they completed
D. 3 story points are unfinished at the end of the iteration

**Correct answer: D.** At the end of the 7-day iteration, 3 story points are left undone (14 planned, 11
finished). The figure is a burndown chart, not a burnup chart. When the actual-progress line sits above
the ideal/projected line, it means the team is finishing *fewer* story points than committed.
