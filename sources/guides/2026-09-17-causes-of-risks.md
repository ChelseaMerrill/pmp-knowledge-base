## 1. Risk identification

Risk identification is the process of systematically recognizing potential risks that could impact a
project, organization, or objective. It involves analyzing internal and external factors that may create
uncertainties, categorizing risks, and documenting them for further assessment.

This process is crucial in proactive risk management, helping teams develop strategies to mitigate or
respond to threats *before* they become major issues. Risk identification is typically conducted through
brainstorming, expert judgment, historical data analysis, and risk assessment tools such as risk
registers or checklists.

## 2. Causes of risks — the 7 categories

Categorizing risks helps streamline brainstorming sessions by providing a structured approach to
identifying potential threats. It ensures that discussions cover all key areas rather than focusing on a
limited set of risks. For instance, the project team can use the following 7 categories to systematically
identify risks:

1. **Project-related** — risks that arise directly from the planning, execution, and management of a
   project.
   *Examples:* lack of knowledge, lack of processes, misunderstanding of requirements, inadequate
   planning.

2. **Financial** — risks related to the financial health and funding of a project or organization.
   *Examples:* raw materials price increase, fluctuations of interest rates, problems in cash flow or
   funding.

3. **Competitiveness** — risks arising from market competition and the ability to remain competitive.
   *Examples:* lack of resources, expensive skillful resources, lower opportunities, low ROI.

4. **Contractual** — risks associated with legal agreements and contracts.
   *Examples:* penalties, loss of the project.

5. **Occupational** — risks related to the health, safety, and well-being of workers.
   *Examples:* injury, burnout, high turnover.

6. **Environment** — risks arising from the impact of a project on the environment or vice versa.
   *Examples:* floods, natural disasters, earthquakes.

7. **Stakeholder-based** — risks arising from the expectations, actions, or reactions of stakeholders.
   *Examples:* communication issues, unsupportive stakeholders.
