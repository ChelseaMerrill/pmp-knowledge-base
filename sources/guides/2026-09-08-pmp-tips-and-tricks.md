# PMP Tips & Tricks (Exam Gift Deck)

*Ahmed Ben Hamouda — Ben Hamouda Consulting (PMP®, SCT®, SAFe®)*

## 1. Exam Logistics

### Exam Process
- **In-person:** test centers around the world.
- **Remote:** proctoring software.

### PMP Question Types
- ≈170 MCQ with 4 choices and 1 possible answer
- ≈5 MCQ with 5 choices and several possible answers
- ≈5 Drag and Drop questions

### Preparation Tips
- Create a study plan/schedule (1–2 hours a day)
- Decide on a specific date to sit for the exam
- Plan for the unexpected (go early to the test center, have a back-up internet connection at home)
- Take some time off: 1–2 weeks
- Commitment is key
- Start with getting familiar with your prep material
- Put your knowledge to the test
- Practice 2–5 full-length mock exams
- Choose your studying style according to your preferences
- Less memorizing & more understanding
- Surround yourself with people who support you
- Visualize reaching your goal for motivation

### Prior to the Exam Day
- Keep the same relaxed pace
- Rely on your personal notes
- Have enough sleep and a balanced diet
- Don't drink too much water

### Taking the Exam Online at Home
- The day of the exam, get your computer ready
- Don't leave anything within arm's reach
- Prepare your space (best to be alone at home)
- Perform a system test with OnVue
- Stay within the proctor/camera's view range
- Don't look around — never try to cheat, in any way

### Taking the Exam in a Test Center
- Visit the test center beforehand
- Check all of your testing center instructions
- Print out a copy of your registration e-mail
- Make sure your ID meets the requirements (passport, driver's license, etc.)
- Consider bringing a second identification document

### The Day of the Exam
- Avoid last-minute studying
- Dress comfortably and according to the rules
- Don't forget your registration proof and ID
- Avoid moving too much, talking to yourself, or looking in other directions
- Arrive 30 minutes early at your testing center

### Do Your Retrospective!
Do a retrospective of the training techniques against your own past and current work/personal life.
Imagine — or recall — how you would apply (or did apply) each technique/process in a real situation.
This helps you memorize the technique three times more effectively than reading it alone.

## 2–3. PMI Mindset and Tips & Tricks

### PMI Mindset
- Manage projects according to PMI's best practices.
- Follow the processes that PMI describes.
- Use the recommended tools and templates.
- Some questions can be answered based on your own experience.
- Most questions are best answered according to what we learn in the training.

The tips below follow the deck's own grouping: **Business Environment**, **Project Stakeholders**, and **Project Team** apply across both a Waterfall Approach and Agile Approaches (with Hybrid & Tailoring blending the two) — Scope, Schedule, Cost, Resources, Communication, Quality, Risk, and Procurement are the processes/tools & techniques layered on top, with Assessing Performance cutting across all of it.

### Business Environment Tips & Tricks
- **Code of ethics:** Honesty, Respect, Fairness, Responsibility.
- **Key skills:** Business acumen, Ways of working, Power skills.
- The project must deliver **Business Value** — Financial Gain, New Customers, Social Benefit, First to Market, Improvement, or Regularization.
- The Project Management Plan (Waterfall) explains how the project will be managed — and so does each knowledge area's subsidiary plan.
- Baselines (Scope, Schedule, Cost) are used for measuring performance.
- A project is temporary and unique; operations are ongoing and repetitive.
- PMO structures, by level of control: **Supportive** (low) → **Controlling** (moderate) → **Directive** (high).
- Organization structures: Functional, Matrix (weak/balanced/strong), Projectized.
  - In a Functional/Weak matrix, the **Functional Manager** decides.
  - In a Strong matrix or Projectized organization, the **PM** leads and decides (~100% PM power in Projectized).
- **OPA** are the company's internal processes, guidelines, and lessons learned.
- **EEF** are conditions surrounding the company.
- **Iron triangle** constraints: Scope, Cost, Schedule.
- Project success criteria must be defined at project start; projects must align with company strategy, meet constraints, and create/deliver value.
- Product Life Cycle > Project Life Cycle. Project < Program < Portfolio.
- **Business Case** = the study phase, before the project — it justifies the project's existence.
  - The PM isn't necessarily involved in the study phase, but must consult the business case if stakeholders aren't aligned on objectives/deliverables at project start.
  - Projects should never harm the company.
- **Project Charter** is based on the business case and is the first official document in the project — it authorizes the project's existence.
- Projects must sustain Planet, People, Profit.
- **SWOT:** SW = internal factors; OT = external (+/−) risks. Use it when the company is considering a new technology project.
- For a transition project, assess company culture / change acceptance.

### Project Stakeholders Tips & Tricks
- A **stakeholder** is anyone interested/involved in the project, who can influence the project work, or might be impacted by its results — Supportive, Resistant, or Neutral.
- Stakeholders must be involved to capture their requirements/expectations and deliver project work correctly.
- The **Sponsor** assigns the PM, provides authority, supports the team, and is the first escalation point when a matter is beyond the PM's authority. The sponsor sits on the Steering Committee (SteeCo); the customer is a key stakeholder.
- All stakeholder data is recorded in the **Stakeholder Register**.
- The **CCB** reviews and approves/rejects changes in Waterfall.
- In case of disagreement with a stakeholder: review the gap between the deliverable and the acceptance criteria, analyze root cause → take action and inform the stakeholder.
- NPS and mood charts are used to measure stakeholder satisfaction.
- Direct communication with stakeholders is encouraged.
- If a stakeholder isn't committed / skips meetings → review the stakeholder engagement plan.
- If there's missing information → review the communications management plan.
- The PM engages stakeholders based on their power/interest levels.

### Project Team Tips & Tricks
- Terminology: **Waterfall** → Project Team; **Agile** → Development Team.
- A team can be Functional (divided per specialty, as in operations) or Cross-functional (as in a project).
- Remote/virtual teams are the new normal — the PM and team lean on online collaboration tools (video-conferencing, meeting recording, digital information sharing, etc.).
- The PM uses skills-assessment techniques to hire people with the right skills.
- The PM ensures team development through training, mentoring, and coaching.
- People have different personalities and cultural backgrounds — the PM improves collaboration through team-building activities.
- Empowering team members (to participate in decisions and own their actions) improves team productivity and outcomes.
- The **Resource Management Plan** covers tools/techniques for managing both human and non-human resources.
- **Servant Leadership** is preferred: coaching, mentoring, and removing impediments.
- If the team is confused → review project goals, roles, and responsibilities with them.
- If a team member has performance issues → the PM should provide coaching/training.
- **RACI:** Responsible (does the task), Accountable (answers for the outcome), Consulted, Informed.
- Motivation theories: Theory X/Y/Z, Expectancy Theory, and Herzberg's intrinsic motivators / extrinsic hygiene factors.
- **Team development stages (Tuckman):** Forming → Storming → Norming → Performing → Adjourning.
  - Team-building is used in Storming.
  - The team charter (which contains Ground Rules) is used in Norming.
- **Emotional intelligence** is about understanding others and sympathizing with them.
- The **Communication Management Plan** defines what information to share, with whom, in what format, and how often.
- Avoid answers that add resources — that adds cost.
- In case of an issue, discussing it with the team is favored.
- In case of conflict: find the root cause first, and use face-to-face communication — in private, not in front of others.
- **Conflict-management techniques**, roughly best → weakest for long-term resolution:
  1. Confronting/problem-solving (win/win)
  2. Collaborating through PM/team (win/win)
  3. Compromising/reconciling (lose/lose, but keeps the relationship)
  4. Accommodating/smoothing (yield/lose)
  5. Forcing/directing (win/lose)
  6. Withdrawal/avoiding (lose/leave)
- **Leadership styles:** Situational (most common — matches style to situation), Servant, Democratic, Transformational, Laissez-faire, Transactional, Charismatic, Authentic, Autocratic, Interactional (a mix of Charismatic/Transactional/Transformational).
- **Power types:** Legitimate, Reward, Punish, Referent, Expert, Situational.

### Agile & Scrum Tips & Tricks
- **Scope:** fixed in Waterfall; flexible in Agile.
- Delivery cadence/release is defined by the PM/PO in consultation with the team, factoring in the customer contract.
- **Agile values:** collaborative team, incremental product, customer involvement, welcoming change. Customer involvement is a feedback source and reduces disagreement about deliverables.
- In Agile, progressive elaboration happens through iterations; Agile (and sometimes Waterfall) uses rolling wave planning through iterative cycles (sprints).
- **Iterative vs. Incremental:**
  - *Iterative* — goal is to refine the product; scope isn't defined up front; expects many changes; uses experimentation; welcomes feedback; delivers at the end.
  - *Incremental* — goal is time-to-market; scope is defined; expects minimal changes; delivers completed increments; earlier ROI; can deliver after each iteration.
  - A **Full Agile Approach** mixes both.
- **Lean** is continuous improvement of existing processes. **Kanban** is a pull-based approach that limits WIP.
- **Scrum values transparency** of information.
  - **Product Owner** — voice of the customer; prioritizes value; refines and owns the **product backlog**.
  - **Scrum Master** — facilitator, coach, servant leader; removes impediments and resolves conflicts.
  - **Development Team** — self-organized and cross-functional; plans/estimates its own work; owns the **sprint backlog**.
- No e-mail between SM/PO/Team — use direct communication.
- User stories (US) are estimated in **story points**. Acceptance criteria (AC) is part of the **Definition of Done (DoD)**.
- US and AC live in the product backlog (maintained by the PO); tasks live in the sprint backlog (maintained by the team). Both feed the shared artifacts: increments, the scrum board, and the impediment log.
- **MVP vs. MMP:** MVP gets feedback and minimizes risk; MMP speeds up time-to-market.
- In the sprint: first day is Planning; last day is Review (demo) + Retrospective; every day has a 15-minute status/impediments meeting.
- No other/urgent meetings are allowed mid-sprint. No scope changes mid-sprint either — the PO cancels the sprint instead if it won't deliver the expected value.
- Blocking issues are surfaced at the stand-up — find the root cause, then decide the action.
- **DoR** (Definition of Ready) applies at Planning; **DoD** applies at Review.
- **Burn-down chart** — tracks remaining sprint work (owned by the PM); below plan is good.
- **Burn-up chart** — tracks release/project scope (owned by the PO); above plan is good.
- Stakeholders can attend the Review meeting. The Scrum Master re-energizes the team at the Retrospective.
- The team aims for consistent velocity across sprints. Unfinished work doesn't count; work ends if the budget ends.
- **Scrum of Scrums** coordinates projects with multiple teams and Scrum Masters.
- **Lead time** (full end-to-end process) vs. **Cycle time** (between individual process steps).
- **On Agile generally:** also called Change-Driven, Iteration-Based, Adaptive, etc. Three characteristics: (1) plan each iteration, no advance planning, (2) changes are very welcome, (3) value is delivered throughout the project. *Doing Agile ≠ Being Agile.*

### Waterfall & Hybrid Tips & Tricks
- The **Scope Management Plan** (part of the Project Management Plan) describes the tools/techniques used to manage scope.
- The **scope baseline** = the approved Scope Statement + WBS + WBS Dictionary.
- **WBS** helps the team visualize project work and organize scope — it's deliverables-oriented.
- **Scope control:** the team verifies deliverables align with requirements. **Scope validation:** the customer approves the deliverables.
- The PM should never accept a requirement without its acceptance criteria — that avoids conflicts with stakeholders later.
- A good deliverable is both **fit for use** and **fit for purpose**.
- Key stakeholders should stay involved in every phase to keep them engaged and secure acceptance of deliverables.
- **Lead** and **Lag** are set during the Planning phase.
- **Resource leveling** — for over-allocated resources; expands the schedule. **Resource smoothing** — uses slack instead; doesn't expand the schedule.
- The **critical path** is the minimum duration to complete the project; every activity on it matters and has zero float.
- **Float** = how much an activity can slip; **slack** = how much its start can be delayed.
- **Schedule crashing** (add resources → costs more) and **fast-tracking** (run activities in parallel → riskier) are compression techniques used during controlling, and both require a change request.
  - When behind schedule, check fast-tracking first — avoid anything that raises cost.
- **Integrated Change Control Process:**
  1. Change is raised (deviation from baselines, new requirements, or unmet acceptance criteria)
  2. PM creates a change request with impact assessment/analysis
  3. PM logs the change into the change log
  4. PM submits the CR to the CCB
  5. CCB reviews and decides (defer / reject / approve)
  6. PM updates the change log with the decision and informs stakeholders
  7. If approved, PM updates baselines and implements the change
  8. Monitor results
- Always evaluate before deciding or acting — include the team. Always check with the team/sponsor before raising a change request.
- **Scope creep** is the uncontrolled expansion of scope.
- A **murder-board** decides whether a project continues past each phase/kill point.
- **Hybrid** mixes Agile and Waterfall — recognizable when a question says so explicitly, or mixes terms from both. Always identify which part of the project you're currently in (Agile or Waterfall) and answer from that practice.
- **Tailoring** happens during planning (and can continue for improvement) — choosing the processes/tools that suit the project, per OPA/PMO.
- **On Waterfall generally:** also called Traditional, Classical, Predictive, Plan-Based, Linear, etc. Three characteristics: (1) a full plan up front — fixed scope, no uncertainty, (2) changes are neither easy nor welcome (a thorough, lengthy change process), (3) deliverables ship at the end.

### Planning Techniques

**Collecting requirements**
- *Waterfall:* happens at any point during planning; any scope change later must go through the change process.
- *Agile:* acceptance criteria mostly firm up during planning.
- Techniques: Expert Opinion, Delphi (anonymous expert questionnaire), Affinity (grouping related ideas), Nominal Group (ranking grouped ideas), Brainstorming (max ideas), Focus Group (PM-facilitated with stakeholders), Benchmarking (comparing products), Voting (Unanimity 100%, Majority 51%+, Plurality = most votes, Dictatorship = one person decides).

**Estimation techniques**
- Preliminary Estimate (Rough Order of Magnitude): −25% / +75%
- Conceptual Estimate (Budgetary): −10% / +25%
- Definitive Estimate: −5% / +10%
- Analogous Estimating (top-down): compares to a prior project's total duration/cost — single point or range.
- Bottom-Up Estimating: the most accurate technique.
- Parametric Estimating: multiplies by a measurement unit.
- Three-Point Estimating — Triangular: (P + M + O) / 3; PERT/Beta: (P + 4M + O) / 6.
- In Agile, estimating leans on average velocity from previous sprints.
- **Wideband Delphi:** the team discusses estimates after each round until they converge.
- **Alternatives analysis:** compares time & cost across options. **Spike:** a timeboxed research investigation.
- A **sunk cost** is already spent and unrecoverable — irrelevant to future decisions.

**Traps and biases to know**
- **Gold Plating:** adding quality the customer didn't ask for and won't pay for.
- **Customer Obsession:** saying yes to every customer request.
- **Multi-tasking:** starting many tasks without finishing any per plan — hurts the schedule.
- **Pareto:** 20% of effort produces 80% of the outcome.
- **Parkinson's Law:** effort expands to fill the time allotted.
- **Student Syndrome:** delaying the start of a task.
- **Dropped Baton:** one late team member delays the whole timeline.
- **Tacit Knowledge:** experience-based, hard to transfer or record. **Explicit Knowledge:** codified and easy to express.
- For a resource conflict in a functional/weak organization, talk to the functional managers first.

### Performing Work Tips & Tricks
- Compliance requirements always take priority.
- **Kanban** works as an information-sharing radiator — post it somewhere central and visible.
- Meetings matter for: sharing information, clarifying disagreements, finding root cause, and resolving conflicts.
- Status meetings happen in Waterfall only; kickoff and Steering Committee meetings happen in both Waterfall and Agile.
- **Lessons Learned** record how issues were resolved, for the benefit of future projects.

### Procurement Management Tips & Tricks
- The **Procurement Management Plan** (part of the Project Management Plan, in Waterfall) sets the approach, processes, guidelines, tools, and templates for procurement.
- In a **bidder conference**, the buyer answers seller questions and clarifies bid terms/conditions.
- **RFQ** = Request For Quotation · **RFP** = Request For Proposal · **RFI** = Request For Information.
- **SoW (Statement of Work)** describes how the work will be performed and under what conditions.
- Vendor selection: Screening → Weighting/Scoring (filtering by qualitative evaluation criteria).
- **Exit criteria** are the conditions a contract/project must meet to be considered complete.
- Procurement documents include: Letter of Intent, Purchase Order (PO), Memorandum of Understanding (MOU), Service Level Agreement (SLA), Memorandum of Agreement (MOA), Basic Ordering Agreement (BOA), and the Contract itself. **Awarding:** the contract goes to the vendor with the highest score.
- **Inspection** reviews the seller's delivery/performance; **statistical sampling** is one inspection technique.
- **Fixed-Price Contract** — used when scope is well-defined and changes aren't expected; cost-overrun risk sits with the seller. (FFP, FPIF, FPEPA)
- **Cost-Reimbursable Contract** — used when scope/requirements aren't well-defined; cost-overrun risk sits with the buyer. (CPFF, CPAF, CPIF)
- **Time & Material Contract** — hybrid; used for freelancers, experts, and consultants.
- For a team performance issue, hiring an external resource is not the favored fix.
- A **procurement audit** formally reviews procurement processes, procedures, and activities.
- **Negotiation** matters both at contract award and in disputes — win-win is favored.
- Force majeure draws on the **management reserve**.
- **Dispute resolution steps:** (1) Direct negotiation (e.g. meeting the vendor), (2) Indirect negotiation (mediation/arbitration), (3) Litigation at court.
- **Closing the contract** happens once all deliverables meet requirements, done administratively correctly.
- Generally, avoid answers that raise cost (awards, hiring externals, crashing, asking for more budget) — unless the situation truly calls for it.

### Quality Management Tips & Tricks
- Quality is a practice, a mindset, and a process. **Grading**, by contrast, is just ranking between products.
- In Agile, quality is customer-centric; in Waterfall, it's process-centric.
- **Regulations** must be complied with; **standards** are guidelines, not requirements.
- **Kaizen** is continuous improvement.
- The **Quality Management Plan** (part of the Project Management Plan, in Waterfall) sets out the quality processes/tools to apply.
- **Quality Assurance** happens during the work, to prevent defects. **Quality Control/Review** happens after work is completed, to detect defects via inspection and testing.
- **Quality audit** checks how well — and how efficiently — the process is being applied.
- **Root cause analysis tools:** Fishbone diagram, 5 Whys, Why-Why diagrams.
- **Cost of conformance:** prevention (training/documentation) + appraisal (testing/inspection).
- **Cost of non-conformance:** internal (rework/scrap, via change request) or external (liabilities/warranty).
- **Technical debt** is delayed error-correction — the longer it waits, the costlier it gets.
- Once the CCB approves a change request, implement it exactly as approved.
- To ensure full quality compliance during execution → continually survey the quality of the deliverable.
- For non-compliance: find the root cause → fix it (may need a CR) → review/update the process.
- In Agile, use the retrospective to dig into the root cause of quality problems and plan fixes with the team.

### Uncertainty & Risk Tips & Tricks
- A **risk** is a potential/uncertain event — positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) — recorded in the risk register. Risk probability is never 100% or 0%.
- An **issue** is recorded in the issue log — it's already happened, and it's always negative.
- **Preventive action** addresses a risk; **corrective action/workaround** addresses an issue. Risks arise from a **trigger condition**.
- **Risk Management Process:**
  1. Prepare the risk management plan
  2. Identify risks (into the risk register)
  3. Qualitative analysis (probability & impact) for prioritization
  4. Quantitative analysis (e.g. Expected Monetary Value, Monte Carlo)
  5. Plan risk responses
  6. Implement risk responses & update the risk register
  7. Monitor risk
- **Volatility** (≠ predictability) — unpredictable, fast-changing.
- **Uncertainty** (≠ understanding) — unclear about present/future.
- **Complexity** (≠ simplicity) — many interconnected factors.
- **Ambiguity** (≠ clarity) — lack of clarity about the event.
- **Urgency** — how soon the risk response needs to happen to be effective.
- **Dormancy** — how long a risk's impact stays hidden after it occurs.
- **Proximity** — how much time remains before a risk can hit its objective(s).
- **Connectivity** — whether the risk relates to other individual project risks.
- **Individual risk** touches one or more objectives; **overall project risk** touches the project as a whole.
- **Insurable risk** can be transferred to an insurer.
- **Known unknowns** (identified risks) draw on the **contingency reserve** (inside the project budget). **Unknown unknowns** (unidentified risks) draw on the **management reserve** (outside PM authority).
- **Risk Breakdown Structure (RBS):** Technical, External, Organizational, Managerial.
- **Risk appetite** — the limit of what's inconsequential. **Risk tolerance** — the limit of what's acceptable. **Risk averse** — unwilling to take on risk.
- **Active acceptance** (take some action) vs. **passive acceptance** (just monitor).
- **Threat responses:** Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer (e.g. insurance), Accept, Escalate.
- **Opportunity responses:** Exploit, Enhance, Share (e.g. with a partner), Accept, Escalate. (Escalate applies when the situation is beyond the PM/team's authority.)
- **Risk audit** — checks the effectiveness of the risk management process. **Risk review** — re-checks the effectiveness of the response plans.

### Assessing Project Performance — Tips & Tricks
- On a performance variance: find the root cause, then take corrective or preventive action.
- Earned Value terms to know: AC, PV, BAC, plus CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC, ETC, VAC, TCPI.
- **CV and SV both > 0** (CPI and SPI both > 1) → ahead of schedule and under budget. Resource leveling / crashing can be considered if needed.
- **CV and SV both < 0** (CPI and SPI both < 1) → behind schedule and over budget. Crashing and schedule extension aren't options here — consider fast-tracking instead.
- Use **Cost-Benefit analysis** to make the case for a solution to stakeholders.
- The project with the highest **IRR** is the most beneficial (ROI is the related metric).
- **NPV** is the present value of future income — negative NPV is a bad sign.
- **Payback period** — how long it takes to break even on the investment.
- **Time2Market** — how long until the product reaches the market.

### General Tips & Tricks
- Prefer the specific answer over the general one (e.g. business case over SWOT, when both seem to apply).
- When a question asks what to do *first*, several options can be technically correct — pick the one that comes first chronologically. It's usually assess / evaluate / analyze / review.
- Always apply the full, correct solution — avoid shortcuts or half-measures.
- Face the issue directly; don't delegate it away.
- Escalation (to sponsor/SteeCo) is a last resort, not a first move.
- The PM chooses the project management methodology — don't back off a transition to Agile once it's underway.

### Scenarios
When a question asks what to do next, work it in order:
1. What's the context/situation in the question?
2. Waterfall? Hybrid? Agile?
3. Risk? Change? Conflict? Sprint?
4. Follow the next step in that process/mindset.

Disagreement with a stakeholder, specifically:
1. Analyze the gap and compare it against the acceptance criteria.
2. **If we're right** → meet with the stakeholder to convince them. Convinced → done. Not convinced → escalate.
3. **If we're wrong** → take corrective action (with a change request) → inform the stakeholder.

Don't change the project management approach just because the team doesn't know it yet — favor bringing the team up to speed (e.g. transitioning them to Agile) instead.

## 4. Elimination Techniques

Some questions can only be solved by elimination. **Automatically rule out an answer that:**
- Does nothing / ignores the issue (a passive action)
- Escalates to a higher authority — unless escalation is genuinely the right call for that scenario
- Fires, replaces, or releases a team member
- Asks the team to work overtime
- Delegates the solution away
- Describes a process that's irrelevant to the situation

## 5. Time Management

**Exam structure:** 180 questions in 3h50min, run as three 60-question blocks with a 10-minute break after each of the first two.

- 1 question ≈ 230 min ÷ 180 = 1 min 17 sec
- Using the translation feature: budget up to 3 questions per 60-question block
- 20 questions ≈ 25 min
- Marking questions for later review: up to 3 per 60-question block
- 60 questions ≈ 1h16min
- Review flagged questions before taking each break

**Practice pacing:**
- First 3 mock exams — 60 questions/day; stopwatch every question against the 1:17 target and track the +/− delta.
- Last 2 mock exams — stopwatch the entire exam as one block.
- **Objective:** if you're still on a question at 50 seconds, that's your cue — you have 20 seconds left to pick an answer.

*Worked example (mock exam 17):* Q1 in 1:02 (−15 sec) · Q2 in 0:51 (+26 sec) · Q3 in 1:40 (−23 sec) → net −18 seconds after 3 questions, i.e. ahead of pace.

## 6. Real Exam Questions

**Q1.** A project member notices that certain team members confuse projects and operations, often treating operations as ongoing projects. The PM clarifies the difference by defining operations as:
A. An undertaken temporary endeavor aimed at making a distinctive product, service, or result
B. The application of skills, knowledge, tools, and techniques in a project to meet its requirements
C. A collection of programs grouped to achieve strategic business goals
D. Ongoing endeavors that generate repetitive outputs

**Correct answer: D**

**Q2.** In a weak matrix organization, who is in charge of hiring when additional employees are needed?
A. The project manager
B. The sponsor
C. The project team
D. The functional manager

**Correct answer: D**

**Q3.** In which type of organization would a project manager have the *most* control?
A. Projectized organization
B. Weak matrix organization
C. Balanced matrix organization
D. Strong matrix organization

**Correct answer: A**

**Q4.** A senior software engineer is newly assigned to manage an Agile project. To ensure success, the PM should first:
A. Identify all risks
B. Determine all of the sprints' activities
C. Define the project success criteria
D. Define quality metrics

**Correct answer: C**

**Q5.** A PM is assigned to build new software for a client; all requirements have been gathered. What should the team focus on?
A. Reducing cost for the customer
B. Delivering value to the customer
C. Working together as a team
D. Finishing the project on schedule

**Correct answer: B**

**Q6.** A large organization adopted Agile based on industry trends and invested heavily, but the implementation failed after a few months. What should the PM have done differently?
A. Conducted Agile training for all employees, from project teams through upper management
B. Conducted a postmortem and captured the learning in OPAs for future use
C. Assessed the organization's culture, performed a suitability analysis, and determined the future state
D. Hired a third-party Agile delivery team to do the implementation

**Correct answer: C**

**Q7.** A company uses a predictive approach for a component per the scope management plan. Due to changing regulatory requirements, the development team requests to use Agile. What should the PM do?
A. Escalate the request for additional financial resources
B. Forward the request to the PMO to amend the OPAs
C. Ask the sponsor to obtain approval for the new approach
D. Analyze the requirements that would need to be addressed under the requested method

**Correct answer: D**

**Q8.** During closing, the PM learns a deliverable isn't meeting customer expectations. How should the PM proceed?
A. Ask the customer to issue a change request with information to analyze and provide a solution
B. Review the deliverable requirements, check the customer approval criteria, and proceed accordingly
C. Meet with the customer to reach agreement on a scope that satisfies both parties
D. Contact the sponsor for help negotiating the closing with the customer
