## A

- **100-point method, 100-dollar, Fixed sum, Fixed allocation:** a prioritization technique where each
  participant has 100 points to distribute across available options according to importance.
- **3 C's of User Stories:** a model for writing user stories using three components — Card,
  Conversation, and Confirmation. Stories are written on cards, discussed to build shared
  understanding, and their completion is confirmed through acceptance criteria.
- **Absolute estimation:** assigns a specific unit of time, effort, or cost to a work item without
  comparing it to other similar items.
- **Acceptance criteria:** required standards, capabilities, or conditions a user story, task, feature,
  or product backlog item must meet to be accepted by the product owner.
- **Adaptive approach, Change-driven approach:** a project development approach suited to high
  uncertainty/volatility, where requirements are likely to change throughout the project.
- **Agile:** a particular mindset based on a well-defined set of values and principles.
- **Agile Center of Excellence (CoE), Agile PMO, Value Delivery Office (VDO):** a department that
  supports and guides agile adoption and practices.
- **Agile Coach:** a project management professional who helps scale agile practices across a team or
  organization.
- **Agile Framework:** a set of principles and practices designed to execute a project in alignment
  with the agile mindset.

## A-B

- **Agile Manifesto:** a document by "The Agile Alliance" identifying 4 core values and 12 principles.
- **Agile mindset:** the way of thinking and acting according to agile values and principles.
- **Agile practices:** the activities and processes an agile team uses to apply the agile mindset to
  its workload.
- **Agile principles:** 12 guiding principles forming the foundation of agile.
- **Agile Transformation:** the process of shifting an organization to agile ways of working.
- **Agile Values:** the 4 values — Individuals and interactions over processes and tools; Working
  software over comprehensive documentation; Customer collaboration over contract negotiation;
  Responding to change over following a plan.
- **Artifact:** any information depicting the product and the actions the agile team takes to keep
  project work aligned with requirements and business goals — a user story, product backlog, burndown
  chart, product increment, etc.
- **Backlog refinement, Backlog grooming:** the process of regularly reviewing and updating the product
  backlog so it stays accurate, detailed, estimated, and prioritized.
- **Behavior-Driven Development (BDD):** a software development process encouraging collaboration
  between developers, testers, and non-technical/business participants.

## B-C

- **Blackout period:** when product development work is paused for a predefined period to dedicate time
  to rolling out a new product increment.
- **Blended agile:** implementing a hybrid framework that combines practices and elements of two or
  more agile frameworks.
- **Burndown chart:** demonstrates remaining work the team has to complete, illustrated with a downward
  line.
- **Burnup chart:** displays the amount of work completed, illustrated with an upward line.
- **Ceremony:** a meeting with a defined length, frequency, and goal. The four main agile ceremonies:
  sprint planning, daily stand-up, sprint review, and sprint retrospective.
- **Commitment:** a flexible agreement based on the team's best understanding during planning.
- **Continuous delivery:** a series of automated processes for delivering software from development to
  production.
- **Continuous deployment:** automatically releasing code changes to production after passing all
  tests.
- **Cross-functional team:** made of members with diverse, substantial skill sets, so the team can
  produce deliverables with no external dependency.

## C-D

- **Crystal Family of Methodologies:** a set of agile software development approaches; the darker the
  color, the bigger the team and the more complex the project.
- **Cumulative Flow Diagram (CFD):** a visual representation of the status of work items in the
  workflow — showing work in progress, completed, and remaining.
- **Customer-Centric Development:** focusing on delivering value to customers through iterative
  feedback and improvements.
- **Daily Scrum, Daily standup, Standup meeting:** a short daily meeting for the team to plan its work
  for the day and identify obstacles that could impact that work.
- **DEEP:** an acronym — Detailed, Emergent, Estimated, Prioritized — a set of criteria for assessing
  and creating a good product backlog.
- **Definition of Done (DoD):** a set of criteria a product or increment must meet to be considered
  ready for use — the product's quality checklist.
- **Definition of Ready (DoR):** a checklist of user-centric criteria a user story must meet to be
  considered ready for execution, moving it from the product backlog to the sprint backlog.
- **Development team:** a small team (10 members or less) committed to delivering a potentially
  shippable product increment at the end of each sprint.
- **DevOps:** "Developers" + "Operations" — a software development strategy helping teams in an
  organization work together more efficiently to produce higher-quality products and apps.

## D-E-F

- **Disciplined Agile (DA), Disciplined Agile Delivery (DAD):** a scaled agile framework that puts
  individuals first, offering only lightweight guidance so teams can optimize processes for their
  specific project's needs.
- **Ease of Use, Usability:** how much effort it takes a user to access a product's functionality.
- **Empirical measurement:** making decisions based on observation and experimentation rather than
  theory and predictions.
- **Epic:** represents a business value to the customer; may fit within, or span across, more than one
  release.
- **Escaped defects:** the number of defects per unit of time, per sprint, or per release, that the
  agile team missed and which are detected by end-users after the product release.
- **Exploratory testing:** software testing where the tester explores the application without
  predefined test cases to identify defects.
- **Extreme Programming (XP):** an agile framework targeting speed and simplicity with short
  development cycles.
- **Fail fast:** testing assumptions and ideas as quickly and cheaply as possible, before investing too
  much time, money, or resources — so you learn what works and what doesn't.
- **Feature:** a shippable component for the customer; usually fits within a release.

## F-G-I

- **Feature chart:** displays completed features using a burnup chart, or remaining features using a
  burndown chart.
- **Feature-Driven Development (FDD):** an agile method suited to larger-scale projects; short
  iterations, features as the basic unit of work.
- **Fibonacci sequence:** a scoring scale for estimating agile story points: 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21…
- **Flow:** the smooth progression of tasks through a process to completion.
- **Go Live:** the moment a product increment gets shipped to end-users.
- **Ideal time, Ideal hour, Ideal day:** the time/hour/day it would take to complete a given task
  assuming zero interruptions.
- **Impediment, Blocker, Bottleneck:** anything that slows the team down and delays completion of
  project work.
- **Increment:** a tested and accepted deliverable at the end of each sprint — a functional subset of
  the project's overall outcome.
- **Incremental approach:** delivers ready-to-use product functionalities to the customer at the end of
  each iteration.

## I-K-L

- **Incremental budgeting, Incremental funding:** allocating budgets in small increments based on
  project progress and needs.
- **INVEST:** an acronym for the criteria used to assess user story quality — Independent, Negotiable,
  Valuable, Estimable, Small, and Testable.
- **I-shaped:** a team member with deep specialization/expertise in one domain, and no interest in
  work outside that domain.
- **Iteration:** the process of repeatedly refining and enhancing a project or product through a
  cyclical approach, with feedback from each cycle informing the next.
- **Iterative approach:** step-by-step development with frequent reviews and feedback loops, based on a
  roughly finalized part of a project or product.
- **Kanban board, Task board:** an agile project management tool for visualizing work, limiting
  work-in-progress, and maximizing efficiency.
- **Kanban framework, Kanban approach, Kanban method:** an agile method aiming at continuous
  improvement, flexibility in task management, and enhanced workflow.
- **Large Scale Scrum (LeSS):** an agile framework for scaling Scrum to multiple teams working together
  on a single product.
- **Lean Software Development:** a concept emphasizing optimized efficiency and minimized waste in the
  software development process.

## M-P

- **Minimum Marketable Product (MMP):** a release-ready version of the product with the minimum
  features required to fulfill end-user needs.
- **Minimum Viable Product (MVP), Proof of Concept (PoC):** a product's basic functionalities/features,
  often shown to early users to solicit feedback and validate the initial idea.
- **Persona:** a fictional model of a potential user, used to help the agile team understand the target
  audience's needs, behaviors, goals, and pain points.
- **Planning poker, Scrum poker, Pointing poker:** a gamified, card-based estimation method where each
  member uses poker cards to estimate and vote on each user story.
- **Potentially shippable product increment:** a completed and tested set of features demonstrated
  during the sprint review, ready to be released to end users.
- **Product Backlog:** a prioritized list of all desired features, enhancements, bug fixes, and other
  work needed on a product.
- **Product Backlog Item (PBI):** a single element of work in the product backlog — a feature, user
  story, defect, technical work, knowledge acquisition, etc.
- **Product owner:** serves as the Single Point Of Contact (SPOC) between stakeholders and the
  development team, facilitating communication and guiding the product's development per the defined
  vision and roadmap.
- **Product roadmap:** a high-level map, created and maintained by the product owner, giving context to
  the vision by defining product development milestones.

## P-R-S

- **Product vision:** a brief statement of the desired future state that developing and deploying the
  product would achieve.
- **Progressive elaboration:** starting with a broadly outlined idea and scope, then elaborating a
  specific, detailed plan as more information becomes available.
- **Relative estimation:** estimating the size/complexity of tasks and stories (e.g. story points) by
  comparing them to each other.
- **Release:** the final delivery of a software package after completing multiple iterations/sprints.
- **Release Plan:** maps out how and when features/functionalities will be released and delivered to
  users.
- **Risk-adjusted Backlog:** a backlog that includes work and actions to address threats and
  opportunities.
- **Risk-value matrix:** a risk management tool that prioritizes user stories according to their risk
  and value.
- **Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe):** a structured framework for implementing agile practices at an
  enterprise level.
- **Scaled Agile frameworks, Enterprise Agile frameworks:** frameworks applying agile principles/
  practices across multiple teams and departments — e.g. SAFe, Scrum of Scrums, LeSS, Disciplined
  Agile.

## S

- **Scrum:** the most common agile framework, guiding teams in the incremental delivery of a product.
- **Scrumban:** incorporates Scrum practices with the Kanban method, giving teams flexibility when
  transitioning between Scrum and Kanban.
- **Scrum Master:** responsible for facilitating the Scrum process, ensuring the Scrum team adheres to
  its principles and practices.
- **Scrum of Scrums:** a scaled agile framework connecting multiple Scrum teams that need to work
  together on a complex product.
- **Scrum pillars:** Scrum rests on 3 pillars — Transparency, Inspection, and Adaptation.
- **Scrum team:** a small team composed of the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Development team.
- **Scrum values:** the five Scrum values — Commitment, Courage, Focus, Openness, and Respect.
- **Self-organized team:** a team that works autonomously and assumes responsibility for achieving
  project objectives without relying on outsiders.
- **Servant leadership:** a leadership style where the leader's primary focus is serving and empowering
  team members.
- **Spike:** a special type of user story used to gain the knowledge needed to reduce risk in a
  technical approach, better understand a requirement, or improve the reliability of a story estimate.
- **Sprint:** a timeboxed iteration, usually 1-4 weeks, during which the development team collaborates
  to deliver a potentially shippable product increment.
- **Sprint backlog:** a list of work items selected from the product backlog by the Scrum team to
  complete during the sprint.
- **Sprint commitment:** the development team's agreement to complete the items on the Sprint Backlog
  within the sprint.
- **Sprint goal:** the objective set for the sprint, describing a business purpose or value. Only the
  Product Owner can cancel the sprint if the sprint goal becomes obsolete.
- **Sprint planning, Iteration Planning:** the process of defining what can be delivered in the
  sprint/iteration and how that work will be achieved.
- **Sprint Retrospective, Reflection:** a review after sprint completion to determine what went well and
  what didn't, to identify areas of improvement.
- **Sprint review, Sprint Demo, Iteration Review, Demonstration:** a meeting at the end of a sprint to
  present a potentially shippable product increment to the product owner and key stakeholders, and get
  their feedback.
- **Story Point:** a unitless, arbitrary measure of the effort required to implement a task or user
  story, factoring in its complexity and associated unknowns.

## S-T

- **Story pointing:** a relative-estimating measurement technique used to determine the size of a work
  unit.
- **Sustainable pace:** working at a pace that can be maintained indefinitely without causing burnout.
- **Team Empowerment, Ownership:** giving teams the authority and responsibility to make decisions.
- **Team-level Agile frameworks:** frameworks applying agile principles/practices within a single team,
  such as Scrum, Kanban, and Extreme Programming (XP).
- **Technical Excellence:** striving for high standards in technical work to improve product quality
  and maintainability.
- **Test Automation:** using software tools to run tests on code automatically.
- **Test-Driven Development (TDD):** an agile method that writes tests before writing the code, to
  detect and correct errors early in development.
- **Theme:** represents large groups of value connected and united under a common area of focus; each
  theme is divided into epics.
- **Throughput:** the amount of work delivered over a specified period.

## T-U-V

- **Throughput chart:** shows accepted deliverables over a given period of time, typically as a bar
  chart.
- **Timebox:** a fixed amount of time allotted to get work done.
- **T-Shaped, Paint drip, Broken comb:** a generalizing specialist — one or multiple skills with deep
  expertise in a particular field, while other skills are less developed. T-shaped people are usually
  more willing to collaborate.
- **T-shirt sizing:** a non-numerical scale for sizing work items, especially epics — e.g. XS for extra
  small, XL for extra big.
- **User Experience (UX):** the overall experience and satisfaction a user has interacting with the
  product — ease of use, accessibility, efficiency.
- **User Interface (UI):** the visual elements, controls, and interactions that let users interact with
  and navigate the product.
- **User story:** a brief description of a software feature/functionality, written simply and
  non-technically, capturing the end user's perspective — what they need and why.
- **User Story mapping:** a tool to visually organize work into models, tracking the value chain of
  each work element by following a user story, its corresponding sprint, and its parent epic.
- **Value Stream:** the sequence of activities/steps an organization undertakes to deliver a product or
  service, from concept to delivery.

## V-W

- **Value stream mapping:** a lean technique for visualizing and measuring value-adding vs.
  non-value-adding activities, to remove waste from an existing process, system, or product.
- **Velocity:** the measure of the amount of work a development team can complete within a single
  sprint, typically in story points.
- **Velocity Chart:** an overview of how much work has been done in each sprint.
- **Wireframing:** mockups depicting the skeleton of the product, used to gather feedback and guide
  designers in developing the UI/UX.
- **Work In Progress (WIP):** the number of tasks in progress — work started but not yet completed.
