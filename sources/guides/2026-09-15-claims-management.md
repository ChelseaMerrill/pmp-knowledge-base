# Claims Management

## 1. Definitions

1. **Risks:** uncertain events or conditions that could have a positive or negative impact on project
   objectives.
2. **Conflicts:** disagreements between team members, stakeholders, or parties involved in the project.
3. **Claims:** formal requests or demands made by one party to another, often related to contractual
   obligations, payments, or performance issues.
4. **Dispute:** a formal disagreement or conflict between parties that may require resolution through
   negotiation, mediation, or legal means.

The four terms form an escalation chain:

- Risks can lead to conflicts if not managed properly.
- Conflicts can escalate into claims if parties feel their interests are not being addressed.
- Claims that remain unresolved can turn into disputes, potentially requiring formal resolution
  mechanisms (see [dispute-resolution-techniques.md](2026-09-15-dispute-resolution-techniques.md)).

## 2. Main factors leading to claims and disputes

Claims and disputes in projects often arise due to a variety of factors:

1. Failure to understand and/or comply with a contractual obligation.
2. Errors and/or omissions in the contract document.
3. Unrealistic contract duration or completion date.

## 3. Claims Management Process

The claims management process is a structured approach to identifying, assessing, resolving, and
preventing claims in a project or contractual environment. It is essential for minimizing disputes,
ensuring fair outcomes, and maintaining positive relationships between parties.

It has four stages:

1. **Claim Prevention:** identifying risks and preventing them from escalating into claims.
2. **Claim Mitigation:** setting up good documentation, change order processes, and a Dispute Resolution
   Board (DRB).
3. **Pursuing Claims:** (1) claim identification — proper documentation; (2) claim quantification —
   determining the additional compensation or time extension being sought.
4. **Claim Resolution:** finding a way to resolve the claim and/or close the contract.

These four stages overlap across the procurement lifecycle rather than happening one after another:

- **Claim Prevention** spans the Discovery and Pre-award stages.
- **Claim Mitigation** starts partway through Pre-award and continues through the Proposal Process stage
  and into Contract Execution & Management.
- **Pursuing Claims** and **Claim Resolution** both run through Contract Execution & Management and
  Contract Closeout.
