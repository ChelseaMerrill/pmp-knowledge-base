## Scenario 1

**Situation:** The PM will manage a project that has some clear information about the requirements, in
addition to some other unclear requirements.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should opt for an iterative, Agile, or hybrid project development approach.
- The PM should not opt for Predictive or Incremental approaches.
- The PM should meet with stakeholders to get more clarification on the unclear requirements.

## Scenario 2

**Situation:** The PM will manage a project that has unclear scope, in addition to requirements to
follow regulations and detailed documentation.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should opt for a hybrid project development approach that combines predictive and agile
  methods.
- Agile is used to address the uncertainty in scope, and predictive is used to follow regulations and
  documentation.

## Scenario 3

**Situation:** After starting the project, the PM has received many change requests from the client on
an ongoing basis.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should request the adoption of the Agile development approach to allow for these continuous
  changes.
- In case the project has to be predictive for certain reasons, the PM should always follow the Change
  Control Process.

## Scenario 4

**Situation:** The PM noticed that some team members complain about the increased workload and pressure
and the lack of time to rest.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should meet with the team to discuss the issue and develop a mechanism to relieve their
  stress.
- As a servant leader, the PM should focus on the needs of the project team.

## Scenario 5

**Situation:** The PM has been tasked to manage a new innovative product.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should identify the key product features that the customer needs in the first stage.
- The PM should adopt an iterative approach.
- The PM should opt for a Minimum Viable Product (MVP).

## Scenario 6

**Situation:** The PM joined a new project and was unsure about the role of each team member. One
member of the project team complained about unclear roles and responsibilities, and about receiving
assignments from multiple stakeholders.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should review the RACI matrix and verify its accuracy.
- The PM should review the Resource Management Plan, as it could include the RACI matrix and other
  additional information.
- In Agile projects, the PM should review the team charter, as it could include the list of team
  members and their roles.

## Scenario 7

**Situation:** A project team member suddenly becomes unhappy and uncooperative.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should use emotional intelligence skills to understand the feelings of the team member.
- The PM should talk to the team member, listen to their concerns, and try to resolve the issue.

## Scenario 8

**Situation:** The PM is not sure how to give a high-level estimate for the project cost or duration.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should look for a similar project that has been done in the past and use its data for
  estimation (analogous estimating).
- The PM could consult an expert to get their evaluations.

## Scenario 9

**Situation:** The PM is asked to prepare an analysis of the project's strengths and weaknesses before
starting its implementation.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should use SWOT analysis to identify internal strengths and weaknesses as well as external
  opportunities and threats.

## Scenario 10

**Situation:** Many issues occurred during project implementation and were not documented.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should create an issue log documenting all impediments and issues faced by the team for
  follow-up.

## Scenario 11

**Situation:** An issue occurred during project execution.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should hold a meeting with the project team and relevant stakeholders to study how to turn
  around the issue.
- The PM should ensure that the issue is recorded in the issue log.
- The PM should issue a change request if the issue leads to changes in the project baselines.
- In Agile, the issue should be recorded in the product backlog.

## Scenario 12

**Situation:** The project is behind schedule, or the sponsor requested completing the project before
its expected end date.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should apply one of the two schedule compression techniques: fast-tracking or crashing.

## Scenario 13

**Situation:** The PM is in the process of planning a detailed schedule of the project activities.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should use the Critical Path Method to determine the project schedule and identify the
  critical activities.

## Scenario 14

**Situation:** A Scrum framework was chosen to implement the project.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should support the team to deliver value in each sprint.

## Scenario 15

**Situation:** The PM finds it difficult to follow the day-to-day progress of the project.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should hold brief daily stand-up meetings with the team to discuss progress and obstacles.

## Scenario 16

**Situation:** The Agile team faces many obstacles and issues that hinder their work progress.

**Course of action:**
- As a servant leader, the PM should facilitate the team's work and work on solving these issues and
  removing impediments.

## Scenario 17

**Situation:** The PM noticed the underperformance of one of the Scrum team members.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should identify the training and development needs of this team member to improve their
  performance.

## Scenario 18

**Situation:** During project execution, the PM received a new requirement from a stakeholder.

**Course of action:**
- In Predictive, the PM should issue a change request and track its progress in the change log.
- In Agile, the PM should document this new requirement in the Product Backlog for later review.

## Scenario 19

**Situation:** The team has just finished executing the final sprint of the project.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should hold a retrospective meeting to review lessons learned, for future reference.

## Scenario 20

**Situation:** The PM wants to know how many user stories have been completed within the sprint, and
wants to check actual versus planned progress during sprint execution.

**Course of action:**
- The PM should use a burndown chart to find out how many user stories have been completed within the
  sprint.
- Measure and track sprint progress against the plan.
