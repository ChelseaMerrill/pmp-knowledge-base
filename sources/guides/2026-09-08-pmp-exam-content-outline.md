# PMP Examination Content Outline — July 2026

*Published by the Project Management Institute (PMI). This is the official document defining what the July 2026 PMP exam covers and how it's structured.*

---

## Introduction

PMI's PMP certification is accredited against ISO/ANSI 17024 and ISO 9001, and its exam-development process follows the *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*. Each exam cycle is built from a **Job Task Analysis (JTA)** — a criterion-referenced study of what practicing project managers actually do — so that exam questions map to real, frequently-performed work rather than abstract theory.

For the July 2026 update, PMI specifically evaluated the impact of emerging trends — **artificial intelligence (AI)** and **sustainability** — on professional practice, and folded relevant tasks into the JTA. All exam questions are written and reviewed by PMP-certified subject matter experts (SMEs) and pretested before being scored.

**This Exam Content Outline (ECO) is not the same thing as the PMBOK® Guide.** The ECO defines the critical *job tasks* a practicing PM performs (validated by the JTA); the PMBOK® Guide is a knowledge reference — principles, technical areas, and processes. The exam tests whether a candidate can apply judgment to realistic, scenario-based situations, not whether they've memorized the Guide.

## Exam Content Outline — Domain Weighting

| Domain | Percentage of Items on the Exam |
|---|---|
| I. People | 33% |
| II. Process | 41% |
| III. Business Environment | 26% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

**Important:** Predictive, adaptive/agile, and hybrid approaches are woven through *all three* domains — none of them is isolated to a single domain or task. Roughly **40% of items reflect predictive approaches**, and the remaining **60% split between adaptive/agile and hybrid approaches**. Exact item counts per approach can vary by exam form.

## Domains, Tasks, and Enablers

The ECO structures each domain into:
- **Domain** — a high-level knowledge area essential to the practice of project management.
- **Tasks** — the underlying responsibilities of the PM within that domain.
- **Enablers** — illustrative (not exhaustive) examples of the work a task involves.

Every exam includes questions covering *all* tasks in *every* domain, at the domain-level percentages above.

> **Example of the task structure (from the ECO):**
> **Domain:** People
> **Task statement:** Help ensure knowledge transfer
> **Enablers:** Identify knowledge critical to the project · Gather knowledge · Foster an environment for knowledge transfer

The ECO also aligns with PMI's *Maximizing Project Success* thought-leadership research, which reframed "project success" beyond the traditional schedule/budget/scope metrics to include **stakeholder value** and **outcome accomplishment** — success now means the project delivered value worth its effort and expense.

### Domain I — People (33%)

| Task | Enablers |
|---|---|
| **1. Develop a common vision** | Help ensure a shared vision with key stakeholders · Promote the shared vision · Keep the vision current · Break down situations to identify the root cause of a misunderstanding of the vision |
| **2. Manage conflicts** | Identify conflict sources · Analyze the context for the conflict · Implement an agreed-on resolution strategy · Communicate conflict management principles with the team and external stakeholders · Establish an environment that fosters adherence to common ground rules · Manage and rectify ground rule violations |
| **3. Lead the project team** | Establish expectations at the team level · Empower the team · Solve problems · Represent the voice of the team · Support the team's varied experiences, skills, and perspectives · Determine an appropriate leadership style · Establish clear roles and responsibilities within the team |
| **4. Engage stakeholders** | Identify stakeholders · Analyze stakeholders · Analyze and tailor communication to stakeholder needs · Execute the stakeholder engagement plan · Optimize alignment among stakeholder needs, expectations, and project objectives · Build trust and influence stakeholders to accomplish project objectives |
| **5. Align stakeholder expectations** | Categorize stakeholders · Identify stakeholder expectations · Facilitate discussions to align expectations · Organize and act on mentoring opportunities |
| **6. Manage stakeholder expectations** | Identify internal and external customer expectations · Align and maintain outcomes to internal and external customer expectations · Monitor internal and external customer satisfaction/expectations and respond as needed |
| **7. Help ensure knowledge transfer** | Identify knowledge critical to the project · Gather knowledge · Foster an environment for knowledge transfer |
| **8. Plan and manage communication** | Define a communication strategy · Promote transparency and collaboration · Establish a feedback loop · Understand reporting requirements · Create reports aligned with sponsors and stakeholder expectations · Support reporting and governance processes |

### Domain II — Process (41%)

| Task | Enablers |
|---|---|
| **1. Develop an integrated project management plan and plan delivery** | Assess project needs, complexity, and magnitude · Recommend a project management development approach (predictive, adaptive/agile, or hybrid) · Determine critical information requirements (e.g. sustainability) · Recommend a project execution strategy · Create an integrated project management plan · Estimate work effort and resource requirements · Assess consolidated project plans for dependencies, gaps, and continued business value · Maintain the integrated project management plan · Collect and analyze data to make informed project decisions |

| **3. | **4. Plan and manage resources** | Define and plan resources based on requirements · Manage and optimize resource needs and availability |
| **5. Plan and manage procurement** | Plan procurement · Execute a procurement management plan · Select preferred contract types · Evaluate vendor performance · Verify objectives of the procurement agreement are met · Participate in agreement negotiations · Determine a negotiation strategy · Manage suppliers and contracts · Plan and manage the procurement strategy · Develop a delivery solution |
| **6. | **7. | Task | Enablers |
|---|---|

| **2. | **5. ## PMP Certification Eligibility Requirements

You must meet **one** of these four educational + experience combinations, with all project management experience accrued within the **last 10 years**:

| Educational Background | Required Professional PM Experience |
|---|---|
| Upper-secondary/secondary school completion (e.g. High School Diploma, GED-equivalent) — EQF Level 4 / ISCED 3–4 | 60 months / 5 years non-overlapping, leading projects |
| Associate's-level, higher-certificate, or advanced technical/vocational program — EQF Level 5 / ISCED 5 | 48 months / 4 years non-overlapping, leading projects |
| Bachelor's degree (or higher), or an advanced qualification formally mapped to EQF Level 6 / ISCED 6* | 36 months / 3 years non-overlapping, leading projects |
| Bachelor's or postgraduate degree from a PMI GAC-accredited program | 24 months / 2 years non-overlapping, leading projects |

\* Non-degree qualifications only count at this tier if a competent authority explicitly maps them to EQF 6 / bachelor-equivalent — titles like "advanced," "professional," or "graduate" alone aren't sufficient. If audited, undocumented framework levels require a national qualifications register extract, an official statement from the awarding body/ministry, or a formal evaluation by a recognized national body.

**What counts as a project:** per the PMBOK® Guide, a temporary initiative in a unique context undertaken to create value — with a beginning and an end. Stand-alone, or part of a portfolio/program. Experience must be in *leading and directing* projects, in a professional setting — school projects, academic research, personal-event planning, and home improvement don't qualify, and routine/operational/administrative tasks should be excluded from entries.

**How months are counted:** by calendar tenure, not by number of projects — overlapping time leading multiple projects isn't double-counted. (PMI's example: Project I runs Jan 1–Mar 31, Project II runs Feb 1–May 31 → together they count as 5 months, Jan 1–May 31, not 3+4=7.)

### Commercial Training Requirement — 35 Hours

In addition to experience, candidates need **at least 35 hours of commercial training** in project management, aligned to this ECO, completed via any of:
- PMI Authorized Training Partner (ATP)
- China Registered Education Provider (REP)
- PMI Global Accreditation Center (GAC) programs
- Project management course(s) from an accredited higher-education institution
- Employer/company-sponsored programs
- Training companies or consultants
- Self-paced on-demand courses that include an end-of-course assessment

One classroom hour = one training hour (e.g. a college course meeting 3 hrs/week × 15 weeks = 45 hours; only the PM-relevant portion of a mixed course counts). Training must cover ECO-aligned topics — scope, schedule, communications, risk, procurement, financials, resources, stakeholders, integration management, governance, etc. **Books and practice exams alone do NOT count.** All training is subject to audit, and PMI may disqualify training it judges to be poor quality.

**Credit for prior learning:** an active CAPM® certification waives the 35-hour documentation requirement entirely (the training requirement is credited). Coursework from a GAC-accredited degree program can also count toward the 35 hours.

If you don't meet PMP eligibility, PMI suggests the **CAPM® certification** as an alternative path to build toward it.

## PMP Certification Exam Information

| | |
|---|---|
| **Scored questions** | 170 |
| **Pretest (unscored) questions** | 10 |
| **Total exam questions** | 180 |
| **Allotted exam time** | 240 minutes |

Pretest questions are unscored and randomly interspersed — they're used to validate future questions, not to judge you. There are **two 10-minute breaks**: one after the case-study section, one roughly midway through the independent-question portion. Once you start a break, you can't return to the prior section's questions. An optional tutorial (before) and survey (after) don't count against the 240 minutes.

### Question Types

- **Case or Scenario** *(new)* — a detailed business/project/decision scenario, possibly with charts, followed by a series of questions based on it. *(All modalities)*
- **Enhanced Matching** — drag items (possibly with images/diagrams) to specific chart locations. *(CBT only)*
- **Graphic-Based Questions** *(new)* — interpret a chart, graph, diagram, or image to answer. *(All modalities)*
- **Multiple-Choice Single Response** — one correct answer among several choices. *(All modalities)*
- **Multiple-Response** — more than one correct answer among several choices. *(All modalities)*
- **Point and Click** — click the correct hotspot location(s) on an image. *(CBT only)*
- **Matching** — drag items from one column to pair with a second column. *(CBT only)*
- **Pull-down List** — select the correct answer from a dropdown. *(CBT only)*

## Taking the PMP Certification Exam

- **In-person (CBT/PBT):** at a Pearson VUE test center (`pearsonvue.com/us/en/pmi.html`). Mainland China is PBT-only, delivered via ATA test services.
- **Online Proctored (OPT):** via OnVue (`pearsonvue.com/us/en/pmi/onvue.html`) — requires a system check and an extensive check-in process; budget extra time beforehand. You may be asked to re-present ID to the proctor after a break. Candidates in Japan and South Korea taking the exam in their local language via OPT are proctored by ATA test services instead.

## Retaking the PMP Certification Exam

- Up to **3 attempts within your 1-year eligibility period**.
- After 3 failed attempts, you must wait **1 year** from the last exam date before reapplying (you *can* pursue other PMI certifications during that year).
- If your 1-year eligibility window simply expires — even with fewer than 3 attempts used — you must reapply, but face **no waiting period** in that case.

## Continuing Certification Requirements (CCR)

Once certified, maintain your PMP by earning and submitting **60 professional development units (PDUs) every 3 years** and paying the renewal fee. Details: the CCR Handbook at `pmi.org/certifications/certification-resources/maintain`.
