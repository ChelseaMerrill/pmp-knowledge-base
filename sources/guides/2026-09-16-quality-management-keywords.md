## A-B-C

- **Accuracy:** a measure of how close the measurement is to reality.
- **Appraisal costs:** costs related to evaluating, measuring, auditing, and testing the deliverables of
  the project.
- **Assumption:** a factor in the planning process considered to be true, real, or certain, without
  proof or demonstration.
- **Assumption and constraint analysis:** an assessment that ensures assumptions and constraints are
  integrated into the project plans.
- **Assumption log:** a project document used to record all assumptions.
- **Bug:** a flaw or defect in software that causes it to behave not as expected.
- **Cause-and-effect diagram, Ishikawa diagram, why-why chart, fishbone diagram:** a visual tool used to
  identify the root causes of a specific problem.
- **Checklist:** used to list items, tasks, or requirements that need to be completed or verified to
  ensure consistency and correctness.
- **Check sheet, tally sheet:** a document used to record and count occurrences of specific events or
  issues.

## C-D

- **Control chart:** a graph that tracks process variation over time, aiming to detect potential issues.
- **Control Quality:** a process performed to measure the completeness, compliance, and fitness for use
  of a product or service prior to final delivery.
- **Corrective action:** addressing the root cause of an encountered issue, aiming to prevent its
  recurrence.
- **Cost of Conformance, Cost of good quality:** costs incurred by carrying out activities to ensure
  deliverables conform to the quality requirements.
- **Cost of Non-Conformance, Cost of bad quality, Failure costs:** the cost incurred as a result of
  failures because quality expectations were not met.
- **De facto regulations, Market-driven, Industry or professional regulations:** regulations that are
  widely accepted but not officially sanctioned.
- **Defect:** a deficiency, error, gap, imperfection, or flaw in a product, process, or service.
- **De jure regulations:** regulations that are formally recognized and established by law or legal
  authority.

## D-E-F-G-H-I

- **Design of Experiments:** controlled tests aiming to understand the relationships between factors
  affecting outcomes.
- **Enterprise Environmental Factors (EEF):** conditions, not under the control of the project team,
  that can influence the project.
- **External Failure Costs:** costs associated with defects found after project delivery.
- **Flowchart:** a visual representation that illustrates the sequence of steps or processes in a
  system, often used to analyze, document, or optimize workflows.
- **Grade:** the level of quality, rank, or classification of a certain product.
- **Histogram:** a graphical representation of data that displays the distribution of numerical values
  through bars.
- **Inconvenience:** a situation that causes discomfort or disrupts normal behavior.
- **Internal Failure Costs:** costs associated with finding and correcting defects before the customer
  receives the product.
- **ISO 9000 series:** a series of standards that define an effective Quality Assurance system for
  manufacturing and service industries.

## J-K-L-M-O-P

- **Just In Time (JIT):** an inventory strategy focused on minimizing waste by ordering materials only
  when they are needed for production.
- **Kaizen:** a Japanese philosophy of continuous, incremental improvement in processes, products, or
  services.
- **Lean management:** a philosophy focusing on maximizing customer value while minimizing waste.
- **Liabilities:** the legal and financial responsibilities a company faces due to defects or failures
  in its products or services.
- **Manage Quality:** the process of ensuring that products, services, and processes meet established
  standards and requirements.
- **Organizational Process Assets (OPA):** the plans, processes, policies, procedures, and knowledge
  base of the performing organization.
- **Pareto principle, 80/20 rule:** states that 80% of the problems come from 20% of the causes.
- **Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA), Deming Cycle:** an iterative management method used to facilitate the
  continuous improvement of processes or products.
- **Precision:** assessment of exactness.

## P-Q

- **Prevention costs:** expenses aimed at preventing defects and errors before they occur.
- **Preventive action:** identifying and eliminating potential sources of problems before they occur.
- **Quality:** the extent to which a set of inherent characteristics fulfills customer requirements.
- **Quality Assurance:** the systematic process of ensuring that products or services meet the required
  quality.
- **Quality Audit:** used to verify adherence to quality standards and processes — usually conducted by
  an external auditor.
- **Quality inspection:** used to verify whether the deliverables conform to the quality requirements.
- **Quality Management Plan:** describes how standards, measures, Quality Assurance (QA), and Quality
  Control (QC) will be implemented to achieve the required quality.
- **Quality Manager, Quality Engineer:** responsible for overseeing and ensuring adherence to quality
  standards, procedures, and policies.
- **Quality Report:** a summary document that outlines findings from quality control or audits.

## R-S

- **Refactoring:** a technique used in software development to improve code quality without altering
  its expected behavior.
- **Regression:** in software development, the reappearance of previously fixed bugs after changes are
  made.
- **Revamp:** the redesign or rework of existing features to improve appearance or functionality.
- **Rework, Rectification:** the correction of nonconforming tasks or deliverables in order to achieve
  the desired level of quality.
- **Root cause analysis:** an analytical method used to determine the basic underlying reason that
  causes an issue.
- **Run chart:** a graph that displays data points chronologically, illustrating trends or patterns over
  time.
- **Scatter diagram:** used to display the relationship or correlation between two variables.
- **Scrap:** defective product or material that cannot be repaired, used, or sold.
- **Six Sigma:** a data-driven philosophy of improvement intended to reduce the probability that an
  error or defect will occur.

## S-T-U-W

- **Standard:** a set of established criteria or guidelines used voluntarily to ensure consistency and
  quality.
- **Technical debt:** in software development, the cost and effort needed to maintain code due to the
  accumulation of quick-fix solutions and shortcuts.
- **Test:** a procedure aiming to compare actual outcomes to expected results.
- **Total Quality Management (TQM):** a management philosophy focused on continuous improvement through
  the involvement of all employees in quality goals.
- **Training:** educating and equipping team members with the necessary knowledge, skills, and
  competencies to perform their work effectively and to prevent errors.
- **Unit testing:** a software testing technique where individual units or components of a software
  application are tested in isolation.
- **Warranty:** a legal contract that defines how a product is expected to perform and the
  responsibility of the product vendor to address problems.
- **Waste:** activities that consume resources or time without adding value.
