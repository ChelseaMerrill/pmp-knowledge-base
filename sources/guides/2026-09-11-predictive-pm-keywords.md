# Predictive Project Management Keywords Review

*Yassine Tounsi — PMP 35 Hours Master Course*

An alphabetical glossary of predictive (waterfall) project management terms.

### A

- **100% rule:** states that the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) must include 100% of the work defined
  in the project scope.
- **Acceleration:** The use of methods to complete work in a shorter time than previously planned or
  required.
- **Accepted deliverables:** deliverables that have been accepted by the client through a formal
  sign-off.
- **Activity:** A task or set of tasks that are carried out in order to create a deliverable.
- **Activity attributes:** the elements associated with each scheduled activity — activity codes,
  predecessor activities, successor activities, logical relationships, leads and lags, resource
  requirements, imposed dates, constraints, and assumptions.
- **Activity duration estimates:** quantitative assessments of the likely duration required to
  complete an activity.
- **Activity list:** a detailed list of all planned project activities.
- **Actual Cost (AC):** The amount of money that has been actually spent to date.
- **Actual duration:** a precise measure of how long a specific task has taken from start to
  completion, or to a current point in time if the task is still underway.

### B-C

- **Backward pass:** a technique used to move backward through a project network diagram to identify
  late start and late finish values.
- **Baseline:** The final and approved estimation of the scope, schedule, and cost.
- **Basis of estimates:** a document that describes how an estimate was developed and defines the
  information used in support of development.
- **Benefits Management Plan:** a document that outlines how the benefits of a project will be
  realized and managed.
- **Beta distribution, PERT:** a weighted average in which more weight is given to the most likely
  estimate in order to increase the accuracy of the estimate.
- **Bottom-up estimation:** Involves separately estimating the cost of each activity, its duration, and
  the resources it requires, in order to determine the overall cost and timeline of a project.
- **Budget at Completion (BAC):** refers to the planned cost of the entire project.
- **Buffer:** an allocated amount of extra time to account for constraints, uncertainties, or potential
  delays. Lags and slacks are time buffers.
- **Change:** a suggested modification to any plan, document, work product, deliverable, or artifact.

### C

- **Change Control, Change Control System, Change Management, Change Order Management, Integrated
  Change Control:** the methodical approach or process of reviewing, analyzing, implementing, and
  documenting change requests.
- **Change Control Board (CCB):** the group of individuals responsible for making the ultimate
  decision as to when and if any particular changes are to be made.
- **Change Control Plan, Change Management Plan:** a document that outlines how you will identify,
  evaluate, approve, communicate, and implement changes in the project scope, schedule, budget,
  quality, or resources.
- **Change log:** a document or record that tracks any modifications, updates, or revisions to a
  project.
- **Change request, Change order:** a formal proposal to modify any element of the project baseline.
- **Closing Process group:** Performed to formally wrap up the project, phase, or contract by ensuring
  the customer's approval and acceptance of the final deliverables.
- **Code of accounts:** a lettering or numbering system that applies a unique identifier to each
  component of the Work Breakdown Structure.
- **Conceptual estimate:** Used to narrow down uncertainties by creating a blueprint, prototype,
  mockup, design, outline, etc. depending on the practice field.
- **Configuration Management Plan:** a document that details how you will record, track, control, and
  audit configuration.

### C

- **Control:** the activities involved in monitoring, measuring, and adjusting project performance.
- **Control accounts:** activities or small groups of similar work with a defined scope, schedule, and
  budget.
- **Cost aggregation:** the process of estimating the total cost of a project by combining the
  individual cost estimates of its activities, work packages, or deliverables.
- **Cost baseline:** an estimate of the budget required to successfully complete a project, excluding
  any management reserves.
- **Cost Management Plan:** a document that describes how costs will be planned, structured, and
  controlled.
- **Cost Performance Index (CPI):** a measure of the conformance of the actual work completed to the
  actual cost incurred. A CPI higher than 1 indicates that a project is under budget. A CPI less than 1
  indicates that a project is over budget.
- **Cost Variance (CV):** the difference between the planned cost of work performed (Earned Value) and
  the actual cost incurred. A CV higher than 0 indicates that a project is under budget. A CV less than
  0 indicates that a project is over budget.
- **Crashing:** a schedule compression technique in which you bring in additional resources to
  complete project tasks earlier.
- **Critical Chain:** Represents the longest chain of activities, but unlike the critical path, the
  critical chain takes into consideration technical dependencies and resource availability.

### C-D-E

- **Critical path:** the sequence of activities that determines the project duration. Any delay in
  these activities will directly impact the project's completion date.
- **Critical Path Method (CPM):** a project management technique for determining the project schedule
  using the network diagram.
- **Deadline:** the latest date or time by which a task or project should be completed.
- **Decomposition:** breaking down project deliverables into smaller, more detailed, and manageable
  work packages.
- **Definitive estimate:** determined at the end of the planning phase, hence providing the most
  accurate cost estimation.
- **Dependencies, Logical relationships:** relationships between tasks that determine their order in a
  project.
- **Discretionary dependency, Soft, preferred, or preferential logic:** created based on the preference
  of the project team and the order of activities they find more suitable for the nature of work.
- **Dropped Baton:** refers to the impact of poor coordination when an activity is completed early and
  the resources for the new dependent activity are not ready, thus the time gained by completing the
  activity early is lost.
- **Early Finish (EF):** refers to the earliest possible date that a task or activity can be completed.

### E

- **Early Start (ES):** refers to the earliest possible date that a task can begin.
- **Earned Value (EV):** Measures the budgeted value of work realized to date. It's calculated to
  determine whether the project is on track, behind or ahead of schedule, and over or under budget.
- **Earned Value Management (EVM):** The process of performance measurement by monitoring project
  progress and comparing its actual performance to its planned performance.
- **Estimate at Completion (EAC):** The expected total cost of completing all work, expressed as the
  sum of the actual cost to date and the estimate to complete.
- **Estimate to Complete (ETC):** The expected cost to finish all the remaining project work.
- **Estimating methods:** Methods used to develop an approximation of work, time, or cost on a project.
- **Executing process group:** Performed to implement the work defined and detailed in the planning
  process group — carrying out all planned activities to produce the project deliverables according to
  requirements.
- **Expert judgment estimating:** making estimations based on the skill, expertise, or specialized
  knowledge of the project team, independent consultants, or subject-matter agencies.
- **External dependency:** the type of dependency between project activities and non-project
  activities that is outside of the direct influence or control of the project team, but must
  nonetheless be accounted for when planning or managing a project schedule.

### F-G

- **Fast Tracking:** a schedule compression technique that consists of concurrently completing tasks
  that would normally be completed sequentially.
- **Finish-to-Finish (FF) dependency:** when one task can only be completed after the other task has
  been completed.
- **Finish-to-Start (FS) dependency:** when the start of the successor activity depends on the
  completion of the predecessor activity.
- **Float, Total Float, Slack:** the amount of time that a task can be delayed without affecting the
  completion date of the project. Total Float can be calculated either by subtracting the Early Start
  date of the activity from its Late Start date, or its Early Finish date from its Late Finish date.
  Critical path activities have zero float.
- **Forecasting:** the process of making predictions, guesses, or assumptions of the possible outcomes
  of a project.
- **Forward Pass:** moving forward through a network diagram to calculate the early start and early
  finish values — the earliest day each project task can begin and wrap up.
- **Free Float:** the amount of time that an activity can be delayed without delaying the Early Start
  date of its successor activity.
- **Gantt chart, Bar Chart:** A bar chart created to depict the scheduled activities' duration and
  sequence; activities are listed on vertical bars, their start and finish dates are displayed across
  the top of the chart, and their durations are shown as horizontal bars.
- **Gold plating:** Performing work that was neither requested nor approved by the client, aiming to
  deliver added value.

### H-I-K-L

- **Historical information:** information that was created and documented in earlier projects and
  developments, including documents such as project manuals, requirement specifications, reports, etc.
- **Initiating process group:** the process group for launching the project — performed to identify the
  project's objectives, scope, purpose, and key stakeholders.
- **Internal dependency:** dependencies between two project activities that, generally, the project
  team has complete control over.
- **ITTOs:** stand for Inputs, Tools & Techniques, and Outputs — a method used to understand how a
  process can be performed.
- **Kill point:** a phase-end review that allows the organization to evaluate the project's performance
  and take immediate action to correct any problems or even cancel the project, hence the name kill
  point.
- **Lag time:** the amount of time whereby a successor activity is required to be delayed with respect
  to a predecessor activity.
- **Late Finish (LF):** The latest date that the activity can finish without causing a delay to the
  project completion date.
- **Late Start (LS):** The latest date that the activity can start without causing a delay to the
  project completion date.
- **Lead:** the amount of time in which a predecessor activity can run in parallel with a successor
  activity.

### M-N-P

- **Mandatory dependency, Hard logic:** a non-negotiable dependency — the next task can't start until
  the previous one has been completed.
- **Master schedule:** a detailed plan that includes all aspects of multiple projects or operations,
  providing a broad overview of various project schedules, such as milestone schedules.
- **Milestone schedule:** A type of schedule that presents milestones with planned dates.
- **Monitoring and Controlling process group:** where the project's performance is tracked, reviewed,
  and adjusted to ensure that it stays on track and meets the planned objectives and requirements.
- **Murder board:** a committee of experts, senior managers, and stakeholders that attends stage gate
  meetings to review and evaluate the phase performance, and accordingly, decide whether the project
  should continue or should be canceled.
- **Network diagram, Precedence Diagramming Method (PDM):** a graphical representation of the project
  schedule, depicting and mapping out all the tasks in a project.
- **Node:** a key element within a project network diagram that represents a specific activity that
  needs to be accomplished.
- **Parametric estimating:** uses statistical relationships and historical data to estimate the cost,
  duration, or resource requirements of a project or activity based on specific measurable parameters.
- **Parkinson's law:** States that work expands to fill the time available for its completion.

### P

- **Performance Measurement Baseline:** Integrated scope, schedule, and cost baselines used for
  comparison to manage, measure, and control project execution.
- **Performance review:** a technique used to gauge, compare, and analyze the performance of work in
  progress against the baseline of the project.
- **Phase review, Stage gate, Phase gate, Phase entrance, Phase exit:** a formal meeting to check that
  the desired outcomes or exit criteria for the phase have been achieved before proceeding to the next
  phase.
- **Planned Value (PV):** The authorized budget for accomplishing the planned work.
- **Planning package:** A Work Breakdown Structure component under control accounts, used to plan and
  track activities that are not yet fully defined.
- **Planning process group:** Involves developing the project management plans including its budget,
  schedule, assignments, and responsibilities.
- **Precedence relationship:** defines the logical sequence between tasks, indicating the order in
  which activities must be performed. There are four types of dependencies: finish-to-start,
  start-to-start, finish-to-finish, and start-to-finish.
- **Predictive approach, Plan-driven approach, Traditional approach, Waterfall:** A development
  approach in which the project scope, time, and cost are determined in the early phases of the life
  cycle.
- **Preliminary estimate, Rough estimate, Screening estimate, Approximate estimate:** carried out at
  the beginning of the project based on historical data of other projects to provide a high-level
  estimate of the cost and time required for completing the project.

### P-R

- **Process group:** A logical grouping of project management processes to achieve specific project
  objectives.
- **Project calendar:** A calendar that identifies working days and shifts that are available for
  scheduled activities.
- **Project Management Plan:** a document that describes how the project will be executed, monitored
  and controlled, and closed.
- **Project schedule:** a detailed plan that outlines the duration of project activities, milestones,
  and deliverables to ensure the project is completed on time.
- **Ranged estimating:** The process of estimating a minimum and maximum value for cost or schedule.
- **Requirements Management Plan:** describes how project requirements will be analyzed, documented,
  and managed.
- **Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM):** A grid that links product requirements from their origin
  to the deliverables that satisfy them.
- **Reserve Analysis:** A method used to evaluate the amount of risk on the project and the amount of
  schedule and budget reserve to determine whether the reserve is sufficient for the remaining risk.
- **Resource leveling:** Consists of altering the duration or the sequence of activities according to
  resources' availability to avoid any overallocation. This can lead to changes in schedule.

### R-S

- **Resource optimization technique:** Used to adjust resource allocation before the schedule is
  approved or baselined.
- **Resource smoothing:** Used to attain a consistent resource utilization over a certain period of
  time using the activities' free and total float, to avoid affecting the critical path or the project
  completion date.
- **Rolling Wave Planning:** An iterative planning method in which the work to be accomplished in the
  near term is planned in detail, while the work in the future is planned at a higher level.
- **Rough Order of Magnitude Estimate:** an estimated cost based on approximate cost models or expert
  analysis, usually based on top-level requirements or specifications.
- **Sandbagging:** refers to the practice of holding a complete work until the true due date arrives.
- **Schedule baseline:** The approved version of a project schedule that is used as the basis for
  comparison to actual progress.
- **Schedule compression:** A method used to shorten the schedule duration without reducing the
  project scope.
- **Schedule forecasts:** Predictions of the project's future timeline based on information and
  knowledge available at the present time.
- **Schedule Management Plan:** establishes the criteria and the activities for developing, monitoring,
  and controlling the project schedule.

### S

- **Schedule Performance Index (SPI):** A measure of schedule efficiency expressed as the ratio of
  earned value to planned value. An SPI higher than 1 indicates that a project is ahead of schedule. An
  SPI less than 1 indicates that a project is behind schedule.
- **Schedule Variance (SV):** A measure of schedule performance expressed as the difference between the
  earned value and the planned value. An SV higher than 0 indicates that a project is ahead of
  schedule. An SV less than 0 indicates that a project is behind schedule.
- **Scope:** all required work, deliverables, and boundaries, defining what is included and excluded
  from the project.
- **Scope baseline:** The approved version of a scope statement, Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), and
  its associated WBS dictionary that is used as the basis for comparison to actual progress. During
  project execution, the scope baseline can only be changed using formal change control procedures.
- **Scope control:** Consists of monitoring and tracking the project scope and managing any changes to
  its baseline throughout the project lifecycle.
- **Scope creep, Requirement creep, Feature creep:** The uncontrolled expansion of project scope
  without following the Change Control System.
- **Scope Management Plan:** describes how the scope will be defined, developed, monitored, controlled,
  and validated.
- **Scope Statement:** The description of the project scope, major deliverables, and exclusions.
- **S-curve:** The cumulative cost of a predictive project over its life cycle usually follows the
  form of an S-curve.

### S-T

- **Self-protection:** when project team members don't report early completion of activities out of
  fear that upper management will adjust future standards and request the same level of productivity
  the next time.
- **Single-point estimating:** Used to produce a single-value estimate of the duration, effort, or
  cost.
- **Start-to-Finish (SF):** A logical relationship in which a predecessor activity can only be
  completed once the successor task has started.
- **Start-to-Start (SS):** a logical relationship in which a successor activity cannot start until a
  predecessor activity has started.
- **Status review:** consists of tracking the progress of the project against the project plan and
  determining what you need to do if the project is veering off track.
- **Student syndrome:** when a team member delays starting a task until the last possible moment,
  often leading to rushed work and increased stress as deadlines approach.
- **To-Complete Performance Index (TCPI):** the ratio of the cost to finish the unfinished work
  divided by the remaining budget.
- **Top-down estimate, Analogous estimate:** uses the values and characteristics of previous similar
  projects as a reference to evaluate the effort and budget needed for the new project.
- **Trend analysis:** An analytical method that uses mathematical models to forecast future outcomes
  based on historical results.

### T-V-W

- **Triangle Distribution, 3-Points estimating, Multipoint estimating:** a technique that uses
  Optimistic, Pessimistic, and Most Likely estimates to calculate the duration or cost of a task or
  project.
- **Validate scope:** the process of formalizing acceptance of the completed project deliverables.
- **Variance:** A discrepancy between the actual and planned performance on a project, in terms of
  scope, quality, schedule, or cost.
- **Variance analysis:** A method for determining the cause and degree of difference between the
  baseline and actual performance.
- **Variance threshold:** acts as a benchmark for acceptable deviations from planned values, whether
  they relate to cost, time, or scope.
- **Verification:** proof of compliance with specification performance requirements.
- **Verified deliverables:** Completed project deliverables that have been checked and confirmed for
  correctness through the Control Quality process.
- **WBS Dictionary:** defines in detail each of the WBS components and their characteristics.
- **Wideband Delphi:** An estimating method in which subject matter experts go through multiple rounds
  of producing estimates individually, with a team discussion after each round, until a consensus is
  achieved.

### W

- **Work Breakdown Structure (WBS):** A tool for defining the hierarchical breakdown of work required
  to deliver the project.
- **Work package:** a unit of work within a Work Breakdown Structure at the lowest level of its branch,
  containing specific tasks and deliverables within a defined scope, budget, and timeline.
