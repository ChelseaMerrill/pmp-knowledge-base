## Question 1

During execution of a construction project, the supplier informs the PM that delivering the new order of
steel bars may have a 3-day delay. What should the PM do next?

A. Negotiate the delay with the vendor to get it reduced
B. Look for another vendor
C. Evaluate the impact of the delay
D. Update the project schedule

**Correct answer: C.** The PM should evaluate the impact of the delay before making any decision. A
delayed activity doesn't necessarily delay the whole project — if the activity isn't on the critical path
and has a lag of more than three days, the delay probably won't affect the project at all. So there's no
need yet to negotiate the delay or look for a new vendor.

## Question 2

A PM made certain all required tasks were accomplished. However, an external post-completion audit
imposed a significant penalty on the performing organization. What could the PM have done differently to
avoid liabilities following the audit?

A. Verify that all tasks in the scope statement were completed
B. Verify that all identified defects were fixed
C. Ensure all documents were updated before closing the project
D. Ensure all closing formalities were followed per the defined contractual procedures

**Correct answer: D.** In the closing phase, the project team should implement the associated processes
to formally complete or close a project (PMBOK 7th edition, page 171). Following the defined procedures
formally is what avoids future liabilities.

## Question 3

A PM is leading a business transformation project. Scope requires a 10-expert survey. During execution,
the sponsor asks to raise the number of experts to 20 for better insight — and asks to make the
adjustment *without* issuing a change request. What should the PM do?

A. Accommodate the change
B. Refuse to make the change
C. Negotiate the number to reach a consensus
D. Inform the sponsor they need to follow the change management process

**Correct answer: D.** Any change to the project management plan should go through the organization's
change control process by issuing a change request — regardless of who's asking.

## Question 4

A newly assigned interim PM gets a call from a project vendor claiming they've already completed 50% of
agreed-upon work and asking for payment. What should the PM refer to, to check whether/how much to pay?

A. Request for proposal
B. Contract
C. Response to bid
D. Procurement management plan

**Correct answer: B.** The contract is a mutually binding agreement obligating the seller to provide
services and the buyer to pay a predetermined amount on an agreed-upon schedule (PMBOK 7th edition, page
191). The response to bid may differ from the final agreement after negotiations; the procurement
management plan is a generic planning document about the *process*, not the payment terms.

## Question 5

A PM leading a huge construction project, after issuing an RFP, wants to ensure all potential providers
fully and clearly understand the bid requirements. How?

A. Conduct face-to-face meetings with each seller
B. Conduct a negotiation meeting with each seller
C. Conduct a virtual meeting with each seller
D. Conduct a bidder conference

**Correct answer: D.** A bidder conference is the best way to explain procurement needs and process to
all potential providers at once, ensuring a common understanding and an equal chance to win the contract.
Individual or virtual meetings are less practical/efficient and contradict that equal-footing goal;
negotiation meetings happen only with the already-selected provider.

## Question 6

A PM leading a 3D modeling project of a castle (to be built near a river) is asked by the sponsor to
terminate the project halfway through execution, since a planned dam nearby will make the location
unappealing. What should the PM do?

A. Terminate the project as per the sponsor's request
B. Proceed with claims administration
C. Try to directly negotiate with the sponsor to allow continuation
D. Insist on delivering the project since the dam has no impact on it

**Correct answer: A.** When the sponsor decides to terminate a project for any reason, the PM should
follow instructions and start the "close project" process. There's no dispute over contract terms here,
so claims administration doesn't apply.

## Question 7

An industrial equipment manufacturing project completes assembly/installation successfully, but the
client complains the maintenance manual wasn't provided. What should the PM do next?

A. Review the contract terms and conditions to ascertain agreed-upon obligations related to deliverables
B. Initiate a formal change request to add the missing manual as an essential deliverable
C. Evaluate the stakeholder engagement plan for gaps that caused the misunderstanding
D. Ask the team to immediately add the manual and log the issue

**Correct answer: A.** Reviewing the contract terms and conditions should be the PM's first step, to
determine whether the manual was actually included in the scope of work before deciding the right course
of action.

## Question 8

A PM overseeing a complex multi-million-dollar bridge-repair project develops an RFP (with source
selection criteria and a detailed SOW), evaluates vendor responses, and selects the most suitable seller.
What's next?

A. Seek upper management approval to finalize the contract award
B. Update the stakeholder register to include the seller and hold a kickoff meeting
C. Create the contract and proceed with execution
D. Update the procurement management plan by incorporating the approved RFP

**Correct answer: A.** Final approval of complex, high-value, high-risk procurements generally requires
organizational senior management approval prior to award (Process Groups: A Practice Guide (2022), page
238). Updating the stakeholder register or procurement management plan isn't the priority right after
seller selection — finalizing the agreement is.

## Question 9

After multiple negotiation sessions, all terms were agreed and the seller got an 18-month building
project contract for $715,500 total. What contract type is this?

A. Time and Material
B. Fixed Price
C. Cost Reimbursable
D. Cost Plus Incentive

**Correct answer: B.** Fixed-price contracts set one total price in return for the seller's
services/products, used when requirements are clear. T&M quotes an hourly rate plus materials rather than
one fixed total; cost-reimbursable pays the contractor's actual costs plus a fee; cost-plus-incentive is
a cost-reimbursable subtype that adds a performance incentive.

## Question 10

During a construction project, the designed swimming-pool model needs a raw material no longer available
locally. An alternative exists at the same price and probably better quality. What should the PM do next?

A. Update the resource management plan to replace the old material with the new one
B. Submit a change request to formally get approval for the new material
C. Order a sample of the new material and check compliance with the quality management plan
D. Use the new material right away since it's the same price and probably better quality

**Correct answer: B.** The PM should submit a change request to the CCB per the change management plan.
The new material shouldn't be used until the CCB approves — the CCB might investigate further, source the
old material elsewhere, or change the design. If the new material is approved, the resource management
plan should then be updated.

## Question 11

After facing endless issues with a project supplier, the PM decides to end the professional
collaboration. Which document should the PM check for the termination clause and the alternative dispute
resolution mechanism?

A. Source selection criteria
B. Supplier bid
C. Agreement
D. Project charter

**Correct answer: C.** Including an ADR clause and a termination clause in a procurement agreement is
common practice. Dispute resolution lets contracting parties settle disputes while saving the time and
cost of going to court.

## Question 12

A PM has a major dispute with a supplier: the PM claims the vendor's raw materials don't meet agreed
quality requirements; the vendor complains about late payments. What should the PM do?

A. Not waste more time and simply replace the vendor
B. Involve a third party for arbitration or mediation
C. Collaborate directly with the vendor to resolve the issue
D. Conduct a procurement audit

**Correct answer: C.** Working directly with the vendor to reach agreement is the more direct
problem-solving approach and should be tried first. If direct negotiation fails, then involve a third
party (mediation/arbitration) — costlier, riskier, and more time-consuming. A procurement audit was most
likely already done before the issue occurred and doesn't address the payment-delay complaint anyway.

## Question 13

A PM leading a construction project (adding bungalows to a seaside hotel) needs to procure construction
materials. The PM is responsible for issuing all of the following documents, **except**:

A. Proposals
B. Source selection criteria
C. Request for quotes
D. Purchase order

**Correct answer: A.** Proposals (which respond to the request for quotes) are created by the *seller*,
not the buyer/PM. Source selection criteria are set by the buyer to choose a provider; an RFQ solicits
quotes/bids from suppliers; a purchase order is issued by the buyer to the seller specifying quantity,
type, and price.

## Question 14

A PM has closed all contracts on a construction project except one for security cameras — the only thing
left is a quick expert inspection (wireless connection, night vision, cloud storage, etc.). The cameras
are working fine and the project needs to close ASAP. What should the PM do?

A. Close the contract and the project with no inspection
B. Close the contract, but keep the project open
C. Close the project, but keep the contract open
D. Keep the contract and the project open

**Correct answer: D.** Keep both open until the seller performs the inspection. Closing a project
requires closing out all its contracts; closing a contract requires verifying the seller delivered as
predetermined — skipping the inspection to save time isn't a valid shortcut.

## Question 15

A PM keeps receiving negative feedback about a project supplier; conflicts between the project team and
the supplier's team are worsening and starting to hurt the project. What should the PM do next?

A. Send an email to the supplier's team manager explaining the alarming situation
B. Meet with the supplier's team manager to discuss the issue
C. Have a phone call with the supplier's team manager and urge commitment to the agreement
D. Proceed with Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)

**Correct answer: B.** A face-to-face meeting is always the best first step for resolving conflict — more
effective than email or phone. If direct negotiation fails, then proceed with ADR (mediation/arbitration).

## Question 16

A solar-powered car project is almost finished when the PM receives an *approved* change request to
replace a defective battery component. What should the PM do?

A. Record the problem in the issue log
B. Meet with the change control board to discuss the matter
C. Repair the defective component
D. Replace the defective component

**Correct answer: D.** Since the change request is already approved, the PM should ensure its appropriate
implementation exactly as specified — replacement, not repair, since that's what the approved CR calls
for.

## Question 17

Five months into an office-renovation project, the PM is called to explain multiple delays from the
contracted construction team (site prep, foundation, structural work) that now threaten the agreed
schedule. What should the PM do first?

A. Implement contractual penalties to incentivize adherence to timelines
B. Collaborate with the contractor to identify root causes and develop recovery strategies
C. Terminate the contract and engage alternative vendors/subcontractors
D. Seek senior management's support and guidance to resolve the delays

**Correct answer: B.** Collaborating with the contractor to find root causes and develop mitigation/
recovery strategies is proactive issue resolution and collaborative problem-solving — emphasizing
communication with stakeholders to address challenges effectively before escalating to penalties or
termination.

## Question 18

For a robotics-field project, the PM floated a tender for high-tech equipment and held a bidder
conference to clarify some points. Which two steps did the PM take during that conference?

A. Award the contract
B. Clarify any doubts
C. Explain the bid terms and conditions
D. Shortlist prospective sellers

**Correct answer: B, C.** A bidder conference (aka contractor/vendor conference) ensures sellers share a
common understanding of procurement requirements (PMBOK 7th edition, page 70) — the PM and stakeholders
discuss needs and answer questions. Shortlisting sellers or awarding the contract does *not* happen during
the conference itself.

## Question 19

During planning, to acquire physical resources, a PM of a high-tech project receives many proposals — from
previously-worked-with qualified suppliers and from others. To compare suppliers and make the best choice,
which tool/technique should the PM use?

A. Procurement performance review
B. Selection criteria
C. Procurement audit
D. Make or buy analysis

**Correct answer: B.** Selection criteria let the PM and team rate/score proposals — for a bid/quote, price
is the main criterion; for proposals, criteria can include experience, references, certifications, etc.
Since proposals have already been received, make-or-buy analysis should already be done at this point; an
audit or performance review happens later, to verify process compliance and conformance to requirements.

## Question 20

A PM on a predictive marketing project disagrees with the client: the client says the PM should cover
billboard rental costs since billboard advertising is in the contract's scope section; the PM argues it's
out of scope since renting costs aren't in the pricing section. After a long discussion with no agreement,
what's the PM's next step?

A. Try direct negotiation
B. Attempt Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR)
C. Set a claims resolution guideline
D. Resort to litigation

**Correct answer: B.** Since direct negotiation was already tried and failed, ADR (arbitration, mediation)
is the appropriate next step. A claims resolution guideline should have been set up front, during contract
elaboration; litigation is the last resort.
