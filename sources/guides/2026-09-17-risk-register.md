## 1. Purpose of a risk register

A risk register is a key tool in risk management that helps document and track risks throughout the
project lifecycle. It is typically structured as a sheet (such as an Excel spreadsheet, Google Sheets, or
a table in a project management tool) to organize risks systematically.

The primary benefits of maintaining a risk register include:

- A structured way to identify, assess, prioritize, and manage risks.
- Accountability, with assigned owners.
- A communication tool for stakeholders.

## 2. Typical components of a risk register

### Risk identification

- **Risk ID** — a unique identifier (e.g. 001) for tracking.
- **Risk description** — a clear explanation of the risk event or condition. It could also include the
  root cause and which parts of the project will be impacted.
- **Risk category** — classification (e.g. Technical, Financial, Operational, Legal, Environmental).

### Risk analysis & assessment

- **Likelihood (probability)** — the chance of the risk occurring (e.g. Low/Medium/High or a 1–5 scale).
- **Impact (severity)** — the potential effect if the risk occurs (e.g. Minor/Moderate/Severe or a 1–5
  scale).
- **Risk level (priority)** — combination of likelihood and impact (e.g. Low, Medium, High, or a
  calculated **Risk Score = Probability × Impact**).

### Risk response planning

- **Response strategy** — how the risk will be managed (e.g. Mitigate, Avoid, Transfer, Accept).
- **Action plan** — steps to address the risk (e.g. contingency plans, preventive measures) and the
  eventual budget required for the risk response.
- **Risk owner** — the person responsible for managing the risk.

### Monitoring & control

- **Trigger indicators** — warning signs that the risk may occur.
- **Status** — current state (e.g. Open, Mitigated, Closed).
- **Due date** — when actions need to be taken.
- **Residual risk** — remaining risk after mitigation.
- **Lessons learned** — insights from managing the risk.
