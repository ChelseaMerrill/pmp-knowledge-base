# Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

*Yassine Tounsi — PMP 35 Hours Master Course*

## 1. WBS

A Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) is a hierarchical decomposition of a project into smaller, manageable
components, breaking down the total scope into work packages that define deliverables, tasks, and
responsibilities. It helps in organizing, planning, and tracking project progress by ensuring all
necessary work is clearly structured and assigned.

Example: a three-level WBS for a Tiny House project, structured as four Level-1 phases, each broken
into Level-2 work packages:

- **1. Planning Phase** — 1.1 Define project objectives and scope · 1.2 Identify project stakeholders ·
  1.3 Develop project budget and schedule · 1.4 Conduct risk analysis and planning
- **2. Design Phase** — 2.1 Design architectural plans · 2.2 Develop the interior design · 2.3 Select
  construction materials
- **3. Construction Phase** — 3.1 Prepare the trailer foundation · 3.2 Construct the walls, roof, and
  flooring · 3.3 Install plumbing and electrical systems · 3.4 Implement interior design & furnishings
- **4. Closeout Phase** — 4.1 Conduct safety inspections · 4.2 Verify functionality of all systems and
  appliances · 4.3 Hand over the tiny house to the owner

The **WBS Dictionary** defines in detail each of the WBS components and their characteristics.

## 2. WBS Characteristics

The WBS's effectiveness depends on several key characteristics, which together ensure it's practical,
comprehensive, and useful for planning, executing, and controlling a project:

- **Definable:** clear description of the work and deliverables.
- **Manageable:** small enough to be effectively controlled.
- **Estimable:** allows accurate time, cost, and resource estimation.
- **Measurable:** clear criteria to track and verify completion.
- **Independent:** minimal overlap or dependencies with other tasks.

## 3. MECE Principle

The concepts of **Mutually Exclusive** and **Collectively Exhaustive** ensure that the WBS is
well-structured, comprehensive, and free from overlaps or gaps.

- **Mutually Exclusive (i.e., independent):** Each component or work package in the WBS should be
  distinct and not overlap with any other component. (Contrast: work packages that overlap in scope
  are *not* mutually exclusive.)
- **Collectively Exhaustive (i.e., 100% rule):** The WBS should include 100% of the work required to
  complete the project, with no gaps or missing components. (Contrast: a WBS whose branches don't add
  up to the full scope is *not* collectively exhaustive.)
