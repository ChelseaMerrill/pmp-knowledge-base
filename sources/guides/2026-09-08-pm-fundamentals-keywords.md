# Project Management Fundamentals — Keywords Review

*Yassine Tounsi — PMP 35 Hours Master Course*

An alphabetical glossary of PMP/project-management terms, spanning agile and predictive vocabulary,
organizational structures, financial formulas, and prioritization/decision-making techniques.

### A–B

- **4Ls:** a retrospective technique where team members identify what they loved, loathed, learned,
  and longed for in a project or sprint. Using this method, the team can reflect back on what
  worked, what didn't, and what can be improved upon following a sprint.
- **Affinity diagram:** classifies large numbers of ideas into groups or clusters for review and
  analysis.
- **Authority:** refers to the right to apply project resources, expend funds, make decisions, or
  give approvals within a project.
- **Balanced Matrix:** A type of organizational structure in which the project manager has more
  authority than in a weak matrix organization. However, the functional manager still acts as the
  primary managerial authority in the decision-making process.
- **Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR):** The relationship between a project's costs and benefits. A BCR
  greater than 1 means that benefits are greater than costs and the project is expected to deliver a
  positive net present value to the organization.
- **Brainstorming:** A data-gathering technique that involves a group discussion in which each
  participant quickly shares as many ideas as they can. Then, all elicited inputs are analyzed to
  define a requirement or find a solution.
- **Brainwriting:** Allows each participant time to reflect on the topic or questions individually
  and express their ideas or impressions through writing before the whole group participates in the
  main session.
- **Budget:** The approved estimate for the project or any scheduled activity.
- **Business Analysis:** aims at understanding the needs of the business stakeholders and at
  defining the characteristics of the project to meet those needs.

### B–C

- **Business Analyst:** analyzes and incorporates information provided by a large number of people
  who interact with the business, such as customers, staff, IT professionals, and executives to
  elicit the actual needs of stakeholders and facilitate communication between organizational units.
- **Business Case document:** A document demonstrating the project's financial and nonfinancial
  potential benefits.
- **Business Model Canvas:** A brief, high-level description of the project purpose and a
  declaration or a commitment to what the project team will accomplish by the end of the project.
- **Business value:** the benefit that the results of a specific project provide to its
  stakeholders. The benefit from projects may be tangible, intangible, or both.
- **Capital projects:** large-scale projects with very high expenditures. Examples include roads,
  railways, manufacturing plants, nuclear power plant construction, power transmission, and
  electrical distribution.
- **Close Project or Phase:** the process of finalizing all activities for the project, phase, or
  contract. This is the stage where deliverables are finalized and formally transferred, and all
  documentation is signed off, approved, and archived.
- **Collect requirements:** The process of determining, documenting, and managing stakeholder needs
  and requirements to meet project objectives.
- **Competing constraints:** When the triple constraints Cost, Scope, and Time extend to also
  include Quality, Resources, and Risk.
- **Conditions of Satisfaction (CoS):** an explicit description by a customer of all the actual
  requirements that must be satisfied by the performer in order for the customer to feel that he or
  she received exactly what was wanted.

### C–D

- **Confidentiality:** involves a set of rules or a promise usually executed through confidentiality
  agreements that limit access to or place restrictions on the distribution of certain types of
  information.
- **Configuration management:** is the process of organizing and controlling changes to a project's
  documents and deliverables.
- **Constraint:** A limiting factor that affects the execution of a project or process.
- **Context diagram:** A visual demonstration and depiction of how different elements interact. For
  example, how third-party applications, APIs, and users will interact with your product.
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** A financial analysis method used to determine the benefits provided by
  a project against its costs.
- **Criteria:** Standards, rules, or tests on which a judgment or decision can be based or by which a
  product, service, result, or process can be evaluated.
- **Customer obsession:** aiming to create a better customer experience from the customer's
  perspective.
- **Cycle time:** how long it takes for the team to complete a task.
- **Data-driven decision-making:** using facts, metrics, and data to guide strategic business
  decisions that align with the project goals and objectives.

### D–F

- **Data representation techniques:** used to graphically visualize and organize project data and
  information to convey it to stakeholders.
- **Decision-making:** the process of evaluating different options, assessing the risks and benefits
  of each option, and selecting the best solution or course of action for the project.
- **Definition of Done (DoD):** A checklist of all the criteria required to be met so that a
  deliverable can be considered ready for customer use.
- **Deliverable:** is a tangible or intangible output that is produced as a result of a project.
- **Delphi technique:** used to make complex decisions based on a consensus reached from expert
  opinions. This entails the use of several rounds of anonymous surveys, where each round is
  designed based on the previous round's answers.
- **Design thinking:** an iterative process that teams use to understand users, challenge
  assumptions, redefine problems, and create innovative solutions to prototype and test.
- **Fit for Purpose:** A product, service, solution, or process that satisfies its initial intended
  purpose, its users' needs, and its clients' expectations.
- **Fit for Use:** A product that is usable and capable of meeting business, technical, or
  operational goals in its current form or state. A product can be subject to further improvements
  even when it's found to be Fit for Use.
- **Focus Groups:** A facilitated interactive face-to-face conversation that brings together all
  concerned parties and subject matter experts to share and discuss their input regarding a
  particular subject.

### F–H–I–K

- **Framework:** a set of practices, standards, processes, and techniques used to carry out a
  project from initiation to closure.
- **Functional manager, Head of office, division, unit, or department:** the person who has
  management authority within a business unit or department with direct supervision over one or
  more resources on the project team, or direct responsibility for the functions affected by or that
  affect the project deliverables.
- **Functional Organization:** an organization that is structured as departments where people with
  similar skills are grouped; e.g., sales, marketing, finance, IT, etc. All authority including
  budget and resource allocation, decision-making, etc. belongs to the functional manager in this
  type of organization.
- **Hierarchy chart:** begins with high-level information that gets progressively decomposed into
  lower levels of detail. Examples include Organizational breakdown structure, Product breakdown
  structure, Work breakdown structure, etc.
- **Hybrid approach:** Combines and incorporates different predictive and adaptive elements and
  components into one development approach. This can be an effective transition method to introduce
  the organization or the team to a new approach.
- **Innovation:** a process by which a domain, product, or service is renewed and brought up to date
  by applying new processes, introducing new techniques, or establishing successful ideas to create
  new value.
- **Inputs:** things that we use in the project to implement it. For example, things like time of
  internal and/or external employees, finances in the form of money, hardware and/or software,
  office space, and so on are all inputs.
- **Internal Rate of Return (IRR):** The discount rate that would cause that investment to have a
  Net Present Value of zero. When comparing different potential projects, the one with the highest
  IRR is often the best choice.
- **Kano model:** an approach to prioritizing product functionalities based on their implementation
  cost versus their potential to satisfy customers.

### K–L–M

- **Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):** quantifiable measures used to evaluate the success of a
  project.
- **Last responsible moment:** A strategy of not making a premature decision but instead putting off
  decision-making until the latest possible time in order to be able to consider more options,
  scenarios, and aspects.
- **Leadership:** the ability to lead a team and inspire them to do their jobs well. It encompasses a
  wide range of skills, abilities, and actions.
- **Lead time:** represents the duration of a task from its creation to completion.
- **Management:** is the process of planning, organizing, leading, and controlling resources,
  including people, finances, and materials, to achieve organizational goals.
- **Matrix organization:** a work structure where team members report to multiple leaders: their
  project manager as well as their department head.
- **Metric:** a measurement that reveals how your project is performing. The metric indicates
  progress toward the project goal.
- **Milestone:** A measurement of the project's progress toward the ultimate goal and an indicator
  of whether or not the project is on track. It represents all the significant events or points in
  the project lifecycle.
- **Mind Map:** Used after brainstorming and nominal group sessions to organize and visually display
  the relationship between the collected ideas.

### M–N–O

- **Modeling:** The creation of a prototype, storyboard, diagram, mock-up, etc. as a simplified
  representation of a particular system, deliverable, or solution.
- **MoSCoW:** A technique for prioritizing requirements into Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, and
  Won't Have.
- **Multicriteria Decision Analysis:** a systematic and structured approach used to evaluate and
  compare options based on multiple criteria or objectives. The purpose is to provide a framework
  for decision-makers to assess various alternatives objectively and transparently.
- **Net Present Value (NPV):** Determines whether a project's anticipated financial gains will
  outbalance its present-day investment. A positive NPV indicates that a project will be profitable,
  whereas a negative NPV indicates an anticipated financial loss.
- **Nominal Group:** A technique that enhances brainstorming with a voting process used to rank the
  most useful ideas for further brainstorming or for prioritization.
- **Objective:** Something that the project team works toward, a strategic position to be attained,
  a purpose to be achieved, a result to be obtained, a product to be produced, or a service to be
  performed.
- **Operation:** the ongoing execution of activities that follow an organization's procedures to
  produce the same result or a repetitive service.
- **Outcome:** An end result or consequence of a process or project. Outcomes can include outputs
  and artifacts, but have a broader intent by focusing on the benefits and value that the project
  was undertaken to deliver.
- **Output:** project activities or work items that are performed in order to achieve outcomes.
  Outputs are easily measurable since they often represent tangible results. Completing project
  activities means creating outputs but does not necessarily mean that project outcomes were also
  achieved.

### P

- **Paired comparison analysis, Pairwise comparison:** a systematic approach for evaluating a small
  range of options by comparing them against each other.
- **Payback period:** How long it takes for a project's revenues to break even with its total
  investment. After recovering its investment, meaning after the payback period is over, a project
  starts generating profit.
- **Phase:** Comprises a number of logically related project activities, aiming to complete one or
  more deliverables and reach a certain milestone.
- **Portfolio:** is a collection of projects, programs, and other work that are managed and
  coordinated as a group to achieve strategic objectives.
- **Portfolio Manager:** manages projects and programs within a portfolio to achieve strategic
  objectives.
- **Procedure:** a set of instructions for completing a single task or activity within a process.
- **Process:** a series of tasks and activities that produce an outcome.
- **Product:** a solution created to meet the needs of a user, providing value and solving specific
  problems or fulfilling particular desires.
- **Product Analysis:** the process of understanding user behavior in regard to a product, using
  real customer feedback and behavioral data to inform future product updates or improvements.
- **Product Life Cycle:** The product life duration and evolution phases, from being a concept or an
  idea to the point of its retirement or discontinuation. A product goes through five stages:
  development, introduction, growth, maturity, and decline.
- **Product Management:** Developing and maintaining a product throughout its life cycle.
- **Profitability:** the ability of a project to yield a financial profit or gain for an
  organization.
- **Program:** Related projects and programs that are managed in a coordinated manner to obtain
  benefits not available from managing them individually.
- **Program Manager:** coordinates the efforts of multiple similar or related projects to achieve
  the program objectives.
- **Project:** A temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.
- **Project Charter:** A document issued by the project initiator or sponsor that formally
  authorizes the existence of a project and provides the project manager with the authority to apply
  organizational resources to project activities.
- **Project development approach:** The method according to which you create and achieve your
  project deliverable whether it's a product, service, or result. It consists of applying a set of
  procedures, techniques, practices, and rules throughout the project life cycle.
- **Project feasibility study:** Assesses a proposed project to determine if it is realistically and
  practically feasible to achieve, and if so, how likely it is to succeed.
- **Project goal, Project objective:** describes the desired state and outcomes that the project
  team expects after executing the project. It's a high-level statement aligning with the company's
  goals and providing an overall context for the expected effects of a project.
- **Projectized Organization:** is a structure where the majority of resources and authority are
  dedicated to project work, with project managers having full authority over their projects.
- **Project Life Cycle:** The process and different phases that a project goes through, from start
  to finish, to create and deliver a product. For example, the lifecycle of a mobile app development
  project can involve six phases: wireframing, prototyping, designing, developing, testing, and
  deploying.
- **Project management:** Applying a set of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to fulfill the
  project requirements.
- **Project Management Office (PMO):** set up to standardize project governance processes across the
  organization by defining project management frameworks, templates, forms, etc., and providing
  administrative support, centralized communication, training, project staffing, etc.
- **Project Manager, Project Lead:** The person assigned by the performing organization to lead the
  team that is responsible for achieving the project objectives.
- **Project Scope:** The work performed to deliver a product, service, or result with the specified
  features and functions.
- **Project strategy:** a plan that outlines the approach, actions, and measures taken to achieve
  specific goals within a project.
- **Project team:** a set of individuals that performs the required work for the project to
  successfully meet its goals.

### P–R–S

- **Project vision:** a short statement or paragraph that summarizes the goal of the project and how
  it fits within the organization's overall strategy.
- **Prototype:** A form of modeling that consists of a primary or partial version of the required
  product or solution. It helps obtain early feedback on requirements before committing to fully
  creating the final deliverable. Software or application mock-ups, 3D models, small-scale products,
  simulations, brochures, etc. are all examples of prototypes.
- **Requirement:** A condition or capability that is necessary to be present in a product, service,
  or result to satisfy a business need.
- **Resource:** a necessary asset whose main role is to help carry out a certain task or project. A
  resource can be a person, a team, a tool, finances, and time.
- **Responsibility:** the state or fact of being responsible, answerable, or accountable for
  something within one's power, control, or management.
- **Return On Investment (ROI), Return on the invested capital:** is calculated to figure out how
  much financial return a project will generate compared to its cost. A negative ROI means you're
  losing money on an investment.
- **Scope stability:** Agile projects are characterized by low scope stability, meaning that
  requirements are expected to undergo many changes throughout the project life cycle. However,
  predictive projects have a high scope stability since the scope is thoroughly defined in the
  planning phase.
- **Simulation:** an analytical mathematical tool that explores the impact of variances on
  deliverables or outcomes during a given project.
- **SMART Objective:** Stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound. These
  criteria provide a clear and structured framework for effectively setting and achieving goals.

### S–T

- **Steering committee, Project board:** an advisory body that provides direction and makes
  decisions outside the project team's authority.
- **Strong matrix organization:** gives more authority to the project manager. This allows the
  project manager to make decisions about the project, while the functional manager only assists as
  needed for specialized tasks.
- **Success criteria:** the measures used to determine whether a project has been successful. The
  criteria depend on the project and its goals but it often includes scope, budget, timeline,
  business goals, deliverables, and risk management.
- **SWOT Analysis:** consists of analyzing the Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats of
  an organization, project, or option.
- **Tailoring:** The deliberate adaptation of approach, governance, and processes to make them more
  suitable for the given environment and the work at hand.
- **Task:** a single unit of work or a single step in a multi-step project. A task is accomplished by
  a set deadline and must contribute towards work-related objectives. In agile projects, user
  stories are split into tasks whereas, in predictive projects, activities are split into tasks.
- **Threshold:** A predetermined value representing an acceptable limit that, when reached, requires
  taking certain actions. This value can be an upper or lower limit depending on the context or
  purpose it's used for.
- **Time-to-Market:** The needed time to create a product from the idea stage until its release. In
  other words, the time duration it takes the project team to launch the final product to the market
  for sale.
- **Triple bottom line:** Tool for evaluating the value of a business opportunity from the
  perspective of 3 basic lines: Profit, People, and Planet. It demonstrates the economic, social, and
  ecological balance a business idea could achieve.

### T–U–V–W

- **Triple Constraints, Iron Triangle:** Time, Cost, and Scope constraints that should be kept in
  check to ensure the deliverables' quality.
- **Use case:** A definition of the sequence of actions a system performs that yields an observable
  result of value to a particular actor.
- **Value creation:** any process that creates outputs that are more valuable than its inputs.
- **Value Engineering:** a systematic and organized approach to providing the necessary functions in
  a project at the lowest cost. Value engineering promotes the substitution of materials and methods
  with less expensive alternatives, without sacrificing functionality.
- **Value proposition:** The value of a product or service that an organization communicates to its
  customers.
- **Vanity metric:** shows certain results that reflect no real value or performance insights nor
  does it provide any useful information to help you evaluate important aspects and make educated
  decisions.
- **Voting:** Consists of picking the option that gets more votes from the involved parties in the
  decision-making process. A voting process can result in a decision made by unanimity, majority, or
  plurality.
- **Weak matrix organization:** leans toward the functional organizational structure. It gives more
  authority to the functional manager, while the project manager works to assist the team.
- **Wireframe:** a top-level blueprint that illustrates the structure of your website, app, or
  project. It doesn't include any designs or a great deal of detail. It simply maps the structure and
  the key elements.
