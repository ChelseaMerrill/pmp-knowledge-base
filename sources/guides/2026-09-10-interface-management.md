# Interface Management

## 1. Interface Management

Interface management (IM) is the management of communications, relationships, and deliverables among
two or more interface stakeholders.

Example diagram: on a construction project, four stakeholders — Owner, Carpenter, General Contractor,
Electrical Subcontractor — connected by five interfaces (Owner↔Carpenter, Owner↔General Contractor,
Owner↔Electrical Subcontractor, Carpenter↔General Contractor, General Contractor↔Electrical
Subcontractor) that must be effectively managed to ensure smooth coordination, communication, and
integration.

## 2. Advantages

Interface Management ensures seamless integration and collaboration among stakeholders, disciplines,
and systems. Key advantages:

- Increase alignment
- Improve visibility and oversight
- Facilitate communication
- Mitigate risks

## 3. Interface Types

Interfaces can be classified along two independent axes:

- **Internal Interface:** those identified within a single scope of work.
- **External Interface:** those identified between two or more scopes of work.
- **Soft Interface:** the exchange of information between delivery teams, or between teams and
  external parties.
- **Hard Interface:** physical connections between two or more components or systems.

Examples for each type:

- **Internal Interface:** the architectural team collaborates with the structural engineering team to
  ensure the building design aligns with load-bearing requirements.
- **External Interface:** the construction company coordinates with the local government to obtain
  necessary permits and adhere to zoning regulations.
- **Soft Interface:** the project manager negotiates with the labor union to maintain good working
  conditions and avoid potential strikes.
- **Hard Interface:** the structural engineering team ensures that the pre-fabricated steel beams from
  the supplier fit precisely with the foundation and load-bearing columns.
