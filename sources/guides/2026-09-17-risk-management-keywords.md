## A-C-D-E-F

- **Active acceptance:** when a risk is recognized and mitigated by a contingency plan with triggers that
  will activate that plan when needed.
- **Alternatives analysis:** breaking down a complex situation to generate and evaluate different
  solutions and approaches.
- **Ambiguity:** a state of being unclear, having difficulty in identifying the cause of events, or
  having multiple options from which to choose.
- **Complexity:** the state of having a large number of interconnected and interdependent parts or
  variables.
- **Contingency reserve:** time or money allocated in the schedule or cost baseline for *known* risks
  with active response strategies.
- **Crisis:** an adverse situation in which urgent decisions are required to prevent the situation from
  deteriorating further.
- **Decision tree analysis:** used to support the selection of the best course of action out of several
  alternatives.
- **Expected Monetary Value (EMV):** a quantitative risk analysis method that consists of calculating the
  average outcome of various risks, factoring in their probabilities and associated costs or benefits.
- **Fallback plan, Plan B:** used when a risk occurs and the primary response proves to be inadequate.

## I-K-M-O-Q

- **Inherent risk:** the level of risk that you start off with before even beginning your project.
- **Issue:** a problem or concern that arises during a project or process, requiring attention and
  resolution to prevent potential negative impacts.
- **Issue log:** used to record and monitor active issues. Issues are assigned to a responsible party for
  follow-up and resolution.
- **Known unknown, Identified risk:** a risk that you're aware of.
- **Manageability:** the ease with which the risk owner (or owning organization) can manage the
  occurrence or impact of a risk. Where management is easy, manageability is high.
- **Management reserve:** an amount of the project budget or schedule under management control that is
  reserved for unforeseen work within the project scope.
- **Monte Carlo analysis, Monte Carlo simulation:** a risk analysis technique that consists of running
  hundreds or thousands of simulations, each time using a different set of values from the cost and
  duration risk model inputs. The result is a total estimate for project cost or schedule presented as a
  range.
- **Opportunity, Positive risk:** a potential event or circumstance that can positively impact the
  project if it occurs.
- **Qualitative risk analysis, Risk assessment:** the process of prioritizing risks by assessing their
  probability of occurrence and impact.

## Q-R

- **Quantitative risk analysis, Risk analysis:** the process of numerically analyzing the effect of
  identified risks on the project to make informed decisions about their management.
- **Residual risk:** the amount of risk left after applying the risk response.
- **Risk:** an uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on the
  project.
- **Risk acceptance, Risk retention, Passive acceptance:** a risk response strategy whereby the project
  team decides to acknowledge the risk without taking any proactive action apart from periodic review to
  ensure that the risk does not change significantly.
- **Risk appetite:** the degree of risk an organization or individual is willing to accept, balancing
  potential benefits with potential losses.
- **Risk audit:** used to verify adherence to risk standards and processes. Usually conducted by an
  external auditor.
- **Risk-averse:** a person or organization that is not comfortable with risks or has a low tolerance for
  risks and tries to avoid them unless the reward is high enough to outweigh their aversion.
- **Risk avoidance:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team acts to eliminate the threat or
  protect the project from its impact.
- **Risk Breakdown Structure:** a hierarchical representation of potential sources of risks.

## R (continued)

- **Risk categorization:** the process of categorizing risks by sources, area of the project affected,
  common root causes, etc. It's used to determine the areas of the project most exposed to risk.
- **Risk enhancement:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team acts to increase the
  probability of occurrence or impact of an *opportunity*.
- **Risk escalation:** a risk response strategy whereby the team acknowledges that a risk is outside of
  its sphere of influence and shifts the ownership of the risk to a higher level of the organization.
- **Risk exploiting:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team acts to *ensure* that an
  opportunity occurs.
- **Risk exposure:** an aggregate measure of the potential impact of all risks in a project.
- **Risk identification:** the process of identifying all possible risk events on a project.
- **Risk management:** the process of identifying, classifying, analyzing, prioritizing, and mitigating
  risks on a project.
- **Risk Management Plan:** a document that describes how risk management activities will be structured
  and performed.
- **Risk Matrix, Risk Assessment Matrix, Probability and Impact Matrix:** a qualitative risk analysis
  tool used to visualize a project's overall risks according to their likelihood of occurrence and their
  impact on the project.

## R (continued)

- **Risk mitigation, Risk control, Risk reduction:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team
  acts to decrease the probability of occurrence or the impact of a *threat*.
- **Risk Mitigation Plan:** a document that specifies the deployment plan to mitigate risks identified in
  the risk register and outlined in the risk assessment matrix.
- **Risk monitoring:** the ongoing tracking and evaluation of identified risks and mitigation efforts.
- **Risk-neutral:** a person or organization that deals with risks objectively; they analyze risks and
  then make informed decisions.
- **Risk owner:** the individual or entity responsible for the management and control of a specific risk,
  ensuring that appropriate actions are taken if the risk occurs.
- **Risk parameters:** risk characteristics such as probability, impact, dormancy, controllability,
  urgency, detectability, connectivity, strategic impact, proximity, propinquity, manageability, etc.
- **Risk perception:** the way a stakeholder views a risk, based on a set of values or concerns.
- **Risk proximity:** when, or how soon, a risk event is likely to occur.
- **Risk rating, Risk ranking:** using a numeric classification, such as 1 to 5, or a scale (e.g. low,
  medium, or high) to determine which risk is the most serious and thus which to prioritize.

## R (continued)

- **Risk register:** a document or spreadsheet used to catalog project risks and then record the
  assessment and analysis results. Helps track risks throughout the entire project life cycle.
- **Risk report:** a document that summarizes the activities related to the assessment, analysis, and
  management of risks within a project.
- **Risk response:** the planned or actual action in response to a risk event.
- **Risk review:** the process of examining the effectiveness of the risk response strategies.
- **Risk review meeting, Risk reassessment:** a meeting to analyze the status of existing risks and
  identify new risks.
- **Risk-seeker, Risk-taker:** a person or organization that has a higher tolerance for risks and
  underestimates their severity, often seeing risks as opportunities.
- **Risk sharing:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team allocates ownership of an
  *opportunity* to a third party who is best able to capture the benefit of that opportunity.
- **Risk threshold:** the measure of acceptable variation around an objective that reflects the risk
  appetite of the organization.
- **Risk tolerance:** the willingness of a person or organization to accept or avoid a risk.

## R-S-T-U

- **Risk transfer, Risk deflection:** a risk response strategy whereby the project team transfers all or
  part of a risk to a third party, usually by some form of contract.
- **Secondary risk:** the risk that arises as a direct result of implementing a risk response to a
  specific risk.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** helps determine which risks have the most potential impact on project
  outcomes.
- **Threat, Negative risk:** a potential event or circumstance that can negatively impact the project if
  it occurs.
- **Tornado diagram:** a bar chart that visually displays the magnitude of each risk in descending order.
- **Trigger:** an indication that a risk is about to occur.
- **Uncertainty:** a lack of understanding and awareness of issues, events, paths to follow, or solutions
  to pursue.
- **Unknown unknown, Unidentified risk:** a risk that you're not aware of.
- **Urgency:** the period of time within which a response to the risk should be implemented in order to
  be effective. A short period indicates high urgency.

## V-W

- **Volatility:** the possibility for rapid and unpredictable change.
- **VUCA:** stands for Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, and Ambiguity.
- **What-if analysis:** a technique used to explore the potential outcomes of different courses of
  action.
- **Workaround, Turnaround:** an unplanned response to a negative event or issue.
