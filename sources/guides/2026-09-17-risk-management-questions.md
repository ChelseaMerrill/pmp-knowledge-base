## Question 1

In a retrospective meeting, a development team expressed their desire to get training in Python
programming. The project manager was checking some well-known training providers when they found a new
provider that offers the same training for really interesting prices. The project manager was hesitant
about getting the training from this new provider since he wasn't sure about the quality.

Link the risk response on the left to the correct strategy on the right.

*Risk responses (left column):*

A. Share the risk with senior management and let them decide
B. Enroll one developer to assess the training quality before enrolling the rest of the team
C. Look for another accredited training agency that has a good reputation
D. Get the training for all of the team since it's not expensive

*Strategies (right column, listed in the slide's order — not the answer order):* Accept · Avoid ·
Escalate · Mitigate

**Correct answers:**

- **A → Escalate.** Handing the decision to senior management shifts ownership of the risk to a higher
  level of the organization.
- **B → Mitigate.** Testing with one developer first reduces the probability/impact of the bad-quality
  threat.
- **C → Avoid.** Going with a known, accredited agency eliminates the threat entirely.
- **D → Accept.** Going ahead anyway acknowledges the risk without acting on it — justified here because
  the impact (the price) is low.

## Question 2

Even though the project management plan was perfectly elaborated, the project manager thinks that
unforeseen events could have a big impact on the whole project. What should the project manager do next?

A. Identify these events and update the project management plan
B. Ensure that a management reserve is allotted
C. Discuss these events with the sponsor in order to develop a fallback plan
D. Perform a qualitative risk assessment to define the priority of each of these events

**Correct answer: B.** Unforeseen events are "unknown unknowns", meaning they cannot be previewed or
identified beforehand. Therefore it is not possible to discuss or prioritize these events. The only action
to take against this type of risk is to set up a management reserve to respond to them when they occur.

## Question 3

During a planning session, a project management team determined that they need to purchase new IT
equipment in November. The project manager thought it would be a good idea to take advantage of Black
Friday's discounts since it takes place in the last week of November. What should the project team do to
not miss this opportunity?

A. Exploit the opportunity
B. Share the opportunity
C. Enhance the opportunity
D. Accept the opportunity

**Correct answer: A.** The project team should *exploit* this opportunity by taking specific actions and
preparing the required conditions to take advantage of it (PMBOK 7th edition, page 220). For example,
before Black Friday the team can prepare a list of sellers, contact them to verify whether they're
planning discounts, subscribe to their newsletters to be notified, etc. The team can exploit this
opportunity without having to share it with a third party. Exploiting means acting to *ensure* the
opportunity occurs; *enhancing* means acting to increase the probability of its occurrence or impact —
e.g. simply purchasing the equipment during the last week of November, which does not guarantee they'll
find discounts on what they want, so they could still miss it.

> Paired strategies, from the slide: Accept/Accept · Mitigate/Enhance · Transfer/Share · Avoid/Exploit ·
> Escalate/Escalate (threat / opportunity).

## Question 4

An agile team is working on developing an innovative digital product. The product owner added a user
story in the product backlog and noted that it should be implemented in the next release. During a
refinement session with the product owner, the development team stated that they are not sure about the
user story implementation since it requires particular inputs from the current release. What should the
development team do next?

A. Further study the different implementation scenarios of the user story
B. Prioritize the user story since it involves a high level of ambiguity and risk
C. Stop spending more time trying to figure out how to implement the user story until they collect more
   information
D. Give the user story a final estimation based on the current understanding and available information

**Correct answer: C.** The agile approach follows Lean principles, among them "deciding as late as
possible": when it's difficult to make a decision or plan an activity, delay it until you have enough
information to make an educated decision. This is the **last responsible moment** concept. Here,
determining how to implement the story requires inputs from the current release, so the team should wait.
Final estimates are usually assigned during sprint planning, or whenever the implementation is clearly
decided upon. The *product owner* is responsible for prioritizing work — and prioritization should be
based on value, not just ambiguity or risk.

## Question 5

A project manager is leading a maintenance project for an energy supply company. During project
execution, an engineer finds out that a spare part should be immediately changed to avoid an imminent
power outage. However, it will take a lot of time to get a new spare part following the organization's
procurement process. What should the project manager do in this case?

A. Buy the spare part without following the procurement process
B. Follow the procurement process, regardless of the risk results
C. Update the organization's procurement process in order to be able to get the spare part as soon as
   possible
D. Escalate the problem to the project sponsor

**Correct answer: D.** The appropriate strategy is **escalation**, since changing or exceptionally
skipping the organization's procurement process is outside the scope of the project or beyond the PM's
authority (PMBOK 7th edition, page 123). In this scenario avoiding one risk means accepting the other:
buying the part without adhering to the process might not be tolerated by the organization, while
following the process exposes the project to the critical risk of an electricity outage.

## Question 6

Mid-sprint, the development team's work was disrupted by a sudden issue concerning a dysfunctioning
software feature. During the following retrospective meeting, the project manager decides to use the Five
Whys method in order to investigate the problem. Why did the project manager choose this specific
technique?

A. To identify the five main factors that caused the issue
B. To identify the root cause of the issue
C. To identify the five members responsible for the issue
D. To identify the five steps to take to resolve the issue

**Correct answer: B.** As a problem-solving technique, the Five Whys is used to explore the *underlying
cause* of a defect or issue. By asking successive "Why?" questions, the team digs deep to figure out what
went wrong and thus how to properly address the problem and avoid similar issues in future.

## Question 7

During the planning of a call center project, a stakeholder mentions that since the used internet cable
(DSL) can be slow, it should be considered a high risk to the project. However, based on their previous
projects, the project manager thinks the cable internet would work just fine. Another stakeholder
intervenes to explain that internet cable speed and reliability vary depending on geographical location.
What should the project manager do first?

A. Analyze and prioritize the risk by using a probability and impact matrix
B. Set a contingency reserve to install fiber Internet cables if the threat occurs
C. Perform a qualitative risk analysis by developing a risk mitigation plan
D. Perform a quantitative risk analysis to determine the risk exposure

**Correct answer: A.** The PM should *first* perform qualitative risk analysis to assess risk occurrence,
probability, and impact. After that, they should further analyze the prioritized risks using quantitative
risk analysis — which is what lets them determine risk exposure, develop a risk mitigation plan, or
define a contingency reserve. Option C is incorrect because qualitative risk analysis is performed *by
using a probability and impact matrix*, not by developing a risk mitigation plan.

> Process order, from the slide: 1. Plan risk management → 2. Identify risks → 3. Perform qualitative
> risk analysis → 4. Perform quantitative risk analysis → 5. Plan risk responses → 6. Implement risk
> responses → 7. Monitor risks.

## Question 8

During the risk identification process, attrition of resources was identified as a major risk. As a
response, the project manager decided to opt for financial incentives to motivate their team members.
What type of risk response are they following?

A. Acceptance
B. Mitigation
C. Transference
D. Avoidance

**Correct answer: B.** The PM is *mitigating* the risk of resource attrition. This type of risk response
aims to diminish the probability and/or impact of a threat (PMBOK 7th edition, page 123).

## Question 9

Working in a risk-averse organization, the project manager suggested using the agile approach for a new
project that has a high level of uncertainty. How should risk management be conducted in this case?
(Select two)

A. The project manager will own the risk management
B. Both the project manager and the project team conduct risk analysis, determine risk responses, and
   update the Risk Register
C. The project team analyzes and addresses risks in all planning meetings, with a focus on qualitative
   rather than quantitative analysis
D. Risks are monitored through the use of information radiators, stand-up meetings, iteration reviews,
   and retrospectives

**Correct answer: C & D.** When managing an agile project, risk identification occurs in all types of
planning meetings — daily stand-ups, release meetings, iteration reviews, and retrospectives. The team
analyzes and addresses risks during planning meetings through *qualitative* rather than quantitative
analysis. In agile projects the **project team owns risk management**, while the project manager is only
responsible for *facilitating* the process.

## Question 10

During the planning phase, the project manager speculated that there will be no changes in the
composition of the project steering committee. But since any change could be very critical to the
project, the project manager assigned a team member to monitor for triggers. Where should the name of the
person responsible for monitoring the steering committee be recorded?

A. Risk register
B. Issue log
C. Change log
D. Stakeholder register

**Correct answer: A.** A risk register contains a list of all identified risks, their description,
planned responses, probability, impact, risk owner, etc. (PMBOK 7th edition, page 185). Here, the change
in steering-committee composition is a *risk*, and the name of the person responsible for managing it
belongs in the risk register. Once a risk occurs it becomes an issue — this situation involves no issue
yet, so the issue log is wrong. There's no change request either, so the change log is wrong. The
stakeholder register holds information about stakeholders and their classification; unlike the
stakeholder *engagement plan*, it doesn't contain action items for when, how, and who should monitor and
engage stakeholders.

## Question 11

A project manager gets assigned to a software development project that requires a specialized programming
skillset currently in high demand in the job market. Additionally, due to the fast-paced evolution of the
programming language, finding and retaining skilled individuals has become increasingly difficult. What
should the project manager do?

A. Opt for alternatives analysis to explore the different options and allocate a sufficient cost reserve
   to mitigate risks associated with the high turnover rate
B. Develop the software using a more common programming language
C. Mitigate the risk by enrolling the entire team in a bootcamp to learn the specialized programming
   skillset
D. Raise a risk to project completion due to the threat posed by the required skillset scarcity

**Correct answer: A.** The PM is facing high **volatility** — an environment subject to rapid and
unpredictable change, which can occur when there are ongoing fluctuations in available skill sets or
materials. Volatility usually impacts cost and schedule, and **alternatives analysis plus use of cost or
schedule reserve** is the response that addresses it (PMBOK 7th edition, page 122).

## Question 12

A project manager is assigned to a new agile project in the finance sector. The project team has
identified several risks that could potentially impact project delivery. The product owner advises the
team to utilize a risk-adjusted backlog to manage these risks effectively. What should the project
manager do?

A. Ensure that at least one or two risk responses are added to each sprint
B. Dedicate a sprint to deal with all identified risks
C. Ensure that the risk register is always up-to-date
D. Dedicate the sprint retrospective meetings to review the status of risks

**Correct answer: A.** A **risk-adjusted backlog** is a backlog that includes work and actions to address
threats and opportunities (PMBOK 7th edition, page 185). By incorporating at least one risk response into
each sprint, the team actively addresses identified risks iteratively throughout the project lifecycle,
letting the project adapt and respond effectively to changing circumstances.

## Question 13

A project manager decides to plan and manage a new project using a waterfall approach. The project team
starts estimating the required resources when the PMO issues a mandate stating that the organization will
no longer use third parties or off-site workers and that all resources must be onsite. Upon reviewing the
project's activity list, the project manager realizes that a crucial part of project work requires
off-site resources. What should the project manager do first?

A. Discuss the issue with the project sponsor to navigate other options
B. Perform alternatives analysis to evaluate the impact of using onsite resources instead of off-site
   resources
C. Update the resource calendar to adapt it to the PMO's mandate
D. Update the resource management plan to adapt it to the PMO's mandate

**Correct answer: B.** The PM should first perform **alternatives analysis** to evaluate the impact of
using onsite instead of off-site resources. Alternatives analysis consists of identifying the variables
to be considered in evaluating options, and the relative importance or weight of each variable (PMBOK 7th
edition, page 122). This lets the PM find the best solution to perform the project activities within the
defined constraints.

## Question 14

A project manager is assigned to lead a megaproject that requires experts and resources from different
countries. Unable to predict worldwide epidemics, the project team is worried about facing another event
like CoronaVirus that might cause delays and cost overruns. What should the project manager do during the
risk planning phase? (Select two)

A. Add the risk to the risk register
B. Perform a qualitative risk analysis
C. Add the risk to the issue log
D. Monitor the development of the risk

**Correct answer: A & B.** The team should perform a qualitative risk analysis; if they find the risk
must be prioritized, a detailed risk response should then be developed. All identified risks should be
added to the risk register. During project *execution*, occurring issues are noted in the issue log, and
monitoring risk development happens in execution too — not in the planning phase.

## Question 15

One of the project team's key members leaves the project for personal reasons. The project manager did
not plan for such a scenario but managed to get a replacement with the help of a staffing agency.
However, the project manager has to pay the agency a higher salary. This will result in extra costs which
fall under the project's:

A. Contingency reserve
B. Cost
C. Management reserve
D. Budget

**Correct answer: C.** **Management reserve** is the dedicated budget or time reserve for handling
unidentified risks, or *unknown unknowns* (unknown = unidentified, unknowns = risks). This reserve is not
a calculated budget and does not take part in the cost baseline, so any time an unknown risk occurs the
PM needs permission to use it. A **contingency reserve**, by contrast, is used for identified risks with
predetermined response strategies — *known unknowns* (known = identified, unknowns = risks) (PMBOK 7th
edition, page 127).

> Decision aid, from the slide — when a risk happens: **Identified** → apply the risk response, use the
> contingency reserve. **Not identified** → workaround, use the management reserve.

## Question 16

A project manager used the Expected Monetary Value method to determine the contingency reserve for a new
industrial project. During the execution phase, the project manager updated the probability of occurrence
in the risk register as follows:

| | Risk impact | Expected occurrence | Expected Monetary Value | Updated occurrence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Risk 1 | $5,000 | 20% | $1,000 | 100% (Occurred) |
| Risk 2 | $2,000 | 50% | $1,000 | 50% |
| Risk 3 | $10,000 | 40% | $4,000 | 0% (Will never occur) |

According to the project manager's updates, how much is left in the contingency reserve?

A. $6,000
B. $5,000
C. $2,000
D. $1,000

**Correct answer: D.** The project's contingency reserve is **$6,000** — the sum of all risks' Expected
Monetary Values ($1,000 + $1,000 + $4,000). During execution, this reserve is used when identified risks
occur. Risk 1 has already taken place, so $5,000 has been spent (the risk *impact*, not its EMV).
Therefore only **$1,000** is left ($6,000 − $5,000).

## Question 17

While working on identifying risks, a project manager noticed that there is a disagreement among team
members concerning the appropriate way to execute an important work item. What can the project manager do
to *mitigate* this risk?

A. Outsource the concerned work item
B. Enroll the team in a training about the technologies related to executing that work item
C. Let the team self-organize and figure out how to execute the work item
D. Escalate the issue to the functional manager

**Correct answer: B.** Providing training on technologies related to the work item helps the team fully
understand how it should be executed. This strategy *mitigates* the risk by reducing conflict between
team members. The other options are different strategies: outsourcing = **avoiding**, letting the team
figure it out alone = **accepting**, going to the functional manager = **escalating**.

## Question 18

A project manager was notified by management that they're sending a team of experts to conduct a risk
audit. Even though the project manager had already completed a risk review process without finding any
shortcomings, management insisted on proceeding with the risk audit. Which of the following statements
are true about risk audits and risk reviews? (Select two)

A. A risk audit is conducted to examine the risk response plan
B. A risk review is conducted to examine the risk response plan
C. A risk audit is conducted to investigate whether the risk response plan is being followed
D. A risk review is conducted to investigate whether the risk response plan is being followed

**Correct answer: B & C.** A **risk audit** checks risk management effectiveness and whether the correct
procedures are *being followed*; risk audits look *backward* at what has occurred. A **risk review**
examines the effectiveness of the risk management plan, re-evaluating the risk environment as well as
risk events and their relative probability and impact (PMBOK 6th edition, pages 456–457).

## Question 19

A project manager is assigned to a new construction project to build a new office complex. During the
planning phase, the project manager identifies a significant risk related to adverse weather conditions
during construction. The project timeline is tight, and any delays due to weather could impact the overall
project schedule and budget. To effectively manage this risk, the project manager must select the most
appropriate risk response strategy. Match the risk response with the corresponding risk response strategy.

*Strategies (left column):* Accept · Mitigate · Escalate · Avoid · Transfer

*Risk responses (right column, in the slide's order — not the answer order):*

- Purchase weather insurance coverage to protect against potential financial losses resulting from
  weather-related delays
- Revise the construction schedule to include buffers for potential weather-related delays
- Seek the approval of senior management for additional budget allocation or resources to implement
  contingency measures
- Implement alternative construction methods or materials that are less susceptible to weather impacts
- Acknowledge the risk of adverse weather conditions without opting for any proactive action

**Correct answers:**

- **Accept:** acknowledge the risk of adverse weather conditions without opting for any proactive action.
- **Mitigate:** revise the construction schedule to include buffers for potential weather-related delays.
- **Escalate:** seek the approval of senior management for additional budget allocation or resources to
  implement contingency measures.
- **Avoid:** implement alternative construction methods or materials that are less susceptible to weather
  impacts.
- **Transfer:** purchase weather insurance coverage to protect against potential financial losses
  resulting from weather-related delays.

## Question 20

A project manager is managing a national book fair. Early in the planning phase, they identified some
low-impact risks. What should the project manager do next?

A. Ignore the risks since their impact is fairly low
B. Add the risks to the risk register and keep monitoring them
C. Develop a detailed risk response
D. Report the risks to the fair sponsor(s)

**Correct answer: B.** **Passive risk acceptance** is an appropriate approach for low-impact risks. No
proactive action is needed for passive acceptance other than periodically reviewing the threat to ensure
it is not significantly changing. **Active** risk acceptance, by contrast, entails developing a detailed
risk response or a contingency plan that would be implemented if the event occurred (PMBOK 7th edition,
page 123).
