## Question 1

A construction PM signs a firm-fixed contract with a supplier to procure materials. On the agreed
delivery date, the supplier delivers all materials to the site. What should the PM do next?

A. Inspect the materials to ensure compliance with the requirements stipulated in the contract
B. Start work using the provided materials to avoid delays
C. Start work using part of the materials while inspecting the rest
D. Pay the supplier and close the contract

**Correct answer: A.** Before using any procured materials, the PM and team must inspect them for
compliance with the contract's specifications and standards. If they pass inspection, then the supplier
gets paid and the contract is closed — inspection always comes before payment/closure, and before use.

## Question 2

A team builds a packaging machine that goes through exhaustive internal verification. When the client
receives it, they call the PM asking whether the machine meets requirements. What should the PM do next?

A. Confirm the product was internally tested and formally close the project
B. Perform the control quality process and use the contingency reserve to fix any detected issues
C. Collaborate with the client to get their formal approval that the product complies with requirements
D. Send an engineer to the client's site to double-check the product requirements

**Correct answer: C.** The main objective of Validate Scope is to work with stakeholders to get their
*formal approval* of scope and deliverables. Internal testing alone isn't sufficient — the client must
validate the product meets *their* requirements. Scope and quality control happen before shipping; here
the client hasn't reported a problem, just asked a validation question.

## Question 3

A team member finds the original code doesn't comply with the organization's coding standards while
working on a new feature. What should the team member do?

A. Ignore the code compliance work as it will be considered Gold Plating
B. Ask the Product Owner to add a new item to the product backlog dedicated to technical debt
C. Start refactoring the code and update the time estimate of the feature
D. Discuss with the rest of the team and add a new technical task to the sprint backlog

**Correct answer: B.** The team member should raise this with the Product Owner to get technical debt
added to the *product backlog* as an item. Doing nothing shows low ownership; making the change right
away by adding it to the sprint backlog compromises the current iteration, since the work wasn't planned
for beforehand.

## Question 5

A construction organization launches a project to build a wastewater treatment center. At project start,
the PM receives updated building codes and regulations from local authorities. To ensure regulatory
compliance, what should the PM do next?

A. Update the project schedule to reflect revised timelines for permits/approvals
B. Modify the quality management plan to include updated quality standards/specifications
C. Modify the procurement management plan to account for changed materials/equipment specs
D. Update the communication management plan to keep stakeholders informed of the changes

**Correct answer: B.** Modifying the quality management plan to include the updated standards/
specifications is the right next step — it ensures deliverables align with the updated requirements and
the project stays regulatory-compliant.

## Question 6

A PM needs 1000 custom-made identical filters for an industrial project from a reliable, well-known
supplier. A tested filter can't be reused once tested. What should the PM do?

A. Skip inspection since he trusts the supplier
B. Order more than 1000 filters to perform acceptance sampling on the batch surplus
C. Require the seller to provide a conformance certificate of the filter's raw materials
D. Conduct a 100% inspection upon delivery, then order another batch if satisfied

**Correct answer: B.** When 100% inspection isn't possible (here, because testing destroys the item),
the PM should sample a portion of the batch/lot and inspect the sample. Since tested filters can't be
reused for the project, ordering more than needed lets the PM run acceptance sampling on the surplus.

## Question 7

A team member assigned to create the project status report doesn't submit it on time, expressing
frustration that the report doesn't reflect reality, is too long, and won't be thoroughly reviewed
anyway. What should the PM do?

A. Review the requirements of the report with the team member to redefine its purpose
B. Instruct the team member to submit it as requested and document the delay in the performance review
C. Reassign the report to another team member
D. Notify the team member's functional manager, seeking their intervention

**Correct answer: A.** Reviewing the report's requirements with the team member to redefine its purpose
addresses the *root cause* of the frustration — collaborating to reassess purpose/requirements ensures
the report actually aligns with the project's and stakeholders' needs.

## Question 8

Before announcing completion of project execution, a construction PM reviews deliverables and performs a
site walkthrough. What is the PM performing?

A. An inspection
B. A handing over
C. An audit
D. A scope validation

**Correct answer: A.** During an inspection, the PM reviews deliverables and performs a site walkthrough
if applicable. Audits check whether project activities comply with organizational/project policies,
processes, and procedures (PMBOK 6th edition, page 294) — audits examine *processes*, inspections examine
*products*. This isn't scope validation (with the customer) or a handover either.

## Question 9

A PM notices colleagues waste office supplies and inventory is wasted, underutilized, or expired. The PM
suggests inventory management should follow the ______ technique to keep less inventory and reduce
wastage. Which technique?

A. Lean management
B. KAIZEN
C. Kanban
D. Just in Time

**Correct answer: D.** Just In Time (JIT) means only using/acquiring resources when they're needed —
instead of a large spare-parts inventory, you obtain parts only as needed. Lean management is a
philosophy (not a technique) that also values eliminating waste; Kanban is a lean method for managing
work; Kaizen is continuous incremental improvement, a different concept from inventory timing.

## Question 10

While beta testing with a small group, a PM finds defects: UI/responsiveness (20%), UX (25%), bugs in
functionality (40%), missing features (10%), other (5%). What tool can illustrate this breakdown?

A. Histogram
B. Quality Checklists
C. Scatter Diagram
D. Flowchart

**Correct answer: A.** A histogram is a vertical bar chart showing how often a particular variable state
occurs (PMBOK 7th edition, page 189) — it breaks data into categories (here, defect types) to support
informed decisions, with each bar representing one category's share.

## Question 11

A PM discovers a contracted software team isn't meeting performance requirements — code quality is below
standard, causing crashes and bottlenecks that compromise the e-commerce platform. What should the PM do?

A. Initiate a formal review process with the team to assess issues and implement a corrective action plan
B. Consider litigation/arbitration to enforce compliance and seek compensation
C. Prioritize essential functionality that other teams can develop/revise instead
D. Conduct an independent assessment of the non-conforming contractor's work

**Correct answer: A.** A structured review process plus a collaboratively developed corrective action
plan is proactive issue resolution — the right first move, before escalating to legal action or working
around the contractor entirely.

## Question 12

A cost-of-quality report totals $30,000: Training $10,000, Rework $10,000, Inspection $5,000, Scrap
$3,000, Warranty charges $2,000. How much is the appraisal cost?

A. $2,000
B. $3,000
C. $5,000
D. $10,000

**Correct answer: C ($5,000 — Inspection).** Appraisal costs (aka inspection costs) are part of the Cost
of Conformance — testing, inspecting, auditing, evaluating, and measuring deliverables to catch defects
before delivery. Training = Prevention cost (Cost of Conformance). Rework = Internal Failure cost, Scrap
= Internal Failure cost, Warranty charges = External Failure cost (all Cost of Non-Conformance).

## Question 13

A few days after a new release, the Product Owner gets a user complaint about an annoying defect. What
should the Product Owner do next?

A. Create an item to fix the defect, add it to the product backlog, then prioritize it
B. Ask the development team to fix the defect immediately since the product is already released
C. Estimate the effort required to fix the defect, then decide what to do
D. Ask the development team to roll back the last release to explore undetected defects

**Correct answer: A.** In Agile, any change request — a new requirement or a defect fix — goes into the
Product Backlog and is prioritized by the Product Owner. If critical, it can be pulled into the next
sprint. Being already released doesn't mean immediate fixing is required; rolling back over one detected
defect is an overreaction; and estimating effort is the development team's job, not the PO's.

## Question 14

An agile team discussing technical choices for a new project recalls a similar past project that went
over budget and behind schedule due to big challenges. What should the PM do next?

A. Look for the past project's performance details in Organizational Process Assets (OPAs)
B. Look for them in Enterprise Environmental Factors (EEF)
C. Look for them in the issue log
D. Since the past project was predictive, its details are irrelevant

**Correct answer: A.** OPAs include organizational processes/procedures and the corporate knowledge
base. To learn about the past project's performance, the PM would check its files — scope, schedule,
cost, and quality baselines, administrative documentation, stakeholder register, issue log, etc. — all of
which live in OPAs.

## Question 15

At a trade show, a prospect asks a security-appliance company to provide and install 10 firewalls in 5
weeks incrementally (2/week), with an external expert running a penetration test, and final acceptance
granted only after all simulated attacks are detected and prevented. What is the prospect defining?
(Select two)

A. Definition of Done
B. Quality Assurance
C. Technical Requirements
D. Quality Control

**Correct answer: A, D.** A Definition of Done (DoD) is a checklist of criteria for considering a
product ready for delivery (PMBOK 7th edition, page 81) — the acceptance condition described is exactly
this. The penetration test focused on *detecting* defects is Quality Control (defect identification); QA
instead focuses on defect *prevention*. Technical requirements would cover product specs (WiFi, storage,
etc.), not acceptance conditions.

## Question 16

After facing multiple quality issues, a PM applies the 80/20 rule (Pareto Chart) to identify top-priority
defects. What should the PM do next?

A. Find out the impact of each quality issue
B. Find out the frequency of each quality issue
C. Find out the urgency of each quality issue
D. Find out the cause of each quality issue

**Correct answer: B.** A Pareto diagram is a histogram ranking issues from highest to lowest *frequency*,
built on Pareto's Law (80% of problems come from 20% of causes).

## Question 17

A PM is asked to develop resource planning for a new, innovative project at organizations they've worked
with before, but they're skeptical about this project's business model. Should the PM use historical
information?

A. No — every project is unique, so there's rarely any correlation between projects' resource needs
B. No — historical information usually doesn't apply to innovative, rapidly changing projects
C. Yes — historical information can provide insight when comparing to resources used on similar past
   projects
D. Yes — accurate resource estimation is impossible without historical information

**Correct answer: C.** Historical information supports analogous and parametric estimating. Analogous
estimating is usually faster/cheaper than other techniques (e.g. bottom-up) but also less accurate — it's
still useful insight, just not the *only* path to an accurate estimate (ruling out D).

## Question 18

A product owner at a company that measures success purely by features-delivered-to-production sees
record feature volume, but customers are frustrated by bugs. What should the product owner do?

A. Keep the same pace; issues will be fixed later
B. Add a sprint backlog item for technical debt
C. Include customer satisfaction and quality KPIs as product success metrics
D. Discuss customer feedback during the demo

**Correct answer: C.** This is the Hawthorne effect (PMBOK 7th edition, page 112) — measuring only
output volume pushes the team toward volume over customer-satisfying quality. Including
customer-satisfaction and quality KPIs balances the team's focus.

## Question 19

One month into a new job, a PM leading their first hybrid project learns an external auditor will
conduct a quality audit next week. What should the PM expect from the audit?

A. It will identify whether the project is under budget and ahead of schedule
B. It will detect nonconformance compared to the scope baseline
C. It will examine whether the project follows company procedures
D. It will detect product defects and errors

**Correct answer: C.** Quality audits verify project activities comply with organizational/project
processes, policies, and procedures. Detecting product defects is Quality Control's job; budget/schedule
status is a performance review; scope-baseline nonconformance is Scope Control's job.

## Question 20

A PM leading a high-risk project with fluctuating performance and a high defect rate is asked by the
sponsor for an overview of work performance and current standing. Which document should the PM send?

A. Quality report
B. Status report
C. Risk report
D. Project management plan

**Correct answer: B.** The status report is a work performance report covering the project's progress
and status (PMBOK 7th edition, page 190) — it can include EV data, trend lines/forecasts, reserve
burndown, and defect/risk summaries. The sponsor asked broadly for performance/standing, not a
quality-specific or risk-specific deep-dive; the project management plan is a *planning* document, not a
reporting one.
