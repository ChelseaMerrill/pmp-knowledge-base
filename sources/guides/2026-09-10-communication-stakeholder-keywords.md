# Communication & Stakeholder Keywords Review

*Yassine Tounsi — PMP 35 Hours Master Course*

An alphabetical glossary of communication and stakeholder management terms.

### A-B-C

- **5Cs of written communication:** Written communication should be Correct, Concise, Clear,
  Coherent, and Controlled in order to be effective.
- **Active listening:** Consists of paying careful attention to what is being said, paraphrasing that
  information, and then feeding it back to the speaker to ensure that you clearly understood what they
  said.
- **Assertiveness:** communicating with others in a direct and honest manner without intentionally
  hurting anyone's feelings.
- **Asynchronous communication:** takes place when the communicating parties are not present at the
  same time. Examples are e-mail, blogs, messaging platforms, etc. where you send a message without
  expecting an instant reply.
- **Buy-in:** The result of reaching a consensus or alignment with a proposition, solution or plan.
- **Collaboration:** a conflict resolution technique that relies on an open dialogue between conflict
  parties to discuss different perspectives and eventually reach a consensus and commit to the final
  resolution.
- **Communication barriers, Communication constraints:** obstacles that one may face when attempting
  to effectively communicate with another person. These barriers can be the lack of clarity and
  context, information overload, cultural differences, language barriers, noise and distractions, etc.
- **Communication channels:** are the possibilities of communication between the parties involved in
  the project. For example, 4 stakeholders have 6 communication channels.
- **Communication flow:** patterns of communication are called flows, and they are commonly
  classified according to the direction of interaction: downward, upward, horizontal, diagonal,
  external.

### C

- **Communication flow chart:** a graphical representation that illustrates how information flows
  within an organization or project. It maps out the process, departments, individuals, and
  communication channels involved.
- **Communication Management:** the processes and procedures needed to ensure that information and
  data throughout the life of a project are properly collected, stored, and distributed across the
  project team.
- **Communication Management Plan:** The documented and agreed policies, procedures and guidelines
  for directing and controlling the project's information and communications, including data storage
  and retrieval.
- **Communication mediums:** the means by which information is transmitted between the sender and the
  receiver. It may range from an individual's voice, writing, clothing, and body language to forms of
  mass communication such as newspapers, television, and the Internet.
- **Communication methods:** Ways of communicating, including verbal communication, non-verbal
  communication, listening, written communication, and visual communication.
- **Communication metrics:** KPIs or measures that help you evaluate the performance and impact of
  your communication. Examples include engagement rate, open rate, page visits, satisfaction rate, etc.
- **Communication model:** represents the communication process. The basic communication model
  comprises five components: sender, message, channel, receiver, and feedback.
- **Communication strategy:** defines how information will be disseminated to and received from all
  stakeholders involved in the project. It identifies the content and frequency of communication
  between the different parties.
- **Communication style:** describes how a person communicates. There are four main styles: passive
  communication, aggressive communication, passive-aggressive communication, and assertive
  communication.

### C-D-E-F

- **Compromise, Reconciliation:** a conflict resolution technique that requires conflicted parties to
  give up something in the process in order to achieve a commonly agreed-upon solution and bring
  everyone involved a certain level of satisfaction.
- **Conflict:** happens when there are incompatible goals, thoughts, or emotions between individuals
  that result in opposition and disagreements.
- **Conflict resolution techniques:** a way for two or more parties to find a solution to a
  disagreement among them. These techniques include Confronting, Collaborating, Forcing, Compromising,
  Smoothing, and finally Avoiding.
- **Confrontation, Problem-solving:** A conflict resolution technique in which the conflict is treated
  as a problem that needs to be solved. Therefore, the conflicting parties have an open discussion
  while focusing on finding the best solution. This is often used when the parties involved in the
  conflict have confidence in each other's judgment and ability to solve the issue.
- **Cultural awareness, Cultural sensitivity, Cross-cultural sensitivity:** being aware of the
  differences between two cultures when communicating or interacting.
- **Decision paralysis, Analysis paralysis, Choice paralysis:** when an individual or group
  overanalyzes or overthinks a situation, causing forward motion or decision-making to become
  "paralyzed", meaning that no solution or course of action is decided upon within a natural time
  frame.
- **Engaged stakeholder, Committed stakeholder:** A stakeholder who is aware and supportive, and wants
  the project to achieve its goal.
- **External stakeholders:** people or groups outside the business. This includes customers, users,
  suppliers, and investors.
- **Feedback loops:** the ongoing process of collecting, analyzing, and implementing feedback.

### F-H-I

- **Feedback mechanisms:** refer to communication structures set up within a project to allow
  stakeholders to give their feedback throughout the project lifecycle.
- **Forcing, Directing:** A conflict resolution technique in which a dominating person forces their
  point of view or solution to resolve the conflict. Forcing is less time-consuming than other
  techniques since there is little or no discussion.
- **Formal communication:** structured communication that follows predetermined channels. This method
  involves official meetings, reports, and documented exchanges that are carefully planned to convey
  critical information.
- **High context culture:** cultures in which people communicate implicitly and rely heavily on
  context.
- **Horizontal communication:** occurs at the same organizational level; with peers or colleagues on
  the same level or between project team members.
- **Influence Diagram:** A model showing how the events or activities in a project influence each
  other.
- **Informal communication:** the casual and spontaneous way of exchanging information in a project.
  It includes meetings, calls, emails, chats, etc.
- **Interactive communication, Synchronous communication:** involves a real-time dynamic sharing of
  information back and forth between one or more people. This type of communication is used in
  meetings, phone calls, videoconferencing, etc.
- **Internal stakeholders:** people or groups within the business, such as team members, functional
  managers, executives, and so on.

### I-K-L-M

- **Interpersonal skills:** Skills used to establish and maintain relationships with other people.
  Interpersonal skills include active listening, empathy, conflict resolution, teamwork, effective
  communication, and relationship building.
- **Keep informed:** the engagement strategy that should be used with stakeholders that have high
  interest and low influence or power on the project; these stakeholders should be kept informed about
  project developments to manage their expectations.
- **Keep satisfied:** the engagement strategy that should be used with stakeholders that have high
  influence or power and low interest in the project. While they have the power to impact the project,
  these stakeholders may not be directly interested in it. Therefore, you should focus on keeping them
  satisfied without overwhelming them with project details.
- **Key stakeholders:** stakeholders who have the influence and authority to dictate whether a project
  is a success or not. These are the people and groups whose objectives must be satisfied, as they
  have the power to make or break the project.
- **Levels of conflict:** a conflict has 5 levels: Problem to solve, Disagreement, Contest, Crusade,
  World War. Each level requires a specific conflict resolution and management technique to stop
  things from escalating to the next level.
- **Lose-leave situation:** The result of withdrawing or avoiding a conflict; Since the conflict is
  not resolved, one party loses while the withdrawing party leaves.
- **Lose-lose situation:** The result of conflict resolution through compromise or reconciliation
  since this technique only brings a temporary or partial solution, and everyone has to make
  concessions.
- **Manage closely:** the engagement strategy that should be used with stakeholders that have high
  interest and high influence and power over the project. Therefore, it's essential to actively engage
  with these stakeholders, keep them informed, and address their concerns.
- **Meeting:** an event where some or all of the project team and stakeholders gather to discuss the
  project.

### M-N-O-P

- **Monitor:** the engagement strategy that should be used with stakeholders that have low interest
  and influence over the project; these stakeholders require a minimum effort by keeping an eye on
  them in case their situation changes without devoting excessive resources to them.
- **Mood Chart:** A visualization chart for tracking stakeholders' emotions, moods or reactions to
  identify areas for improvement.
- **Net Promoter Score (NPS):** Allows stakeholders to assign a numerical score that ranges from 0 to
  10 to indicate their satisfaction level with the project, product, or service, as well as their
  willingness to recommend it to others.
- **Neutral Stakeholder:** A stakeholder who is aware of the project but is neither supportive nor
  resistant.
- **Noise:** anything that interferes with the delivery of the message in the sender-receiver model,
  including physiological noise such as talking too fast or too slow, psychological noise such as
  judgment or sarcasm, physical noise such as a baby crying, and semantic noise such as the use of
  jargon.
- **Nonverbal communication:** the non-linguistic messages that are consciously or unconsciously
  encoded and decoded through facial expressions, bodily gestures, space, touch, eye contact, tone,
  etc.
- **Osmotic communication:** Absorbing information subconsciously by overhearing useful discussions of
  other colocated team members.
- **Paralingual communication:** A subpart of nonverbal communication that involves the use of voice
  tone, volume, pitch, intonation, etc., to express feelings or opinions.
- **Power/Interest Grid, Stakeholder Analysis Matrix, Stakeholder Prioritization Matrix:** a technique
  used to categorize stakeholders based on their levels of power or influence and interest in a
  project.

### P-R-S

- **Project governance:** The framework of policies, processes, and practices that guide project
  decision-making, ensuring alignment with organizational objectives, accountability, and successful
  project delivery.
- **Pull communication:** the sender creates content that receivers can passively access at any time.
  Examples include blog posts, corporate wikis or handbooks, file sharing services such as Google
  Drive, etc.
- **Push communication:** the sender pushes out information to the receiver without their active
  request. Examples of push communication include emails, newsletters, memos, and automated
  notifications.
- **Resistant stakeholder:** a stakeholder who's aware of the project but doesn't support it.
- **Round-Robin:** involves going around the group and asking each person to share their ideas,
  opinions, or feedback on a specific topic or question. This technique ensures that everyone has a
  chance to contribute and that no one dominates the discussion.
- **Sender/Receiver model:** a communication process in which a message is encoded, from the sender to
  the receiver through a communication technology or channel, and decoded by the receiver to easily
  understand it.
- **Smoothing, Accommodating:** a conflict resolution technique that consists in smoothing
  disagreements to maintain harmony and reduce disputes especially when there is a difference in power
  or authority between the conflicting parties.
- **Sponsor:** an individual or a group that provides financial and other resources and support to a
  project. The project sponsor is usually at the senior management level and either owns the project
  or has contributed to its existence.
- **Stakeholder:** Any individual or organization that is actively involved in the project, or who may
  be positively or negatively affected by the project.

### S

- **Stakeholder advocacy:** involves actively representing the interests, views, and concerns of
  project stakeholders, ensuring that their needs and expectations are met or exceeded.
- **Stakeholder alignment:** the process of creating consensus for project decisions among all
  relevant key stakeholders.
- **Stakeholder Analysis:** A method of systematically gathering and analyzing quantitative and
  qualitative information to determine whose interests should be taken into account throughout the
  project.
- **Stakeholder Communication:** the correspondence among all of the people invested in a project.
  Stakeholder communication strategies and mediums differ and depend on stakeholders' preferences and
  the type of communicated information.
- **Stakeholder Engagement, Stakeholder Involvement:** includes implementing strategies and actions to
  promote the productive involvement of stakeholders. Stakeholder engagement activities should be
  carried out throughout the whole project lifecycle.
- **Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix:** A matrix that compares current and desired stakeholder
  engagement levels.
- **Stakeholder Engagement Plan:** identifies the strategies and actions required to promote
  stakeholders' involvement in the project.
- **Stakeholder Identification:** the process of identifying which parties are classified as
  stakeholders in a project, either because they can influence the project or because they are
  directly affected by its result.
- **Stakeholder Influence:** indicates a stakeholder's relative power over and within a project. A
  stakeholder with high influence would control key decisions within the project and have a strong
  ability to either facilitate or hinder project implementation.

### S-U-V

- **Stakeholder Management:** a process that includes communicating with stakeholders, identifying
  their needs, and managing their expectations of a project.
- **Stakeholder Management Plan:** a document that outlines how the project team plans to manage the
  goals and expectations of key stakeholders during the project lifecycle.
- **Stakeholder register:** A record that includes information about project stakeholders such as
  their names, contacts, roles, classifications, etc.
- **Stakeholder satisfaction:** a key indicator of how well the project is meeting the needs and
  expectations of its various groups of interest, such as customers, employees, investors, suppliers,
  partners, regulators, and communities.
- **Stakeholder Sensitivity Analysis:** a tool intended to provide a picture of the key stakeholders
  and what they think of the project or a particular issue.
- **Status meeting:** is a regularly scheduled event to exchange and analyze information about the
  current progress of the project and its performance.
- **Storytelling:** a narrative method that uses telling and listening to convey information.
- **Unaware stakeholder:** A stakeholder that doesn't know about the project, its objectives, and its
  impact.
- **Verbal communication:** the use of spoken and written words to get a message and information
  across to the other person.

### V-W-Y

- **Vertical communication:** occurs between different hierarchical levels, for example communication
  between the project manager and the CEO.
- **Virtual communication:** refers to communication that includes the use of technology such as
  emails, text messages, webchats, and other forms of communication that do not happen in person.
- **Win-lose situation:** The result of the forcing or directing conflict resolution technique; Since a
  dominating perspective is imposed on the conflict parties, they end up being the winner in the given
  situation while the other party loses.
- **Win-win situation:** The result of conflict resolution through collaboration; since everyone's
  viewpoints are discussed and incorporated into the agreed-upon final solution, everyone is a winner.
- **Withdrawal, Avoiding:** A conflict resolution technique that implies the unwillingness to deal with
  a conflict by either postponing it or completely avoiding it. This is used when one side of the
  conflict is outranked or they're trying to cool things down, or when the other side of the conflict
  is unassailable or uncooperative.
- **Yield-lose situation:** The result of smoothing a conflict.
