# Requirements-Gathering and Decision-Making Techniques

Covers **Collect requirements** (determining, documenting, and managing stakeholder needs to meet
project objectives) and the broader toolkit for making group decisions and prioritizing work. The exam
likes to test "which technique fits this scenario" — the distinguishing detail is usually who's
involved and how.

## Gathering ideas / requirements

- **Brainstorming:** a group discussion where participants quickly share as many ideas as possible;
  inputs are analyzed afterward to define a requirement or find a solution.
- **Brainwriting:** like brainstorming, but each participant reflects and writes their ideas
  individually before the group session — reduces groupthink/dominant-voice bias.
- **Focus Groups:** a facilitated, face-to-face conversation bringing together stakeholders and SMEs to
  share input on a specific subject.
- **Delphi technique:** builds consensus from expert opinion through several rounds of *anonymous*
  surveys, each round informed by the previous round's answers. **Wideband Delphi** is a variant where
  the team discusses estimates together after each round until they converge.
- **Design thinking:** an iterative process to understand users, challenge assumptions, redefine
  problems, and prototype/test innovative solutions.

## Organizing and prioritizing ideas

- **Affinity diagram:** classifies a large number of ideas into groups/clusters for review.
- **Mind Map:** used after brainstorming/nominal-group sessions to visually organize relationships
  between collected ideas.
- **Nominal Group technique:** enhances brainstorming with a voting step to rank the most useful ideas
  for further work or prioritization.
- **MoSCoW:** prioritizes requirements into Must Have, Should Have, Could Have, Won't Have.
- **Kano model:** prioritizes product functionality by weighing implementation cost against its
  potential to satisfy customers.
- **Multicriteria Decision Analysis:** a systematic, structured way to evaluate and compare options
  against multiple criteria/objectives, for a transparent, objective decision.
- **Paired comparison / Pairwise comparison:** systematically compares a small set of options against
  each other, one pair at a time.

## Deciding as a group

- **Voting:** picking the option with more votes — can resolve by unanimity (100%), majority (>50%),
  or plurality (most votes, without needing a majority).
- **Last responsible moment:** deliberately delaying a decision until the latest possible point, to
  keep more options/information available before committing.

## Visualizing and analyzing

- **Context diagram:** shows how different elements interact — e.g. how third-party apps, APIs, and
  users interact with a product.
- **Data representation techniques:** graphical methods to visualize and organize project data for
  stakeholders.
- **Data-driven decision-making:** using facts, metrics, and data — rather than opinion — to guide
  decisions aligned with project goals.
- **Simulation:** an analytical/mathematical tool exploring the impact of variance on deliverables or
  outcomes.
- **Alternatives analysis:** compares time and cost across multiple options.
- **Spike:** a short, timeboxed investigation to answer a question or reduce uncertainty before
  committing to work (see also the glossary's agile Spike entry).
