# Interface Management

Covers the coordination side of "develop an integrated project management plan and plan delivery" —
managing the connections between parties and systems, not just the parties themselves.

## Definition

Interface management (IM) is the management of communications, relationships, and deliverables among
two or more **interface stakeholders**. The exam frame: with `n` stakeholders who must all coordinate
pairwise, there are potentially many interfaces to manage — much like the communication channel count
(see [communication-management.md](../people/communication-management.md)) — and IM is the discipline
of managing those connections deliberately rather than leaving them to chance.

## Advantages of doing it well

- Increased alignment
- Improved visibility and oversight
- Facilitated communication
- Mitigated risk

## Interface types — two independent axes

Interfaces are classified along two separate dimensions, and a given interface can be described by
both at once:

| Axis | Type | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | **Internal** | Identified within a single scope of work. |
| Scope | **External** | Identified between two or more scopes of work. |
| Nature | **Soft** | The exchange of information between delivery teams, or between teams and external parties. |
| Nature | **Hard** | Physical connections between two or more components or systems. |

Examples:

- **Internal:** the architectural team coordinates with the structural engineering team so the design
  aligns with load-bearing requirements — one scope of work (design), information exchange (soft).
- **External:** the construction company coordinates with local government for permits and zoning —
  two distinct parties/scopes (external), information exchange (soft).
- **Hard:** pre-fabricated steel beams from a supplier must physically fit the foundation and
  load-bearing columns — a physical connection between components (hard), which can be either
  internal or external depending on who's involved.

The exam trap: don't treat internal/external and soft/hard as a single four-way exclusive category —
they're two separate questions ("whose scope?" and "what kind of connection?") that combine.
