# Information & Knowledge Management

Covers "manage project knowledge" and the surrounding information/document-management vocabulary. See
[pmis.md](pmis.md) for the *tooling* layer (the software that stores/moves this information) and
[../people/communication-management.md](../people/communication-management.md) for the *plan* that
decides what information goes where — this note covers the data itself, the knowledge-transfer
mechanics, and the records/documents that carry it.

## Data → Information → Knowledge

Three distinct levels, often tested as a matching/classification exercise:

- **Data:** raw facts and records collected for reference or analysis (e.g. "task completion status,"
  "a burndown chart value").
- **Information:** *processed* data that provides meaning, context, or understanding (e.g. "this task's
  delay puts the release at risk").
- **Knowledge:** information and skills acquired through experience or education, applied to improve
  outcomes — the product of **Knowledge Management**, the process of creating and applying that
  knowledge.

A raw fact ("task not yet complete") is data; an interpreted signal built from several facts ("this had
a huge impact on the burndown chart") is information; a lesson drawn from a pattern of such signals,
kept for future use, is knowledge.

## Explicit vs. tacit knowledge

- **Explicit (codified) knowledge:** can be captured directly using words, pictures, or numbers —
  documentation, procedures, specifications.
- **Tacit knowledge:** difficult to articulate and share — beliefs, experience, insights, judgment calls.
  This is the harder kind to transfer, and the main reason **lessons learned meetings** exist: to
  surface tacit knowledge (a senior member's memory of a past project's struggles) before it's lost.

## Lessons learned

- **Lessons learned:** the knowledge gained during the *current* project, meant to improve *future*
  performance.
- **Lessons learned meeting:** the event used to identify and share that knowledge during a project,
  phase, or iteration — this is where tacit knowledge (a team member's recollection of a similar past
  project) gets pulled into the open.
- **Lessons learned register:** the document that actually records it, for later reuse (see the seeded
  glossary entry).
- When a team recalls a *past* project's performance details (e.g. "it went over budget and behind
  schedule"), the place to actually go find those details is **Organizational Process Assets (OPA)** —
  OPAs bundle the organization's processes/procedures with its corporate knowledge base, including past
  projects' scope/schedule/cost/quality baselines, administrative documentation, stakeholder registers,
  and issue logs. This holds regardless of whether the past project was predictive or adaptive — the
  performance data is still relevant, just filed under OPA rather than EEF (which covers external
  conditions, not internal historical records) or the current project's own issue log.

## Documents and records

- **Documentation:** creating written/electronic records describing procedures, specifications, or
  information.
- **Document Management:** the system/process for capturing, tracking, and storing documents.
- **Records Management (Records Keeping):** creating, storing, and maintaining project records more
  broadly (not just documents).
- **Version / Version Control:** a version is one iteration/variant of a product, document, or software
  (later versions typically expand on earlier ones); version control applies numerical identifiers to
  track changes, so a team can manage and collaborate on different versions without overwriting each
  other's work.
- **Naming convention:** rules for consistent, meaningful file/variable names — a low-tech but
  frequently-tested piece of document-management hygiene.
- **Classification:** assigning a document to relevant categories for easier management and analysis.
- **Archive:** documents preserved and stored for reference, research, or historical purposes (as
  opposed to active working documents).
- **Backup / Recovery:** a backup is a copy of data usable if the original becomes unavailable;
  recovery is the act of restoring that backup to replace lost/damaged original data.
- **Cloud Storage:** data stored on a server, accessible over the Internet.
- **Database:** a collection of data/information specially organized for rapid computer search and
  retrieval.
- **Information Retrieval:** the process of finding relevant information/a document from a large
  repository (a database, an archive).
- **Access Control (Authorization):** regulating who can access/interact with documents, for security
  and confidentiality — paired with **Information Security**: identifying, assessing, and managing
  information security risks more broadly.
- **Project Librarian (Project Data Administrator):** the role responsible for managing a project's
  information and documents — the person actually running document management day to day.

## Meeting artifacts

- **Agenda:** the list of topics to be discussed at a meeting.
- **Facilitator:** encourages knowledge sharing and decision-making during a meeting.
- **Meeting minutes:** the written record of everything presented and discussed.
- **Action items:** activities agreed to as a result of the discussion — usually recorded in the
  minutes.
- **Kick-off meeting:** held at the start of a project or phase to communicate objectives and gain
  stakeholder commitment.

## Reporting

- **Report:** a formal document presenting information, findings, analysis, or recommendations on a
  topic or issue — the general category.
- **Status Report:** a specific report type covering the project's *current* progress/status, including
  cost and schedule performance (can include EV data, trend lines/forecasts, reserve burndown, and
  defect/risk summaries). When a sponsor asks broadly "where does the project stand," the status report
  is the right document — not a narrower quality report or risk report (unless those were specifically
  requested), and not the project management plan, which is a *planning* document, not a reporting one.

## Information radiator

**Information radiator (Visual control, Big Visible Charts):** a visible, physical display giving
instant project information without needing to ask or disturb the team — e.g. a posted Kanban board.
This overlaps with, but is broader than, the Kanban board itself (see
[../people/agile-roles-and-events.md](../people/agile-roles-and-events.md)): any big, visible chart
serving that purpose counts.

## People roles

- **Learning and Development (L&D) Manager:** designs and implements programs to build employees'
  knowledge, skills, and capabilities — an organizational role, distinct from the project-level Project
  Librarian.
