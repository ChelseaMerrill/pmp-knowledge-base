# Team Resource Management

Covers the administrative/documentation side of running a team — acquiring and tracking resources,
the RACI/RAM distinction, onboarding through offboarding, and training/performance assessment
vocabulary. Pairs with [leadership-and-team.md](leadership-and-team.md) (styles, motivation, Tuckman)
and [conflict-resolution.md](conflict-resolution.md) (handling disagreement).

## Acquiring and developing the team

- **Acquire Resources:** the process of obtaining team members, facilities, equipment, materials,
  supplies, and other resources needed for project work — via assignment (when available) or by
  hiring/recruiting.
- **Pre-assignment:** resources are assigned/acquired *before* the project starts, used when the
  project depends on a specific person's expertise.
- **Develop Team:** the process of improving competencies, team member interaction, and the overall
  team environment to enhance project performance — distinct from Acquire Resources (getting people)
  vs. growing their capability once they're on the team.
- **Under-allocation:** more resources than necessary for the work at hand — not enough workload to
  fill the available resources.

## RACI / RAM vs. resource calendars — know which document answers which question

- **RACI:** Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed — a type of **Responsibility Assignment
  Matrix (RAM)**, which visually defines team members' roles/responsibilities and how they're involved
  in project activities. Answers: *who does what, and who signs off?*
- **Resource calendar:** a schedule of when team members (and equipment) are available to work —
  answers: *is this resource free in the next two weeks?* This is the document to check for
  cross-team scheduling dependencies (e.g. "is the electricity team free to unblock this task?") — not
  the RAM, RACI, or Organigram, which describe responsibility/reporting structure, not availability.
- **Resource Breakdown Structure (RBS), Organogram:** a hierarchical representation of a project's or
  organization's resources.
- **Resource histogram:** a bar chart showing utilization frequency of physical/human resources across
  the project life cycle.
- **Resource Optimization Technique:** adjusts activities' start/finish dates so planned resource
  utilization stays at or below resource availability.
- **Resource Management Plan:** describes how project resources are acquired, allocated, monitored,
  and controlled — the governing document; the RAM/RACI, resource calendar, RBS, and histogram are the
  tools/outputs that support it.
- **Team Management Plan:** describes when and how team members will be acquired and how long they'll
  be needed.
- **Team Development Plan:** describes a training program to enhance the team's skills — both the
  project-specific work and working together as a team.
- **Project Team Directory:** a documented list of team members, roles, and communication addresses.
- **Team assignments:** specify the resources assigned to each activity.
- **Team Member Turnover:** the rate at which people leave the team over a period — a high rate
  signals a serious organizational issue (decreased productivity, higher recruitment costs, bad
  reputation, missed deadlines).

## Onboarding, offboarding, and transitions

- **Onboarding:** introducing the team to the project during initiation, to get everyone on the same
  page.
- **Offboarding:** transitioning a departing team member out — capturing knowledge, an exit interview,
  fulfilling HR requirements — so they leave on good terms with loose ends tied up.
- **Transition plan:** defines priorities, goals, strategies, and techniques for a successful
  responsibility transfer, helping the team cope with change and stay stable through a permanent or
  temporary stakeholder departure.

## Training and assessment vocabulary

A sequence, not independent terms — read top to bottom as a pipeline:

1. **Pre-training assessment:** establishes a baseline of where the team stands before training.
2. **Training gap analysis:** determines which skills the team is lacking, to plan adequate training.
3. Training happens (**Training:** teaching/developing skills and knowledge related to useful
   competencies).
4. **Mid-training assessment:** conducted mid-way through, to evaluate efficiency so far and allow
   adjustments.
5. **Post-training assessment:** compares results against the pre-training baseline to determine
   efficiency.
6. **Training Baselining:** the overall practice of pairing a pre-training assessment with a
   post-training assessment to understand the training program's actual impact on team skills.

Separately: **Ability test / Aptitude test** assesses skills/abilities in different domains —
used by recruiters to find fit candidates, and by PMs to measure the team's abilities/aptitudes to
plan proper training.

## Performance and satisfaction measurement

- **360-degree appraisal / Multi-rater feedback:** the PM evaluates a team member using feedback from
  their superiors, peers, clients, etc. — a holistic view, not just the PM's own opinion.
- **Quarterly, Biannual, or Annual Performance Review:** structured sessions reviewing recent
  achievements, improvement areas, and goals ahead.
- **Attitudinal survey:** measures team members' satisfaction and feelings about their roles, work
  environment, career opportunities, and organizational culture.
- **1on1 / 1:1 / 1to1 meeting:** a regular check-in, often PM-to-team-member, on work, career
  development, and growth.

## Workplace/office design and terms

- **Co-located team:** the whole team works in the same physical workspace.
- **Caves and Common office design:** blends private "Caves" (uninterrupted individual work) with a
  shared open "Common" space (typical team work).
- **Calendar day:** the daily working period, typically 9am–5pm.
- **War room:** a physical space for the team to collectively address project activities, issues, or
  workflows.
- **Virtual collocation:** team members "meet" as often as necessary via teleconferencing, without a
  shared physical space.

## Reward strategy design

- Reward the team **throughout the project life cycle**, not only at the end (e.g. every 6 months on
  a multi-year project) — recognition needs to happen while it's still motivating, not just as a
  closeout gesture.
- **Consider cultural differences** when designing rewards — recognition and rewards are perceived
  differently across cultures, especially on distributed/multicultural teams.
- **Zero Sum reward:** rewarding only one or a few team members (e.g. "employee of the month") — can
  create contempt or envy among those not rewarded; generally a pattern to be cautious of.

## Cross-cultural terms

- **Cultural norms:** expectations for behavior within a culture or social group.
- **Cultural shock:** the confusion/uncertainty of encountering a very different culture — 4 stages:
  honeymoon, anxiety/frustration, adjustment/adaptation, acceptance.
