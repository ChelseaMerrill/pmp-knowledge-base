# Conflict Resolution

Covers "manage conflicts" — the technique ranking, and the procedural habits the exam expects around
handling conflict day to day.

## The five (six-named) techniques, ranked best → weakest for long-term resolution

1. **Confronting / Collaborating / Problem-solving** (win/win) — addresses the actual underlying
   concern; the strongest long-term technique. ("Confronting" and "collaborating" name the same top
   tier in different sources — treat them as one.)
2. **Compromising / Reconciling** (lose/lose, but preserves the relationship) — both sides give up
   something.
3. **Accommodating / Smoothing** (yield/lose) — one side yields to preserve harmony.
4. **Forcing / Directing** (win/lose) — one party pushes their position through regardless of input.
5. **Withdrawal / Avoiding** (lose/leave) — the conflict isn't addressed at all.

See also the glossary's seeded "Conflict Resolution Techniques" entry — same ranking, slightly
different naming convention. Know both sets of names.

## How to actually run it

- Find the **root cause** first — don't jump to a resolution technique before understanding what's
  actually driving the disagreement.
- Use **face-to-face communication**, and do it **in private**, not in front of the rest of the team.
- If the team surfaces the issue at a stand-up or similar: find the root cause, then decide the action
  — don't just note it and move on.

## Disagreement with a stakeholder specifically

1. Analyze the gap between the deliverable and the agreed acceptance criteria.
2. **If the PM/team is right:** meet with the stakeholder to explain and convince them. Convinced →
   done. Not convinced → escalate (as a last resort, not a first move).
3. **If the PM/team is wrong:** take corrective action — typically via a change request — then inform
   the stakeholder.

Escalation to the sponsor or Steering Committee is always the last resort in these scenarios, never
the first move.
