# Stakeholder Engagement

Covers the People domain ECO tasks: engage stakeholders · align stakeholder expectations · manage
stakeholder expectations.

## What counts as a stakeholder

A stakeholder is anyone involved in or impacted by the project — not just the people doing the work.
The exam likes to test this broad definition: it includes people who are affected by the outcome even
if they never touch a deliverable (e.g. neighbors of a construction site, regulators).

Common stakeholder categories, with a construction-project example of who falls into each:

| Category | Example roles |
|---|---|
| Project Team | Architects & designers, engineers, foreman, labor |
| Project Manager | Project manager, superintendent, team manager |
| Sponsor | Customer, client, owner |
| Users / End-users | Homeowner, tenant |
| Suppliers | General contractors, providers & vendors, subcontractors, legal advisors |
| Government | Local authorities, regulators |
| Financial Institutions | Banks, venture capital firms, business angels |
| Others | Neighbors, family members |

The point of the taxonomy isn't to memorize the table — it's to prompt a wider stakeholder identification
than "the people in the room." Government bodies, financial institutions, and even neighbors are
legitimate stakeholders if the project affects them, and missing them during identification is a common
source of late-project risk and delay.

## Stakeholder Management Plan

The Stakeholder Management Plan is the project document that formalizes the approach above: it guides
how stakeholders will be identified, analyzed, prioritized, engaged, and monitored throughout the
project. It ensures systematic engagement, improves project success, and clarifies who is responsible
for managing which stakeholder interactions — i.e. it's the plan; the register, power/interest grid,
and align/manage activities below are how that plan gets executed.

## Identifying and analyzing stakeholders

- **Stakeholder register:** the document that captures identified stakeholders along with assessment
  and classification information (interest, influence, attitude toward the project).
- **Power/interest grid:** a stakeholder-analysis tool that plots stakeholders by their level of
  authority (power) and level of concern (interest), used to decide how much engagement effort each
  stakeholder warrants (e.g. manage closely vs. monitor).

## Aligning vs. managing expectations

These are two distinct ECO tasks and the exam draws a real line between them:

- **Align stakeholder expectations** happens early — setting realistic, shared expectations about
  scope, outcomes, and constraints before/as the project starts.
- **Manage stakeholder expectations** is ongoing — actively communicating and negotiating as the
  project runs, so expectations stay aligned with reality as things change.

Effective communication with stakeholders throughout is what actually minimizes risk and avoids delay
— identification alone isn't enough. See [communication-management.md](communication-management.md)
for the Communication Management Plan and the channel-count formula.

## The Sponsor, specifically

The Sponsor assigns the PM, provides authority, supports the team, and is the first escalation point
when a matter is beyond the PM's own authority. The sponsor sits on the **Steering Committee (SteeCo)**
— an advisory body that provides direction and makes decisions outside the project team's authority
(see the glossary's Steering Committee entry). The customer is a key stakeholder but a distinct role
from the sponsor.

## Measuring engagement, and which plan to check

- **NPS (Net Promoter Score)** and **mood charts** are the tools used to measure stakeholder
  satisfaction over time.
- If a stakeholder isn't committed or keeps skipping meetings → review the **stakeholder engagement
  plan** (are they even correctly categorized/targeted for engagement?).
- If information isn't reaching the people who need it → review the **Communications Management Plan**
  instead (this is a delivery-mechanism problem, not an engagement-strategy problem).
- When there are too many stakeholders with very different update needs (some need only a high-level
  summary, others need frequent detail), the tool to build is the **stakeholder engagement plan** —
  not just the power/interest grid, which classifies stakeholders but doesn't itself define the
  communication cadence.
