# Organizational Context

Covers the organizational backdrop a project sits inside: structure types, authority, business value,
and where a project fits among product/program/portfolio.

## Organizational structures and PM authority

The exam consistently tests how much authority a PM has based on organizational structure — memorize
the authority gradient, not just the names:

| Structure | Who holds authority | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Functional | Functional manager | Departments grouped by skill (sales, IT, finance, etc.); the functional manager controls budget, resources, and decisions. |
| Weak Matrix | Functional manager (mostly) | Leans toward functional; the PM has little formal authority. |
| Balanced Matrix | Shared, functional manager still primary | PM has more say than in a weak matrix, but the functional manager remains the primary managerial authority. |
| Strong Matrix | Project manager | PM makes project decisions; functional manager only assists with specialized tasks. |
| Projectized | Project manager (full) | Most resources and authority are dedicated to project work; PM has full authority over the project. |

The **functional manager** (also: head of office/division/unit/department) has direct supervision over
resources within their business unit and direct responsibility for functions that affect or are
affected by the project.

**Authority** itself means the right to apply project resources, expend funds, make decisions, or give
approvals within a project.

## Business value and hierarchy

- **Business value:** the benefit — tangible, intangible, or both — that a project's results provide
  to its stakeholders.
- **Business Analysis:** understanding business stakeholders' needs and defining the project
  characteristics that meet them. Performed by a **Business Analyst**, who elicits needs from
  customers, staff, IT, and executives and facilitates communication across organizational units.
- **Business Model Canvas:** a brief, high-level description of the project's purpose and a commitment
  to what the team will accomplish by the end.
- **Hierarchy chart:** any chart that starts with high-level information and progressively decomposes
  it into more detail (WBS, organizational breakdown structure, product breakdown structure are all
  examples of the pattern).
- **Hierarchy of groupings:** Project < Program < Portfolio (a program groups related projects; a
  portfolio groups programs/projects/operations to achieve strategic objectives). See the seeded
  Project/Program/Portfolio glossary terms.
- **Product Life Cycle vs. Project Life Cycle:** the product life cycle is the longer arc — a product
  moves through development, introduction, growth, maturity, and decline — and can span multiple
  projects. The project life cycle is the specific set of phases one project goes through to deliver
  (e.g. wireframing → prototyping → designing → developing → testing → deploying for a mobile app).
  Product Life Cycle > Project Life Cycle in scope/duration.
- **Capital projects:** large-scale, high-expenditure projects (roads, railways, power plants,
  manufacturing plants) — a category, not a technique.

## Sustainability and value framing

- **Triple bottom line:** evaluating a business opportunity across three lines — Profit, People, and
  Planet — the economic, social, and ecological balance an idea could achieve. This is the 2026 ECO's
  sustainability emphasis showing up conceptually.
- **Value proposition:** the value of a product/service an organization communicates to its customers.
- **Value creation:** any process that creates outputs more valuable than its inputs.
- **Value Engineering:** systematically providing the necessary functions of a project at the lowest
  cost — substituting cheaper materials/methods without sacrificing functionality.
- **Vanity metric:** a metric that looks impressive but provides no real insight into performance and
  doesn't support decision-making — contrast with a genuinely useful KPI.
- **Time-to-Market:** how long it takes to get a product from idea to market release.
- **Profitability:** a project's ability to yield financial profit or gain for the organization.
- **Innovation:** renewing or updating a domain, product, or service via new processes, techniques, or
  ideas to create new value.

## Confidentiality

A set of rules or promises — usually via confidentiality agreements — that limit access to or
distribution of certain information. Relevant to compliance and governance (ECO Business Environment
task 2).
