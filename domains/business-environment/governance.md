# Project Governance

Covers ECO Business Environment task 1, "define and establish project governance."

## Definition

Project governance is the framework of **policies, processes, and practices** that guide project
decision-making, ensuring alignment with organizational objectives, accountability, and successful
project delivery. Effective governance keeps a project well-structured, aligned with organizational
objectives, effectively managed, and compliant with established standards and regulations.

## The five pillars of effective governance

1. **Clear Roles and Accountability** — everyone knows who decides what, and who answers for it.
2. **Decision-Making Framework** — a defined process for how project decisions actually get made.
3. **Effective Risk Management** — see [risk-management.md](risk-management.md).
4. **Performance Monitoring and Control (Oversight)** — tracking whether the project stays on track
   against its plan.
5. **Stakeholder Engagement & Transparency** — see
   [stakeholder-engagement.md](../people/stakeholder-engagement.md).

Governance is the structural container these five pillars operate inside — it's not itself a single
document, but the overall framework of oversight bodies, decision rights, and controls.

## Example: governance structure on a large project

For a project like a new airport terminal, governance typically means establishing a **steering
committee** (see the seeded Steering Committee / Project Board glossary entry) to oversee the project
against government regulations, budget constraints, stakeholder expectations, and environmental
standards. The governance structure defines decision-making processes, risk management protocols, and
performance monitoring to keep the project on track.

A typical governance organigram splits into two levels:

- **Project Board** (oversight): finance board, design authority, regulatory authority, government
  group, community stakeholder group, procurement board — the bodies that hold decision-making
  authority and approve direction.
- **Project Team** (delivery): design/engineering team, construction teams, technical teams — the
  groups actually doing the work, reporting up into that oversight structure.

This two-level split is the general governance pattern the exam expects: an oversight/decision layer
distinct from (and structurally above) the delivery layer, with clear accountability running between
them.
