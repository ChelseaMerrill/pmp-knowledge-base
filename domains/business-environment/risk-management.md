# Risk Management

Covers ECO Business Environment task 5, "plan and manage risk." See glossary for Risk Register, Risk
Appetite/Tolerance/Threshold, Qualitative vs. Quantitative Analysis, and Risk Response Strategies —
this note adds the process flow and the vocabulary those seeded terms don't cover.

## Risk vs. issue

- A **risk** is a potential/uncertain event — positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) — recorded
  in the **risk register**. Risk probability is never 100% or 0% (at either extreme, it's no longer
  uncertain).
- An **issue** is recorded in the **issue log** — it has already happened, and it's always negative.
- **Preventive action** addresses a risk before it occurs; **corrective action/workaround** addresses
  an issue after it's already happened. Risks arise from a **trigger condition**.

## Causes of risk — 7 categories

A structured brainstorming aid so risk identification covers all key areas instead of a narrow set of
obvious threats:

1. **Project-related** — lack of knowledge/processes, misunderstood requirements, inadequate planning.
2. **Financial** — raw-material price increases, interest-rate fluctuations, cash-flow/funding problems.
3. **Competitiveness** — resource scarcity or cost, lower opportunities, low ROI.
4. **Contractual** — penalties, loss of the project.
5. **Occupational** — injury, burnout, high turnover.
6. **Environment** — floods, natural disasters, earthquakes (project's impact on environment, or
   vice versa).
7. **Stakeholder-based** — communication issues, unsupportive stakeholders.

## The risk management process

1. Prepare the risk management plan.
2. Identify risks (into the risk register).
3. Qualitative analysis — probability and impact — for prioritization.
4. Quantitative analysis — e.g. Expected Monetary Value, Monte Carlo simulation.
5. Plan risk responses.
6. Implement risk responses and update the risk register.
7. Monitor risk.

## VUCA and related risk-characterization factors

- **Volatility** (opposite: predictability) — how unpredictable and fast-changing something is.
- **Uncertainty** (opposite: understanding) — lack of clarity about present/future.
- **Complexity** (opposite: simplicity) — many interconnected factors.
- **Ambiguity** (opposite: clarity) — lack of clarity about the event itself.
- **Urgency** — how soon the response needs to happen to still be effective.
- **Dormancy** — how long a risk's impact stays hidden after it occurs.
- **Proximity** — how much time remains before a risk could hit its objective(s).
- **Connectivity** — whether the risk relates to other individual project risks.
- **Manageability** — how easily the risk owner can manage the risk's occurrence or impact; easy to
  manage = high manageability.
- **Risk perception** — how a given stakeholder views a risk, shaped by their own values and concerns.

## Quantitative analysis techniques

- **Expected Monetary Value (EMV):** probability × impact, summed across a risk's possible outcomes —
  the standard way to size a **contingency reserve**. When a risk occurs, the reserve is drawn down by
  the risk's actual *impact*, not its EMV; remaining reserve = sum of all risks' EMVs minus impacts
  already spent.
- **Monte Carlo simulation:** runs thousands of "what-if" iterations, each drawing different values from
  the probability distributions assigned to uncertain variables (activity duration, cost, etc.), to
  produce a **probability distribution** of overall outcomes rather than a single-point estimate (e.g.
  "70% chance of finishing within 11 months, 90% chance within 14"). Steps: define uncertain variables →
  assign probability distributions → run simulations → analyze the resulting probability curve/histogram
  to size contingency for a target confidence level.
- **Decision tree analysis:** supports choosing the best course of action among several alternatives by
  mapping out decisions, chance events, and their payoffs.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** determines which risks have the most potential impact on project outcomes —
  often visualized with a **tornado diagram**, a bar chart ranking each risk's impact magnitude in
  descending order.
- **What-if analysis:** explores the potential outcomes of different courses of action.

## Individual vs. overall risk, and reserves

- **Individual risk** touches one or more specific objectives; **overall project risk** touches the
  project as a whole.
- **Insurable risk** can be transferred to an insurer.
- **Known unknowns** (identified risks) draw on the **contingency reserve**, which sits inside the
  project budget. **Unknown unknowns** (unidentified risks) draw on the **management reserve**, which
  sits outside the PM's own authority to release. Force majeure events draw on the management reserve.
- **Risk Breakdown Structure (RBS):** categorizes risk sources — Technical, External, Organizational,
  Managerial.
- **Risk appetite** is the limit of what's inconsequential to the organization; **risk tolerance** is
  the limit of what's acceptable; a **risk-averse** party is unwilling to take on risk at all.
- **Active acceptance** takes some preparatory action (e.g. setting aside contingency); **passive
  acceptance** just monitors and responds if/when the risk occurs.
- **Risk attitudes:** risk-averse (avoids risk unless reward clearly outweighs it), risk-neutral
  (analyzes objectively, then decides), risk-seeker/risk-taker (higher risk tolerance, tends to
  underestimate severity and see risks as opportunities).
- **Inherent risk** — the level of risk present before any project work has even begun.
- **Risk exposure** — an aggregate measure of the potential impact of *all* risks on a project, not
  just one.
- **Residual risk** vs. **secondary risk** — residual risk is what's left over after a response is
  applied; secondary risk is a *new* risk created as a direct result of implementing that response.
- **Fallback plan (Plan B)** — used when a risk occurs and the primary response strategy proves
  inadequate.
- **Risk rating/ranking, Risk Matrix (Probability and Impact Matrix):** qualitative tools that score or
  visualize risks by likelihood and impact (e.g. 1–5 scale, or Low/Medium/High) to determine which to
  prioritize.

## Response strategies (see also the seeded glossary entry)

- **Threats:** Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer (e.g. insurance), Accept, Escalate.
- **Opportunities:** Exploit, Enhance, Share (e.g. with a partner), Accept, Escalate.
- **Escalate** applies specifically when the risk is beyond the PM/team's own authority to handle.

## Auditing the process itself

- **Risk audit:** checks the effectiveness of the risk management *process*.
- **Risk review:** re-checks the effectiveness of the *response plans* already in place.
