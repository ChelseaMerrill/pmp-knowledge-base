# Risk Management

Covers ECO Business Environment task 5, "plan and manage risk." See glossary for Risk Register, Risk
Appetite/Tolerance/Threshold, Qualitative vs. Quantitative Analysis, and Risk Response Strategies —
this note adds the process flow and the vocabulary those seeded terms don't cover.

## Risk vs. issue

- A **risk** is a potential/uncertain event — positive (opportunity) or negative (threat) — recorded
  in the **risk register**. Risk probability is never 100% or 0% (at either extreme, it's no longer
  uncertain).
- An **issue** is recorded in the **issue log** — it has already happened, and it's always negative.
- **Preventive action** addresses a risk before it occurs; **corrective action/workaround** addresses
  an issue after it's already happened. Risks arise from a **trigger condition**.

## The risk management process

1. Prepare the risk management plan.
2. Identify risks (into the risk register).
3. Qualitative analysis — probability and impact — for prioritization.
4. Quantitative analysis — e.g. Expected Monetary Value, Monte Carlo simulation.
5. Plan risk responses.
6. Implement risk responses and update the risk register.
7. Monitor risk.

## VUCA and related risk-characterization factors

- **Volatility** (opposite: predictability) — how unpredictable and fast-changing something is.
- **Uncertainty** (opposite: understanding) — lack of clarity about present/future.
- **Complexity** (opposite: simplicity) — many interconnected factors.
- **Ambiguity** (opposite: clarity) — lack of clarity about the event itself.
- **Urgency** — how soon the response needs to happen to still be effective.
- **Dormancy** — how long a risk's impact stays hidden after it occurs.
- **Proximity** — how much time remains before a risk could hit its objective(s).
- **Connectivity** — whether the risk relates to other individual project risks.

## Individual vs. overall risk, and reserves

- **Individual risk** touches one or more specific objectives; **overall project risk** touches the
  project as a whole.
- **Insurable risk** can be transferred to an insurer.
- **Known unknowns** (identified risks) draw on the **contingency reserve**, which sits inside the
  project budget. **Unknown unknowns** (unidentified risks) draw on the **management reserve**, which
  sits outside the PM's own authority to release. Force majeure events draw on the management reserve.
- **Risk Breakdown Structure (RBS):** categorizes risk sources — Technical, External, Organizational,
  Managerial.
- **Risk appetite** is the limit of what's inconsequential to the organization; **risk tolerance** is
  the limit of what's acceptable; a **risk-averse** party is unwilling to take on risk at all.
- **Active acceptance** takes some preparatory action (e.g. setting aside contingency); **passive
  acceptance** just monitors and responds if/when the risk occurs.

## Response strategies (see also the seeded glossary entry)

- **Threats:** Avoid, Mitigate, Transfer (e.g. insurance), Accept, Escalate.
- **Opportunities:** Exploit, Enhance, Share (e.g. with a partner), Accept, Escalate.
- **Escalate** applies specifically when the risk is beyond the PM/team's own authority to handle.

## Auditing the process itself

- **Risk audit:** checks the effectiveness of the risk management *process*.
- **Risk review:** re-checks the effectiveness of the *response plans* already in place.
